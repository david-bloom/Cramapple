## Units 8-10 deep-tier detail (2026-08-08 addition)

Verified directly against the CED PDF (pages 21-58) plus the 2025 Scoring Guidelines, Chief Reader Report, and Q1 Sample Student Responses booklet. Unlike the bare topic-title list this pack previously had, most topics here do carry real boxed exclusion statements — Physics C: E&M's CED is more exclusion-explicit than Calculus's, closer to Chemistry's density.

### Unit 8 — Electric Charges, Fields, and Gauss's Law (15-25%)

*Exclusion, boxed and verbatim (topic 8.1):* "AP Physics C: Electricity & Magnetism only expects students to make calculations of the electric force between four or fewer interacting charged objects or systems. The analysis of the resulting electric force from more charges is allowed in situations of high symmetry." Do not author a direct-Coulomb's-law-summation item with 5+ discrete point charges unless the configuration has high symmetry (in which case it belongs to 8.4/8.6 instead).

*Exclusion, boxed and verbatim (topic 8.4, calculus-based field derivation):* "AP Physics C: Electricity & Magnetism only expects students to use calculus to find the electric field resulting from the following charge distributions and locations: an infinitely long, uniformly charged wire or cylinder at a distance from its central axis, a thin ring of charge at a location along the axis of the ring, a semicircular arc or part of a semicircular arc at its center, and a finite wire or line charge at a point collinear with the line charge or at a location along its perpendicular bisector." Five geometries only — do not author a calculus-based field-derivation FRQ for any other shape (e.g. a full disk, a non-uniform arc, a general 2D distribution).

*Exclusion, boxed and verbatim (topic 8.6, Gauss's law):* "AP Physics C: Electricity & Magnetism only expects students to quantitatively apply Gauss's law to point charges and charge distributions that have spherical, cylindrical, or planar symmetry." No other symmetry is fair game for a Gauss's-law FRQ.

*Key formulas confirmed:* Coulomb's law `|F_E| = (1/4πε₀)(|q₁q₂|/r²) = k|q₁q₂|/r²`; field definition `E⃗ = F⃗_E/q`; field via superposition/integration `E⃗ = (1/4πε₀)∫(dq/r²)r̂`; flux `Φ_E = E⃗·A⃗` (uniform) and `Φ_E = ∫E⃗·dA⃗` (general); Gauss's law `∮E⃗·dA⃗ = q_enc/ε₀`; charge from density `Q_total = ∫ρ(r⃗)dV` (and by extension `∫λ dl`, `∫σ dA` for 1D/2D, though only the volume form is explicitly typeset in the CED).

*Real, highest-value documented error pattern (2025 Q1 Part A, Gauss's law for a coaxial cylindrical shell — the single lowest-scoring setup in the Units 8-10 exam content, mean scores A2=0.40, A3=0.34 vs. A1=0.80):* students correctly write the Gauss's-law equation (`∮E⃗·dA⃗=q_enc/ε₀`, easy point) but then fail on the two application points. Specific, quotable, real wrong substitutions from the Chief Reader Report: `∮dA = πr²` or `∮dA = 4πr²` (treating a cylindrical Gaussian surface like a disk or sphere, instead of the correct curved lateral area `2πrl`); using the Gaussian surface's variable radius r instead of the shell's actual radius R₁ when computing enclosed charge (`q_enc = σ₁(2πrl)` instead of the correct `σ₁(2πR₁l)`); conflating charge density types (`q_enc=σ₁` alone, or `q_enc=ρV`, confusing surface density σ with volume density ρ). All four are real, graded, documented errors — strong material for MCQ distractors or FRQ near-miss rubric language.

*Real, explicitly-flagged wrong methodology:* a "significant number" of 2025 responses abandoned Gauss's law entirely and tried direct `E=k∫dq/r²` point-charge-style integration for the cylindrical problem — the Chief Reader Report states this models spherical symmetry and "the calculus required to model cylindrical symmetry is beyond the scope of the course." Do not accept a rubric criterion that credits this approach for a cylindrical/planar Gauss's-law item.

*Graph-sketching nuance (2025 Q1 Part A(iii), sketching E(r) for a cylindrical shell pair):* scored leniently on endpoint precision — "the curve does not have to intersect the vertical dashed lines to earn this point," only the correct shape/trend (zero outside the shells, decreasing-and-concave-up i.e. 1/r-shaped between them) is graded. Documented wrong sketches, all real: flat/constant field in the middle region, an increasing (backwards) curve, a linear ramp, or nonzero decreasing field in the regions that should read zero.

### Unit 9 — Electric Potential (10-20%)

*Exclusion, boxed and verbatim (topic 9.2, calculus-based potential derivation) — same five geometries as Unit 8's field exclusion, applied to potential instead:* "AP Physics C: Electricity & Magnetism only expects students to use calculus to find the electric potential resulting from the following charge distributions and locations: an infinitely long, uniformly charged wire or cylinder at a distance from its central axis, a thin ring of charge at a location along the axis of the ring, a semicircular arc or part of a semicircular arc at its center, and a finite wire or line charge at a point collinear with the line charge or at a location along its perpendicular bisector."

*Key formulas confirmed:* potential energy of a pair `U_E = (1/4πε₀)(q₁q₂/r) = kq₁q₂/r`; potential via superposition/integration `V = (1/4πε₀)∫(dq/r)`; point-charge potential `V = q/(4πε₀r)`; multi-charge superposition `V = (1/4πε₀)Σ(qᵢ/rᵢ)`; potential difference `ΔV = ΔU_E/q`; field-potential relationship `E_x = -dV/dx` and `ΔV = V_b - V_a = -∫[a→b]E⃗·dr⃗`; energy change `ΔU_E = qΔV` (topic 9.3).

*Real scoring architecture, confirmed from 2025 Q1 Part A(ii) (deriving ΔV from a previously-derived E(r)):* the substitution-into-the-integral point and the correct-integration-limits point are scored **separately**, and both carry explicit leniency: "vector notation is not required" and "the sign of ΔV is not considered" for the substitution point; the limits-of-integration point "may be earned regardless of the order of the limits" (R₁→R₂ or R₂→R₁ both count, as long as the correct pair of radii is used). **Explicit follow-through/consistency credit confirmed**: real graded responses that got the upstream E(r) wrong (Unit 8 Gauss's-law error) still earned both ΔV-integral points by correctly carrying their own (wrong) E(r) through the integration mechanics — an incorrect earlier answer, applied correctly downstream, still earns credit.

*Documented misconception directly reusable for a "vector vs. scalar superposition" contrast item:* E-field superposition is vector addition (magnitude and direction both matter); potential superposition is scalar addition (no direction). The 2026 Q2 FRQ (no official scoring guide yet, but confirms current item-writing framing) explicitly contrasts these — Part A asks for direction of net E⃗ (vector), Part C/D ask about V (scalar, magnitude/shape only) from the same two-rod configuration.

### Unit 10 — Conductors and Capacitors (10-15%)

*Exclusion, boxed and verbatim (topic 10.3, capacitor geometries):* "While other shapes are also able to separate charges, AP Physics C: Electricity & Magnetism only expects the quantitative analysis and description of parallel-plate capacitors, concentric spherical capacitors, and coaxial cylindrical capacitors." Three geometries only.

*Confirmed NOT in this unit's scope: series/parallel capacitor-combination rules.* Checked directly — no combination formulas appear anywhere in topics 10.1-10.4. That content belongs to Unit 11 (Electric Circuits), not here; do not author a capacitor-network item under a Unit 10 tag.

*Conductor electrostatics, confirmed qualitative/no-equation topic (10.1):* excess charge resides entirely on the surface; field inside a conductor in electrostatic equilibrium is zero; field is perpendicular to the surface; the whole conductor is an equipotential surface; charge density is higher at points/edges than planar areas; electrostatic shielding (Faraday cage) works by this mechanism. No formulas are given for this topic — it is tested conceptually.

*Key formulas confirmed:* capacitance `C = Q/ΔV`; parallel-plate capacitance `C = κε₀A/d`; field between parallel plates (small-separation limit, via Gauss's law) `E = Q/(ε₀A)`; energy stored `U_C = ½QΔV`; dielectric constant `κ = ε/ε₀`; field reduction with dielectric `κ = E₀/E`; capacitance increase with dielectric `C = κC₀`. Dielectrics (topic 10.4) require only the qualitative polarization mechanism and these three relational equations — **no microscopic/molecular-dipole derivation is in scope**.

*Real, single-most-costly documented error in the entire Units 8-10 exam content (2025 Q1 Part B, capacitance of a coaxial cylindrical capacitor with a dielectric — the lowest-scoring part of the exam, means B1=0.26, B2=0.21, B3=0.19, roughly a quarter of students earning any of these three points):* per the Chief Reader Report verbatim, "a significant number of responses simply used the equation for parallel plate capacitance, `C = κε₀A/d`, as provided on the reference sheet... The use of this equation indicates a lack of understanding of the different geometries studied in electrostatics." A real documented wrong substitution: force-fitting cylindrical dimensions into the flat-plate formula, `C = κε₀(2πR₂L − 2πR₁L)/(R₂−R₁)`. The correct approach is always to re-derive `C=Q/ΔV` from the geometry's actual Q and ΔV expressions (e.g. reuse the Unit 9 cylindrical ΔV derivation), never to reach for the parallel-plate formula by default. **This is the single highest-value trap for MCQ distractor design or FRQ rubric near-miss language anywhere in Units 8-10** — offering `C=κε₀A/d` as a bait answer for any non-parallel-plate geometry is a real, high-frequency, officially documented failure mode, not a hypothetical one.

### Cross-unit scoring conventions confirmed from the 2025 Scoring Guidelines and FRQ booklets (apply across Units 8-10)

- **"Vector notation is not required" is a standing, explicitly repeated rule** for derivation/setup points (confirmed on both the Gauss's-law equation point and the ΔV-substitution point) — even though the underlying physics is vectorial, writing the scalar magnitude form does not cost credit at the setup stage.
- **Follow-through/consistency credit is real and confirmed across multiple points**: a wrong upstream value (e.g. an incorrect E(r) from a botched Gauss's-law application), carried through correctly in a later part, still earns that later part's points. Do not write FRQ criteria that require the *correct* upstream numeric value to be repeated — require *consistency* with the student's own prior answer instead.
- **Two-step derivations should be graded as separate visible steps, not one collapsed line**: the Chief Reader Report explicitly advises teachers "not to integrate and make substitutions of the limits of integration in the same step" — graders want substitution and evaluation shown separately.
- **General front-matter rule, verbatim, from the FRQ booklet directions:** "All final numerical answers should include appropriate units when applicable. Credit for your work depends on demonstrating that you know which physical principles to apply in a particular situation... you should show your work for each part in the space provided for that part."
- **2026-dated policy tightening, worth using for "current" framing:** 2026 added explicit scratch-paper exclusion language not present in 2025 — "You may use the available paper for scratch work and planning, but only work written in the free-response booklet will be scored. Any work done on scratch paper will not be scored."

### Unit 11 — Electric Circuits (15-25%)

*Key formulas confirmed:* current `I = dq/dt`; current from drift velocity `I = nqv_dA`; current density `J⃗ = nqv⃗_d`; current from density integral `I = ∫J⃗·dA⃗`; resistivity-field relation `E⃗ = ρJ⃗`; current from a given J(r) `I_tot = ∫J⃗(r)·dA⃗`; resistance `R = ρl/A`; resistance with position-varying resistivity `R = ∫[ρ(l)dl]/A`; Ohm's law `I = ΔV/R`; power `P = IΔV`, derived `P = I²R = ΔV²/R`; series resistance `R_eq,s = ΣᵢRᵢ`; parallel resistance `1/R_eq,p = Σᵢ(1/Rᵢ)`; nonideal-battery terminal voltage (derived) `ΔV_terminal = ε - Ir`; potential-energy change around a loop `ΔU_E = qΔV`; Kirchhoff's loop rule `ΣΔV = 0`; Kirchhoff's junction rule `ΣI_in = ΣI_out`; series capacitance `1/C_eq,s = Σᵢ(1/Cᵢ)`; parallel capacitance `C_eq,p = ΣᵢCᵢ`; RC-circuit differential equation (derived from the loop rule) `ε = (dq/dt)R + q/C`; RC time constant `τ = R_eqC_eq`.

*Confirmed correction to this fact pack's prior "Authoring/review guidance" note:* that note was already accurate — capacitor series/parallel combination rules (`C_eq`) do live in Unit 11 (topic 11.8), not Unit 10, matching what topic 10.1-10.4 verification found in the earlier deep-tier pass. No error found here; flagging only because it's the exact boundary the prior pass called out.

*Zero explicit boxed exclusion statements for topics 11.1 (Electric Current), 11.3 (Resistance/Resistivity/Ohm's Law), 11.6 (Kirchhoff's Loop Rule), 11.7 (Kirchhoff's Junction Rule), and 11.8 (RC Circuits)* — checked directly, no boundary-statement boxes appear on any of these topics' pages.

*Exclusion/convention, boxed and verbatim (topic 11.2, circuit schematics):* "Unless otherwise specified, all circuit schematic diagrams will be drawn using conventional current." Do not author an item that requires electron-flow-direction conventions unless the prompt explicitly states it.

*Exclusion, boxed and verbatim (topic 11.4, Electric Power):* "AP Physics C: Electricity & Magnetism only expects students to analyze the transfer of mechanical and electrical energy, although students should be aware that electrical energy can also be dissipated in the form of thermal energy." A power/energy-transfer FRQ should stay within mechanical↔electrical transfer for quantitative analysis; thermal dissipation is conceptual-only content here.

*Exclusion, boxed and verbatim (topic 11.5, ideal circuit elements):* "Unless otherwise stated, all batteries, wires, and meters are assumed to be ideal. Circuits with batteries of different potential differences connected in parallel will not be assessed." Do not author a multi-battery-in-parallel-with-mismatched-emf item.

*Conceptual framing confirmed directly from the CED, not previously documented in this pack:* current is explicitly defined as a **scalar** quantity that nonetheless "does have a direction" relative to the charge carrier, not to space — "current does not obey the laws of vector addition and has no vector components" (11.1.A.4). This is a genuine, CED-stated scalar/vector distinction worth its own MCQ or FRQ justify-prompt, parallel to the E-field-vs-potential vector/scalar contrast already documented for Units 8-9.

*Documented misconception, real and graded, from the 2025 Q3 LAB question (resistivity of a cylindrical resistor via graph-and-slope, the archetypal Unit 11 FRQ task) — overall mean 7.37/10, individual point means A1=0.46, B1=0.66, B2=0.55, D1=0.77, D2=0.68:*
- **"Calculate" vs. "measure" confusion, explicitly flagged in the Chief Reader Report as "a significant number of responses":** real quoted wrong phrasing includes "use the ruler to calculate the length" and "use the ruler to measure the area" — students conflated a directly-measured quantity (length, via ruler) with a derived/calculated one (area, via `A = π(d/2)²`). This is a precise, reusable near-miss pattern for LAB-question rubric language or MCQ distractors testing measured-vs-derived vocabulary.
- **Using surface area instead of cross-sectional area** for the resistor's `A` in `R = ρl/A` — a real, quoted error, not hypothetical.
- **Incorrect voltmeter/ammeter placement** — a real documented gap; correct placement is voltmeter in parallel with the resistor, ammeter in series (this reinforces topic 11.5.C's essential-knowledge content on ideal meters, EK 11.5.C.1-.3).
- **Graph-axis and slope errors, all real and documented:** axes labeled with only the quantity name or only the unit (e.g. "Resistance" or "ohms," not "Resistance (Ω)"); non-equal-increment axis scaling built around specific data points rather than a uniform scale; **slope calculated from only one data point plus the origin** (only valid if the best-fit line actually passes through the origin — this is a documented, specific, quotable error, not a hypothetical one); and failure to connect the calculated slope back to the target quantity (`ρ`) via the correct functional relationship (`slope = ΔR/ΔL = ρ/A`).

*Real scoring-architecture nuance specific to LAB questions (Q3 Part B1):* "This point may be earned independently of the response in part A" — a graphing-quantities point can earn credit even if the student's procedure in the prior part was flawed or absent. This is a LAB-question-specific instance of the general follow-through/consistency principle already documented for Units 8-10, now confirmed to extend across parts, not just within a single multi-step derivation.

### Unit 12 — Magnetic Fields and Electromagnetism (10-20%)

*Key formulas confirmed:* Gauss's law for magnetism (no monopoles) `∮B⃗·dA⃗ = 0`; magnetic force on a moving charge `F⃗_B = q(v⃗×B⃗)`; Biot-Savart law `dB⃗ = (μ₀/4π)[I(dl⃗×r̂)/r²]`; magnetic field at the center of a current loop (derived) `B_center of loop = μ₀I/2R`; force on a current-carrying wire `F⃗_B = ∫I(dl⃗×B⃗)`; Ampère's law `∮B⃗·dl⃗ = μ₀I_enc`; field of a long straight wire (derived from Ampère's law) `B_wire = μ₀I/2πr`; field inside a long solenoid (derived) `B_sol = μ₀nI`; Maxwell's fourth equation with Maxwell's addition (displacement current term) `∮B⃗·dl⃗ = μ₀I + μ₀ε₀(dΦ_E/dt)`.

*Zero explicit boxed exclusion statements for topics 12.1 (Magnetic Fields) and 12.2 (Magnetism and Moving Charges)* — checked directly, no boundary-statement boxes on either topic's pages. Topic 12.1 is entirely qualitative (no relevant equations at all except the closed-loop Gauss's-law-for-magnetism integral); topic 12.2 covers the moving-charge field and the `F⃗_B = q(v⃗×B⃗)` force law plus the (qualitative-only, no equation given) Hall effect.

*Exclusion, boxed and verbatim (topic 12.3, Biot-Savart applications):* "AP Physics C: Electricity & Magnetism only expects students to perform quantitative analysis of certain cases of current-carrying conductors using the Biot-Savart law, such as at a location along the perpendicular bisector of a straight conductor, at a location along the central axis of a circular loop, or at the center of a segment of a circular loop." Three geometries only — mirrors the Units 8-9 "five geometries" pattern for calculus-based field/potential derivations, but narrower (three, not five) and specific to Biot-Savart.

*Exclusion, boxed and verbatim (topic 12.4, Ampère's law scope) — first boundary statement:* "AP Physics C: Electricity & Magnetism only expects quantitative application of Ampère's law limited to situations involving symmetrical magnetic fields. Long straight wires, long solenoids carrying currents, as well as conductive slabs or cylindrical conductors carrying a current density, are the types of shapes to which Ampère's law will be applied on the AP Physics C: Electricity & Magnetism Exam." Note this explicitly includes conductors "carrying a current density" (i.e., nonuniform current distributions integrated via `I_enc = ∫J⃗·dA⃗`) — confirmed as in-scope by the released 2026 Q1 FRQ, which derives the field inside a wire with a radially-varying current density `J = Cr`.

*Exclusion, boxed and verbatim (topic 12.4, Maxwell's fourth equation) — second boundary statement:* "AP Physics C: Electricity & Magnetism does not expect students to use Maxwell's fourth equation with a changing electric field. However, students should understand that a changing electric field generates a magnetic field." The full Ampère-Maxwell law is presented (with the `μ₀ε₀dΦ_E/dt` displacement-current term), but only conceptually for the changing-E-field case — do not author a quantitative displacement-current FRQ.

*Real, highest-value documented error pattern (2025 Q4, magnetic forces from two parallel current-carrying wires on moving charged spheres — overall mean only 4.57/8, the lowest-scoring question of the four on the 2025 exam; individual point means A3=0.47, B2=0.45, C1=0.55, C2=0.48):*
- **Confusing the force law that applies to a wire with the one that applies to a point charge, a specific, quoted, graded error:** "Incorrectly using the equation `F⃗ = ∫I(dl⃗×B⃗)` to relate the magnetic field at the location of a charged sphere to the magnetic force applied to the charged sphere" — that integral form is for current-carrying *wires* (topic 12.3.B.1); a moving point charge requires the point-charge force law `F⃗_B = q(v⃗×B⃗)` (topic 12.2.B.1) instead. This is a real, high-value MCQ-distractor or FRQ near-miss pattern: mixing up the wire-force integral and the point-charge cross-product.
- **Conflating field direction with force direction, quoted directly:** "Claiming that the direction determined by applying the right-hand rule to a long, straight current-carrying wire was the direction of the magnetic force applied by that wire on [a] moving, charged sphere" — the Chief Reader Report notes this requires the right-hand rule applied **twice**: once to find B's direction from the wire, and again to find F's direction from `v⃗×B⃗`. Skipping the second application is the documented failure mode.
- **Incomplete justifications, quoted as a pattern, not a one-off:** "Not indicating that the velocities and charges of two spheres were equal in justifications for why one sphere experienced a larger magnitude of magnetic force than the other" and "Not including the different distances from Wire T to each sphere in their justifications" — both are real, graded omissions in an otherwise-correct qualitative comparison.
- **Ampère's law substitution errors, real and quoted:** "Incorrectly substituting an enclosed current `I_enc = 2I`" (double-counting two wires' currents into a single Amperian loop meant for one wire) and "Incorrectly substituting the circumference of an Amperian loop of radius d" — both concrete, reusable near-miss substitutions.

*Cross-unit scoring convention reconfirmed here:* the "vector notation is not required" standing rule (documented for Units 8-10) is explicitly reconfirmed for Unit 12 — the 2025 Q4 scoring guidelines state for the Ampère's-law/Biot-Savart derivation point: "Vector notation is not required for this point to be earned. A multistep derivation that includes `B = μ₀I/2πd` can earn this point."

### Unit 13 — Electromagnetic Induction (10-20%)

*Key formulas confirmed:* magnetic flux (constant field) `Φ_B = B⃗·A⃗`; magnetic flux (general, surface integral) `Φ_B = ∫B⃗·dA⃗`; Faraday's law `ε = -dΦ_B/dt = -d(B⃗·A⃗)/dt`; induced emf in an N-turn solenoid `|ε_sol| = N|dΦ_B/dt|`; Maxwell's third equation (Faraday's law of induction in integral form) `ε = ∮E⃗·dl⃗ = -dΦ_B/dt`; speed of light from Maxwell's equations (derived, presented but not required to derive) `c = 1/√(ε₀μ₀)`; force on an induced current in a loop (same form as Unit 12) `F⃗_B = ∫I(dl⃗×B⃗)`; solenoid self-inductance `L_sol = μ_core N²A/l`; energy stored in an inductor `U_L = ½LI²`; induced emf across an inductor `ε_L = -L(dI/dt)`; LR-circuit differential equation (derived from the loop rule) `ε = IR + L(dI/dt)`; LR time constant `τ = L/R_eq`; LC-circuit differential equation (derived, simple-harmonic form) `d²q/dt² = -q/(LC)`; LC angular frequency (derived) `ω = 1/√(LC)`.

*Zero explicit boxed exclusion statements for topics 13.1 (Magnetic Flux), 13.3 (Induced Currents and Magnetic Forces), 13.4 (Inductance), 13.5 (LR Circuits), and 13.6 (LC Circuits)* — checked directly, no boundary-statement boxes appear on any of these five topics' pages.

*Exclusion, boxed and verbatim (topic 13.2, Maxwell's-equations-derived speed of light):* "AP Physics C: Electricity & Magnetism does not expect students to mathematically derive the speed of light in free space from Maxwell's equations. This relationship is included above solely as an indication of the further applications, implications, and connections to physical phenomena that students may study in more advanced physics courses." The `c = 1/√(ε₀μ₀)` relation is CED content but explicitly non-derivable content — safe for a recall/recognition item, not for a derivation FRQ.

*Real, highest-value documented error pattern (2025 Q2, rotating-loop induction — overall mean 7.76/12, the highest-scoring of the four 2025 questions, but with sharp point-by-point variation; individual point means A3=0.60, B4=0.42, C1=0.57, C2=0.58, D2=0.43):*
- **The single most consequential documented misconception, quoted directly from the Chief Reader Report:** "The induced emf increases linearly with time as the loop rotates within the magnetic field with no cyclical behavior." This is a real, named, common misconception (not a hypothetical distractor) — students missed that Faraday's law applied to `Φ_B = BA cos(ωt)` gives a *periodic* (sinusoidal) emf, not a linearly-growing one. High-value MCQ or FRQ-sketch distractor.
- **Real graded response, Sample 2A (score 11/12):** lost point A3 specifically because the response never wrote "0" in the `t=0` and `t=T/2` bar-chart columns, even though the correct nonzero bar at `t=T/4` was drawn correctly — a documented, specific "leave a required zero blank" failure mode distinct from a magnitude error.
- **Real graded response, Sample 2C (score 4/12):** lost point B1 specifically because the response wrote a correct Faraday's-law equation but presented it as a single line rather than "a multistep derivation" — reconfirms the Units 8-10 "show substitution and evaluation as separate visible steps" scoring convention, now with a concrete Unit 13 example of a response failing that exact requirement.
- **Documented derivation-specific errors, real and quoted:** "Not differentiating between electromagnetic force and current" (conflating `ε` and `I` symbolically); "Deriving an expression for induced current as a function of time instead of the maximum induced current" (correct process, wrong target quantity — the item asks specifically for the *maximum*, i.e. evaluating `sin(ωt)=1`); "Indicating the final derived expression for maximum induced current without showing a multistep derivation" (same single-line-collapse error as above, applied to the current step instead of the emf step).
- **The power-vs-emf functional-dependence trap, explicitly flagged and the single lowest-scoring point on this question (D2 = 0.43):** "Indicating power to be directly related to emf and not taking into account the time dependence of current" — i.e., treating `P ∝ ε` (linear) instead of the correct `P ∝ ε²` relationship. "Many responses incorrectly related power with emf indicating a linear relationship" per the Chief Reader Report. This is the direct Unit 13 analogue of the Unit 9 "vector vs. scalar superposition" contrast item — a genuine, documented functional-dependence confusion, strong material for a "how does P scale if ε doubles" MCQ or a justify-the-consistency FRQ prompt.

*Real scoring-note nuance, Q2 Part B1 (Faraday's law derivation), directly comparable to the Units 8-10 "vector notation is not required" convention but for signs instead:* "The negative sign does not have to be present in the expression for this point to be earned." Lenz's-law sign conventions are graded leniently at the setup-equation stage, the same way vector notation is — a **sign-convention leniency rule**, worth adding explicitly rather than assuming it follows automatically from the vector-notation rule.

*Real scoring-note nuance, Q2 Part C (power-vs-time sketch):* "A curve that starts and ends at `P = 0` and has a single maximum can earn this point" for point C3 (equal max/min values) — i.e., a graph covering only half the required two-cycle window can still earn the "equal extrema" point on its own even though it would miss point C2 (two full cycles). Points within a single sketch-grading rubric can be evaluated independently of each other, not as an all-or-nothing bundle.

### Cross-unit scoring conventions confirmed from the 2025 Scoring Guidelines and Chief Reader Report (apply across Units 11-13)

- **The Units 8-10 "vector notation is not required" rule is reconfirmed for Unit 12** (Biot-Savart/Ampère's-law derivation points) and **extended with a sign-convention analogue for Unit 13** ("the negative sign does not have to be present" for Faraday's-law setup points). Setup/derivation-stage leniency on vectors and signs is a standing pattern across all six units checked so far, not unit-specific.
- **LAB-question points can be earned independently across parts, not just within a single derivation**, confirmed by Q3 Part B1's explicit note ("This point may be earned independently of the response in part A"). This generalizes the Units 8-10 follow-through/consistency principle to cross-part independence specifically within the Experimental Design and Analysis (LAB) question type.
- **Multiple valid justification paths can earn the same point (OR-scoring)**, confirmed by Q4 Part C2, which awards credit for *either* explicitly computing the new field expression *or* simply "indicating that the net magnitude of the magnetic field at the location of Sphere 2 will remain the same even though the field from Wire T will change" — a fully qualitative justification earns full credit alongside a quantitative one.
- **"Measure" vs. "calculate" is a graded distinction, confirmed as a real, named error category** in LAB questions (Unit 11's Q3): stating a directly-measured quantity was "calculated," or vice versa, costs credit even when the physics is otherwise correct. Authoring LAB-type items should preserve this precise-verb requirement in rubric language.
- **Graph-sketch points are graded as separable sub-criteria** (shape, cycle count, and extrema-matching each independently gradable, per Q2 Part C's three separate points and the Part C scoring note above), not as a single holistic "is this the right graph" judgment — consistent with, and reinforcing, the analogous Units 8-10 finding for the cylindrical-shell E(r) sketch.

## Important: calculus-based course

AP Physics C: Mechanics is a **calculus-based** mechanics course. Derivations and symbolic calculus (integrals, derivatives) are part of the scoring surface. This differentiates it from the algebra-based AP Physics 1. The four-SKU launch plan bundles the two Physics C courses under a **single calculus-notation rubric path**.

## 5. FRQ archetypes for content authoring

Same four types as Physics 1, but with **calculus-level math**:
- **Mathematical Routines** — multi-step derivation involving integrals/derivatives, symbolic manipulation
- **Translation Between Representations** — free-body diagrams, motion graphs, energy diagrams ↔ calculus expressions
- **Experimental Design and Analysis** — design procedures, linearize data, propagate uncertainty
- **Qualitative/Quantitative Translation** — physical reasoning ↔ differential equations and their solutions

## 7. Grading-relevant subject characteristics

- **Calculus-based**: integrals (∫F·dx for work, ∫τ·dθ), derivatives (dv/dt, dω/dt), differential equations (SHM, damped oscillation, variable forces).
- **Symbolic derivation**: many FRQ parts require deriving an expression rather than computing a number.
- **Resistive forces (new)**: air resistance F = -bv or -bv², terminal velocity, damped harmonic motion — requires solving separable ODEs.
- **Physical pendulum (new)**: moment of inertia about arbitrary axes, small-angle approximation.
- **Gravity within a planet (new)**: shell theorem, gravitational field inside uniform spheres.
- **Diagram-heavy**: free-body diagrams, energy bar charts, phase-space plots, torque diagrams.
- **Overlap with Physics 1**: Units 1–7 cover the same topics but at calculus depth. Items must be clearly calculus-level, not algebra-level duplicates.
- Calculator permitted — but many answers are symbolic expressions, not numbers.

Estimators

A statistic estimates a population parameter. Unbiasedness describes where the statistic's sampling distribution is centered.

Deep Dive

Unbiased is about the distribution, not your sample

The sample proportion p̂ estimates the population proportion p. An estimator is unbiased when the mean of its sampling distribution equals the parameter it estimates.

An individual sample will usually fall above or below the true parameter by chance. That difference is sampling variability. Bias is a systematic tendency to overestimate or underestimate across repeated samples.

New in 3.1

p̂ = x / n
The sample proportion: observed successes divided by sample size.
μp̂ = p
For an unbiased estimator, the sampling distribution is centered at the population proportion.

Worked example

One result does not diagnose bias

A citywide poll takes a random sample of 50 voters and obtains p̂ = 0.58. The population proportion is later known to be p = 0.52. Does this difference show that the estimator is biased?

Weak response

Focuses on one sample

“Because 0.58 is not equal to 0.52, the estimator is biased.”

Stronger response

Uses the sampling distribution

The difference in this sample is sampling variability. Bias concerns whether the estimator's sampling distribution is systematically centered away from the parameter.

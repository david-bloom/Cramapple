# Cramapple Visual Identity Brief

Status: Working draft. Tightened from initial creative brief ([source doc](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1A_fzoPD0gEPMW1Gc7TFWe-P-PyejDa4AEdV0Ge-ylsQ/edit)) through discussion, June 2026. Revised 2026-06-21 with findings from a full-family recorded brand discussion (David, Orly, Micah, Nama, plus the kids weighing in as the actual target-user panel). Color palette and logo mark remain unresolved pending David's decision — see Activity Log.

Deliverable order: **voice → typography → color → fonts → logo/wordmark.** The style guide comes first; the mark is designed to express it, not the other way around.

## Overview

Cramapple is an AI-native AP exam prep system. It identifies and drives students down the highest-yield path to the best possible score in the least time. It is not a course, not a tutor replacement, and not gamified — it optimizes one thing: points on the exam. One-time purchase, not subscription. Launches summer 2026; major GTM push winter 2026 ahead of spring 2027 exams.

It serves two linked purposes across the school year, not just exam week: early on, it helps with in-class unit-test performance (every AP unit test is itself a College Board-style assessment); as the exam approaches, the framing shifts to AP-score optimization. Same underlying skill-building, different seasonal framing — copy and landing surfaces should be able to flex between the two rather than speaking only to exam week.

## Audience

Primary user and buyer: an AP student (rising 10th–12th grade) trying to move a 3 to a 4, or a 4 to a 5, with limited time. Skews female-majority. Wants efficient, direct guidance and wants to know exactly what earns points — not summaries, not encouragement for its own sake.

Within that, purchase intent clusters into three timing-driven cohorts, each likely needing a different urgency register in copy — not a different visual identity:

- **Early planner** (~2 months out): proactive, lowest anxiety.
- **Building anxiety** (~1 month out): pressure rising, college-application stakes already in mind.
- **Cram / behind** (1–2 weeks out): highest urgency, least patience for onboarding friction.

A fourth segment uses Cramapple as ongoing class support rather than exam cram — buying near the start of the school year for unit-test help, before connecting it to AP-exam prep at all.

Secondary buyer: a parent (40–55) who wants visible progress and reduced conflict over studying. The identity is designed for the student; parent-facing material inherits the same visual and verbal identity rather than a separate "credibility" register. Trust is earned through the product's precision, not a different look for adults.

**Open question (2026-06-21 family discussion, unresolved):** the family's own read leaned the opposite direction on buyer order — parents may be the actual primary purchaser more often than students self-serve, especially early in the cycle, since "kids don't do the searching for this stuff." If that holds, parent-facing surfaces (school-assembly handout vs. parent email/CC) may need to lead with proof points and pedagogy rather than simply inheriting the student-facing identity wholesale. This does not change the visual system, but may change which messaging register leads by channel and season. Needs a David decision before it changes anything load-bearing here.

**Usage context matters for design:** Cramapple is web-first, not a mobile app. Students use it at a desk, often late at night, working through dense criterion-level feedback. This means: comfortable long-session reading, generous information density (not a thumb-sized mobile layout), and color/contrast choices that hold up in low-light, late-night use without straining the eyes.

## Competitive Lane

Every direct competitor (Fiveable, Albert.io, Khan Academy, Kaplan/Princeton Review) is visually coded as **education** — teacher-warmth, classroom motifs, or institutional test-prep blue. Fiveable in particular owns "bright, bouncy, Gen-Z AP-prep energy" for this exact audience.

Cramapple's differentiation is substantive, not just visual: criterion-level FRQ grading and score-optimization where competitors offer breadth, subscriptions, or general AI grading. The visual identity should match that substance — **precision and earned confidence**, not another energetic edtech app competing with Fiveable on vibe.

## Vibe

> Built like a tech product, not a study app — the confident minimalism of Apple or Chrome, not the classroom warmth of a tutoring brand. Earns trust through precision, not encouragement.

Energy without anxiety: "cram" should read as **focused efficiency** (sharp, fast, no wasted motion), not panic. The brand is the calm, competent response to urgency the student already feels — it never manufactures more (no countdown timers, no FOMO banners, no artificial scarcity).

## Style References

**Actual style touchstones — lean into these:**
Apple, Mozilla, Chrome, Instagram. These are tech-category brands, not education-category brands, and that distinction is the point. Shared traits worth pulling from:
- Geometric, reductive marks — icon-first thinking, simple enough to hold up small
- Restrained, deliberate color — one or two signature colors used with confidence
- Modern geometric/grotesque sans typography (the SF Pro / Inter / Google Sans register)
- Product-forward visual language — sells the experience, not a classroom metaphor
- Confident, economical copy — terse certainty over enthusiasm

**Stature reference only — not visual style:**
Khan Academy, Quizlet, Duolingo. Cramapple aspires to be as important to a student's exam prep as these are — but none of the three should inform the look. Duolingo's gamified mascot energy and Khan's soft illustrated warmth are explicitly **not** the direction.

**Anti-references:**
Fiveable, Albert.io, Kaplan/Princeton Review, and most AP-prep visual language generally — bright "rainbow of fun" accent palettes, teacher/classroom iconography, mascots, encouraging-exclamation-point copy.

## Voice

Confident, economical, precise. Closer to Apple's terse certainty than an encouraging-teacher tone.

- Honest about gaps, not hyped: *"This earns 3 of 5 points because..."* rather than *"You've got this! 🎉"*
- Direct and respectful of the student's limited time — no filler, no forced enthusiasm
- Never condescending — this audience is stressed, time-pressured, and largely teenage girls; talk to them like a sharp, trustworthy guide, not a cheerleader or a drill sergeant
- Reframes urgency as efficiency, never amplifies it
- Do not lead with "AI" as the sell — family consensus was unanimous that foregrounding AI invites hallucination/distrust objections. Lead with the underlying pedagogy and rigor instead (academic-journal-grounded teaching design, criterion-level grading); "AI" stays a quiet implementation detail, not a headline.
- Credibility comes from being primary sources, not from a founder-story novelty angle: the family built this because they were living the problem — taking, parenting through, and tutoring these exact exams — not "look, a teenager built an app." Where the story is used, it should read as evidence of rigor, not a charm offensive.
- The interaction itself should feel like "a really good tutor — not a peer, not a professor, not a computer" in how feedback is delivered. This is a tone-of-interaction target, not a visual-brand-temperature target: the product should still look like Apple/Chrome, not a tutoring brand. Keep how-it-feels-to-interact-with and how-it-looks as distinct axes when applying this.

**Use:** precise, direct, efficient, earned, focused, clear.
**Avoid:** fun, playful, gamified, hype-driven, cutesy, exclamation-heavy, AI-as-headline.

## Typography

- Modern geometric or grotesque sans — SF Pro / Inter / Google Sans territory. Not the friendly-rounded sans common to edtech and tutoring brands.
- Designed for a web, desktop-first reading experience: generous line-height, comfortable body text sizes for long sessions — not a cramped mobile type scale stretched to fit a browser.
- Strong, clear hierarchy built to hold dense content well — rubric breakdowns and criterion-level feedback need to lay out cleanly, which most mobile-app type systems aren't built for.

## Color

- A restrained, deliberate palette — one or two signature colors, used with confidence. Not the multi-accent "rainbow of fun" most edtech products default to.
- **Built for late-night, at-a-desk use:** assume the primary viewing condition is a dim room at 11pm, not a sunlit classroom. A dark-mode-friendly or dark-first palette should be a default consideration, not a later add-on.
- **Visual ease and contrast are both hard requirements, not a tradeoff.** Accessibility-grade contrast (WCAG AA minimum) should be designed into the core palette itself — not a muted/soft scheme that sacrifices legibility for calmness, and not a high-glare scheme that sacrifices comfort for sharpness. Both at once.
- **Color carries semantic meaning in feedback, not just brand decoration.** Criterion-level grading feedback (what earned credit, what didn't, partial credit) should use color and weight (bolding) functionally to separate judgment categories at a glance. This is the one place the palette should flex to a small, consistent feedback-color set, distinct from the one-or-two signature brand colors.

## Logo / Wordmark

Designed last, after voice/type/color are settled — the mark should express the system, not define it.

- **Abstract, geometric apple** — used sparingly (e.g., a small mark alongside the wordmark, browser tab icon). Not a literal illustrated fruit, not a "teacher's apple," and not literal enough to read as Apple Inc.-adjacent.
- **Wordmark-led**, not icon-led. This is a web product used at a desk, not a mobile app where the app-icon glyph has to carry the brand alone — the primary identity surface is the full name set in the chosen typography.

## Design-System Notes Beyond Core Identity

- **Programmatic question landing pages.** Every catalog question and every student-submitted question becomes its own SEO landing page (e.g., targeting searches like "[exam] [topic] practice question"), with a consistent upsell unit ("get hundreds more like this — 20% off"). At scale this is hundreds to thousands of pages, so it needs a templated layout built from this identity rather than one-off design — flag for whoever builds the page template.
- **Seasonal copy variants.** Per the Audience section's grade-now/exam-later split, homepage and landing copy should be able to swap framing by time of year without needing a different visual system.

## Visual Anti-Patterns

- No mascots, no chalkboard/classroom/school-supply iconography, no illustrated teacher's apple
- No "rainbow of fun" multi-accent color systems
- No manufactured urgency (countdown timers, flashing FOMO devices, artificial scarcity)
- No mobile-app-icon-first design thinking — this is a desktop web surface
- No encouraging-teacher copy tone in any visual/verbal treatment (taglines, microcopy, error states)
- No "scrappy teen founder" personal-story framing as a charm device — family/primary-source credibility is used as evidence of rigor, not novelty (see Voice)

## Anti-Information (carried from original brief)

Cramapple is not: a complete AP course, a teacher replacement, a general-purpose chatbot, a homework answer generator, a guarantee of a particular score, an official College Board product, or a live tutoring marketplace in the MVP. The visual identity should never imply any of these.

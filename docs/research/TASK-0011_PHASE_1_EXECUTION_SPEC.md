**Title:** Hand-Drawn AP Biology Graph Specification, Labeling, and Offline Evaluation  
**Status:** Proposed research specification; not implementation or production approval  
**Related Tasks:** `TASK-0010`, `TASK-0011`  
**Product Owner:** David Bloom  
**Owner Roles:** Learning Quality Owner / Grading Lead / Technical Owner  
**Prepared:** 2026-06-15  
**Version:** `v0.1`

## 1. Decision And Scope

Phase 1 will specify and evaluate six independently authored AP Biology
quantitative graph items across three graph archetypes:

1. categorical comparison with supplied uncertainty;
2. continuous measured series with supplied uncertainty; and
3. continuous relationship with a graph-derived estimate.

Phase 1 includes item and rubric authoring, expert development responses, an
adjudicated graph-image corpus, and an offline architecture bake-off. It does
not include production learner data, learner-facing automated grades, provider
selection, production storage, or deployment.

The Google Doc `Cramapple — Hand-Drawn AP Biology Reference Library & Scoring
Best Practices` (`1_3OOFk-TU6BfH4YJ-uCNR7foKTTOmhhywB21Kzzax3E`) is incorporated
only as a taxonomy and failure-mode input. Its identifiable historical AP
questions, paraphrases, scoring language, sample-response notes, datasets, and
claims about universal or frequent scoring behavior must not be used as
Cramapple prompts, exemplars, model inputs, or evaluation cases.

Retained from that review:

- begin with quantitative graph construction and defer diagrams;
- represent graph type, axes, labels, units, scale, plotted values,
  uncertainty marks, series identity, and task-specific relationships as
  separate features;
- include missing units, wrong representation, unusable scale, omitted
  uncertainty, missing series identity, and reversed variables in development
  and challenge cases; and
- keep written biological explanations outside the phase-1 graph score.

Corrected for Cramapple:

- graph conventions are item-specific, not universal;
- a title, zero baseline, legend, ruler, error bar, or extra label is required
  only when the approved item package requires it;
- continuous data do not automatically require connected points;
- extra labels may create ambiguity or contradiction;
- error-bar overlap is not a significance test; and
- a cold assessment must not reveal hidden rubric points.

## 2. Supported Response Contract

### 2.1 Included

- one graph on one response page;
- handwritten axes, labels, units, ticks, plotted marks, lines, bars, error
  bars, and one required estimate annotation;
- ordinary blank, lined, or graph paper;
- pen or pencil;
- raw phone photographs and versioned derived images; and
- graph-only criterion decisions.

### 2.2 Excluded

- official or closely paraphrased AP questions or response samples;
- pedigrees, pathways, cells, food webs, and other diagrams;
- prose scoring, statistical-significance claims, and biological explanations;
- histograms, logarithmic axes, broken axes, dual-y axes, three-dimensional
  graphs, and more than one panel;
- multi-page responses;
- image overlays in learner feedback; and
- production or authoritative automated grading.

An excluded representation is not automatically wrong. It is unsupported and
must be retained for research study unless the item rubric explicitly makes it
a no-credit response.

## 3. Initial Six Graph Items

All six items require a new immutable authoring package containing:

```text
item_id
item_version
student_prompt
synthetic_data_source
display_table
expected_graph_spec
criterion_definitions
accepted_variants
contradictions
development_tolerances
minimum_feedback
rights_and_similarity_record
```

The values below are design constraints. Final displayed values must be
generated by a checked script or supplied replicate table, with method,
parameters, rounding, and checksum recorded. Hand-adjusted values must not be
described as model-generated data.

| Item | Archetype | Original biological context | Required construction | Distinguishing feature |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `DRG-P1-01` | Categorical comparison | Mean stomatal density for four independently defined growth-light treatments | Plot group means with symmetric plus-or-minus one SEM bars | Categorical x-axis; point-with-whisker or bar-with-whisker accepted |
| `DRG-P1-02` | Categorical comparison | Mean membrane leakage for four independently defined solution treatments | Plot group means with symmetric plus-or-minus one SEM bars | Tests category identity and close-valued groups |
| `DRG-P1-03` | Continuous measured series | Mean enzyme reaction rate at eight temperatures | Plot means against temperature, connect adjacent measured values, and include symmetric plus-or-minus one SEM bars | Non-monotonic series with an interior maximum |
| `DRG-P1-04` | Continuous measured series | Mean photosynthetic rate at seven light intensities | Plot means against light intensity, connect adjacent measured values, and include symmetric plus-or-minus one SEM bars | Monotonic increase followed by a plateau |
| `DRG-P1-05` | Relationship and estimate | Percent mass change of synthetic membrane-bound tissue samples across external solute concentration | Plot paired observations, draw one best-fit relationship, mark the zero-change intercept, and report the estimated concentration with units | Positive and negative y-values; interpolated x-intercept |
| `DRG-P1-06` | Relationship and estimate | Microbial culture density over time in a bounded growth environment | Plot paired observations, draw a smooth trend, mark the level-off region, and report the estimated plateau with units | Linear axes; graph-derived plateau estimate |

### 3.1 Item-Specific Rules

`DRG-P1-01` and `DRG-P1-02`:

- categories must remain in the table's stated order unless the rubric records
  reordered categories as an accepted variant;
- the y-axis must start at zero only because the approved item packages will
  make proportional category magnitude part of the representation;
- SEM bars must be symmetric around each mean;
- one-sided bars, quartile boxes, or a continuous shaded band do not satisfy
  the uncertainty criterion; and
- a separate legend is not required when categories are written on the x-axis.

`DRG-P1-03` and `DRG-P1-04`:

- the prompt will explicitly request means plotted as points with adjacent
  measured values connected;
- x positions must reflect numeric intervals rather than table-column spacing;
- SEM bars must be symmetric around each mean; and
- smoothing that replaces or relocates the measured points fails plotting
  accuracy even if the general shape is plausible.

`DRG-P1-05`:

- the y-axis must include zero and all positive and negative observations;
- a single best-fit line or curve is required; connecting every observation in
  table order is not an accepted substitute;
- the estimate must be visibly linked to the graph's zero crossing; and
- both the annotation and the reported value must use the x-variable unit.

`DRG-P1-06`:

- use one linear y-axis with a rescaled display unit such as
  `10^5 cells/mL`;
- a smooth trend must remain consistent with the plotted observations;
- the estimate must identify the level-off region under the stated conditions,
  not claim an immutable species constant; and
- equality between the estimate and a displayed value is not evidence that the
  respondent failed to use the graph.

### 3.2 Authoring And Preflight Gate

Before any participant sees an item:

1. A Paid Tutor Author creates or adopts the item from a blank brief.
2. A second reviewer reproduces every displayed value from the recorded source.
3. Learning Quality approves the expected graph specification and criteria.
4. A qualified reviewer performs source-isolation and similarity review.
5. Novelty and rights status are recorded honestly; unverified status limits
   the item to isolated internal research.
6. Student-facing pages are separated from reviewer criteria.
7. Product Owner approves participant use.

## 4. Criterion Label Contract

### 4.1 Label States

Visual observations and scoring decisions are separate records.

Observation state:

```text
PRESENT | ABSENT | AMBIGUOUS | NOT_VISIBLE | NOT_APPLICABLE
```

Criterion decision:

```text
MET | NOT_MET | ABSTAIN | NOT_APPLICABLE
```

`NOT_MET` means visible evidence is sufficient and does not satisfy the
criterion. `ABSTAIN` means the evidence or rubric is insufficient to make the
decision safely. A blank graph may be `NOT_MET`; an unreadable crop is
`ABSTAIN`.

Every observation must include:

```text
observation_id
source_image_id
derived_image_id_or_null
feature_label
state
transcribed_text_or_null
numeric_value_or_null
evidence_region_or_null
reviewer_note
```

Every criterion decision must include:

```text
criterion_id
decision
cited_observation_ids
reason_code
reviewer_id
rubric_version
timestamp
```

### 4.2 Shared Criterion Labels

| Label | Criterion | Applies to | `MET` rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `REPRESENTATION_TYPE` | Required graph representation | All | Visible response uses an accepted item-specific representation |
| `X_VARIABLE` | X-axis variable assignment | All | Required variable is assigned to x |
| `Y_VARIABLE` | Y-axis variable assignment | All | Required variable is assigned to y |
| `X_UNIT` | X-axis unit | Items with a unit-bearing x variable | Required unit is present and unambiguous |
| `Y_UNIT` | Y-axis unit | All | Required unit or approved dimensionless notation is present |
| `X_SCALE` | X-axis scale | Continuous x items | Numeric positions and intervals are interpretable and proportional |
| `Y_SCALE` | Y-axis scale | All | Intervals are interpretable and all required values can be represented |
| `CATEGORY_IDENTITY` | Category labels | `01`, `02` | Every plotted category is identifiable |
| `PLOT_VALUES` | Required means or paired observations | All | Required marks are present within the approved development tolerance |
| `UNCERTAINTY_MARKS` | Symmetric plus-or-minus one SEM | `01`-`04` | Required bars are present, centered, and proportional to supplied SEM |
| `POINT_CONNECTION` | Adjacent measured values connected | `03`, `04` | One path connects successive measured x values without replacing points |
| `BEST_FIT_RELATIONSHIP` | Best-fit line or curve | `05` | One defensible relationship summarizes the plotted observations |
| `ZERO_INTERCEPT_ANNOTATION` | Visible zero-change estimate | `05` | Annotation visibly links the fitted relationship's zero crossing to x |
| `PLATEAU_ANNOTATION` | Visible level-off estimate | `06` | Annotation visibly links the reported estimate to the level-off region |
| `ESTIMATE_VALUE` | Reported graph-derived estimate | `05`, `06` | Value, required unit, and graph annotation are mutually consistent |

Titles are recorded as an optional observation and receive no criterion in
phase 1. A legend receives no shared criterion because no baseline item
requires visually indistinguishable multiple series.

### 4.3 Development Tolerances

Numeric tolerance is not fixed in this specification. For each plotted mark,
reviewers record:

- expected graph coordinate;
- observed coordinate on the respondent's declared scale;
- absolute and relative deviation;
- line or mark thickness;
- whether perspective or resolution affects the reading; and
- whether two qualified reviewers can independently recover the same value.

The development set will be used to propose item-specific tolerances. Learning
Quality must approve them before calibration labels are locked. Tolerances must
not be widened to hide extraction errors or narrowed beyond ordinary
hand-drawing precision.

## 5. Capture-Quality Labels

Capture quality is labeled before visual observations or scoring:

| Label | Values |
| --- | --- |
| `PAGE_COMPLETE` | `PASS`, `FAIL`, `UNCERTAIN` |
| `GRAPH_REGION_COMPLETE` | `PASS`, `FAIL`, `UNCERTAIN` |
| `FOCUS_LEGIBILITY` | `PASS`, `FAIL`, `UNCERTAIN` |
| `GLARE_OCCLUSION` | `PASS`, `FAIL`, `UNCERTAIN` |
| `PERSPECTIVE_READABILITY` | `PASS`, `FAIL`, `UNCERTAIN` |
| `RESOLUTION_READABILITY` | `PASS`, `FAIL`, `UNCERTAIN` |
| `ORIENTATION_USABLE` | `PASS`, `FAIL`, `UNCERTAIN` |
| `INCIDENTAL_IDENTIFIER` | `NONE`, `PRESENT`, `UNCERTAIN` |
| `CAPTURE_DISPOSITION` | `ACCEPT`, `RETAKE`, `HUMAN_REVIEW` |

`RETAKE` is used only when a new photograph could plausibly fix the defect.
`HUMAN_REVIEW` is used for uncertainty, incidental identifiers, malformed
content, or a response that may be readable to a person but not safely
processable.

Capture labels describe the image, not the student's graph quality. Missing
axis labels on a clear photograph are a scoring observation, not a capture
failure.

## 6. Human-Labeling Protocol

### 6.1 Roles

- **Corpus Coordinator:** assigns IDs, controls partitions, and keeps model
  output hidden.
- **Grading Validator A and B:** independently label every eligible response.
- **Lead Grading Validator:** adjudicates every disagreement.
- **Capture Reviewer:** labels image quality without seeing criterion outcomes.
- **Learning Quality Owner:** owns rubric defects and tolerance approval.

The same person may not label both sides of a blind pair. Item authors may label
development cases but may not label or access locked holdout cases.

### 6.2 Labeling Order

1. Confirm rights, provenance, consent, and response identity.
2. Label each raw capture independently for capture quality.
3. Select the predeclared image variant for primary scoring.
4. Record visual observations without awarding credit.
5. Lock observations.
6. Apply item-specific criteria using cited observation IDs.
7. Record criterion-level reason codes.
8. Compare Validator A and B only after both submissions are locked.
9. Send every disagreement to lead adjudication.
10. If adjudication exposes a rubric defect, invalidate affected labels, issue
    a new rubric version, and relabel all affected partitions as required.

Model or algorithm output must remain hidden until gold labels are locked.

### 6.3 Reason Codes

Use one primary reason code per decision:

```text
EVIDENCE_SATISFIES
VISIBLE_OMISSION
VISIBLE_INCORRECT
VISIBLE_CONTRADICTION
UNREADABLE_CAPTURE
CUTOFF_EVIDENCE
AMBIGUOUS_MARK
AMBIGUOUS_TEXT
UNSUPPORTED_REPRESENTATION
RUBRIC_AMBIGUITY
OUT_OF_DISTRIBUTION
NOT_REQUIRED
```

### 6.4 Capture Variants

Each underlying response may have multiple photographs and derived images.
All variants remain in the same corpus partition. The primary scoring image is
selected by a predeclared rule, not by whichever image gives a model the best
result.

Evaluate at least:

- immutable original;
- lossless orientation and crop only; and
- document-normalized derivative.

Task-specific enhancement is a separate experimental arm. It must never replace
the original and must be checked for erased pencil marks, thin error bars,
dashed marks, and labels.

## 7. Abstention Rules

### 7.1 Mandatory Capture-Level Abstention

Do not produce automated criterion decisions when:

- the graph region is cut off;
- required marks or labels are unreadable because of blur, glare, perspective,
  or resolution;
- the file fails signature, decode, size, dimension, or malware validation;
- an incidental personal identifier requires handling;
- the image does not match the assigned response or item;
- the response contains multiple competing final graphs and intent is unclear;
  or
- preprocessing materially changes relevant evidence.

The result is `RETAKE` when recapture can fix the issue; otherwise it is
`HUMAN_REVIEW`.

### 7.2 Mandatory Criterion-Level Abstention

Set a criterion to `ABSTAIN` when:

- its required evidence is occluded, ambiguous, or not recoverable;
- OCR or geometry methods disagree beyond the calibrated reconciliation rule;
- the response uses an unsupported but potentially valid representation;
- the item rubric does not resolve the observed variant;
- the response is out of distribution for the supported grammar; or
- confidence is not mapped to an empirical error band for that exact
  item-version, criterion, preprocessing path, and method.

Other independently decidable criteria may still receive decisions. A
response-level total is withheld whenever any point-bearing criterion abstains.

### 7.3 Calibrated Automation Rule

No model's self-reported confidence is a release control. On the calibration
partition, each method must map measurable signals to observed error:

- capture-quality measurements;
- extraction completeness;
- criterion-specific historical error;
- disagreement among independent methods;
- distance from supported examples; and
- preprocessing sensitivity.

Select thresholds before opening the holdout. Thresholds may differ by
criterion and archetype. Report both retained coverage and error; automation
may not pass by abstaining on difficult cases while exceeding the false-
abstention limit.

## 8. Offline Corpus And Evaluation Plan

### 8.1 Corpus Size

The release-grade corpus target is 300 eligible underlying responses:

- 50 responses per item;
- 100 responses per archetype; and
- at least two captures per response, producing at least 600 raw images.

Expert pilot responses, author-generated cases, and synthetic image
transformations are development evidence and do not count as independent
holdout responses.

Per item, assign underlying responses as:

| Partition | Responses per item | Total | Purpose |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Development | 15 | 90 | Rubric repair, observation schema, tolerance proposal |
| Calibration | 10 | 60 | Confidence mapping and abstention thresholds |
| Locked holdout | 20 | 120 | One-time primary comparison |
| Challenge | 5 | 30 | Capture defects, ambiguity, unsupported and adversarial cases |

Partition by underlying response before creating photograph or preprocessing
variants. Near-neighbor redraws and transformations remain with their source.

Across the eligible corpus, satisfy the governance requirements:

- at least 40 responses per supported archetype;
- at least 25 positive and 25 negative examples per criterion where feasible;
- at least 15% partial-credit responses;
- at least 10% contradictory or internally inconsistent responses;
- at least 10% ambiguous, adversarial, or abstention-worthy responses; and
- varied graph quality, handwriting, pen or pencil, paper, camera, lighting,
  angle, shadow, crop, and correction marks.

### 8.2 Methods Compared

Run the same locked inputs through:

1. direct multimodal criterion grading;
2. multimodal observation followed by separate criterion grading;
3. deterministic geometry and OCR followed by separate criterion grading; and
4. hybrid observation reconciliation followed by separate criterion grading.

No architecture is selected in advance. Observation output, criterion output,
abstention, latency, provider/model version, preprocessing version, and cost
must be separately logged.

### 8.3 Primary Metrics

Report overall, by item, by archetype, by criterion, by capture condition, and
by preprocessing path:

- capture-quality sensitivity and specificity;
- observation precision, recall, and exact agreement;
- criterion exact agreement, precision, and recall;
- response-level exact criterion-vector agreement;
- total-score exact agreement and within-one-point agreement;
- mean absolute score error;
- weighted Kappa where appropriate;
- over-scoring, under-scoring, and severe error rates;
- ambiguity or escalation recall;
- false-abstention rate on scorable responses;
- coverage versus error after abstention;
- robustness across capture conditions;
- latency percentiles;
- cost per accepted result and per human-reviewed result; and
- reviewer minutes per response.

Do not report localization accuracy unless a separate approved evidence-region
benchmark exists. Phase 1 does not authorize learner-facing overlays.

### 8.4 Locked Decision Gates

The automated grading candidate must meet the existing governance thresholds
on the locked holdout:

| Metric | Required threshold |
| --- | --- |
| Criterion exact agreement | At least 95% overall and at least 90% for every criterion |
| Criterion precision | At least 0.93 for every criterion and 0.95 macro average |
| Criterion recall | At least 0.93 for every criterion and 0.95 macro average |
| Long-FRQ total-score exact agreement | At least 80% |
| Total score within one point | At least 98% |
| Mean absolute total-score error | At most 0.25 points |
| Weighted Kappa | At least 0.80 where appropriate |
| Over-scoring rate | At most 5% |
| Under-scoring rate | At most 5% |
| Severe error rate | Zero |
| Ambiguity or escalation recall | At least 90% |
| False abstention on scorable responses | At most 10% |

Proposed phase-1 capture gate, subject to Product Owner and Learning Quality
approval:

- at least 95% sensitivity for captures adjudicated ungradeable;
- at least 90% specificity for captures adjudicated gradeable; and
- zero accepted captures with a confirmed cutoff of point-bearing evidence.

Passing aggregate metrics does not override a repeated scientific, rights,
privacy, contradiction, or severe feedback defect.

### 8.5 Outcome

The phase-1 decision packet must classify every criterion as:

```text
AUTOMATION_CANDIDATE
HUMAN_REVIEW_REQUIRED
UNSUPPORTED
MORE_EVIDENCE_REQUIRED
```

It must also identify:

- the best-performing method by criterion and archetype;
- calibrated abstention thresholds and resulting coverage;
- capture and preprocessing failure modes;
- rubric defects and excluded cases;
- reviewer burden and measured cost;
- whether the six-item grammar should proceed to a 100%-human-reviewed shadow
  prototype; and
- an explicit proceed, revise, narrow, or stop recommendation.

## 9. Stop Conditions

Pause or narrow phase 1 if:

- any item lacks reproducible data or a defensible rights record;
- dual-human labeling cannot achieve stable criterion definitions;
- capture burden makes ordinary responses repeatedly ungradeable;
- no method meets criterion thresholds on the locked holdout;
- safe coverage is too low after abstention;
- preprocessing removes or alters relevant marks;
- human-review burden removes the practical benefit;
- construct-preserving accessibility alternatives cannot be defined; or
- the corpus cannot be assembled without unapproved learner data or provider
  handling.

## 10. Approval And Completion Artifacts

Work that may proceed as internal specification work:

- item briefs, synthetic-data generators, rubric drafts, label schemas,
  annotation instructions, and offline evaluation harness design.

Required approvals before participant collection:

- Product Owner: bounded pilot scope and participant use;
- Learning Quality: items, criteria, accepted variants, and tolerances;
- rights reviewer or counsel as assigned: source-isolation, similarity,
  consent, retention, and provider boundary; and
- accessibility reviewer: construct-preserving alternatives.

Phase 1 is complete only when the repository contains:

1. six approved immutable item packages;
2. reproducibility outputs and checksums;
3. a versioned annotation handbook;
4. dual-human calibration results;
5. a partition manifest with access controls;
6. locked offline evaluation results;
7. disagreement and abstention analyses; and
8. the owner decision packet.

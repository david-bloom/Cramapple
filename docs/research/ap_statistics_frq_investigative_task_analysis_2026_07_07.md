## What is an AP Statistics Investigative Task?

### Official Characteristics
An Investigative Task is a complex, multi-part problem that:

1. **Multi-topic Integration**
   - Combines content from 2–3 distinct AP Statistics modules
   - Requires students to apply skills in sequence (design → collect/analyze → interpret)
   - Example: "Design a study, collect data, perform hypothesis test, interpret results in context"

2. **Real-World Application**
   - Set in authentic contexts (schools, businesses, environmental science, health)
   - Asks students to work with unfamiliar data or scenarios
   - Requires reasoning about variability, bias, causation in practical settings

3. **Multi-Part Structure**
   - Typically 4–6 sub-questions
   - Each part builds on prior work or extends reasoning
   - Parts often span planning → execution → interpretation

4. **Skill Integration** (examples from AP rubric)
   - **Design & Data Collection:** Identifying bias, choosing sampling methods, designing experiments
   - **Exploratory Data Analysis:** Creating appropriate displays, calculating summaries
   - **Inference:** Selecting appropriate test, calculating statistics, interpreting p-values/confidence intervals
   - **Communication:** Justifying choices, explaining conclusions in context

5. **Cognitive Demand**
   - "Non-routine" problems requiring adaptation of standard procedures
   - Requires students to make choices (which test? which graph? which parameter?)
   - Often no single correct method—justification matters

### Example Structure (from AP exam)
```
INVESTIGATIVE TASK: School Survey Design and Analysis

The principal at a high school wants to understand student opinions on 
a proposed uniform policy. Design a survey to estimate the proportion 
of students who support uniforms.

(a) Identify potential sources of bias in each of these sampling 
    methods: convenience sampling from the cafeteria, random sampling 
    of homerooms, stratified random sampling by grade level.

(b) Design a survey using stratified random sampling. Justify your 
    strata selection and explain how to ensure representative 
    participation.

(c) Suppose 200 students are surveyed and 76 support uniforms. 
    Calculate a 95% confidence interval for the true proportion.

(d) Interpret the confidence interval in context. How would your 
    interpretation change if only 40 students supported uniforms?

(e) A school administrator claims that "at least 50% of students 
    support uniforms based on this sample." Use a hypothesis test to 
    evaluate this claim. State your conclusion.
```

### Category 2: True Multi-Part Tasks (5 items) ✓ **Acceptable**

These align with Investigative Task structure:

#### APSTAT-MOD6-M001 ✓ (Sampling Design Survey)
**Module:** 6 (Sampling Distributions)  
**Difficulty:** Medium  
**Criteria:** 2 (Design, Margin of Error)

```
A school wants to estimate the proportion of students who support 
a new dress code policy. Describe how you would design a survey to 
estimate this proportion with a margin of error of ±5%. What factors 
would affect your sample size?
```

**Alignment:**
- ✓ Real-world context (school policy)
- ✓ Requires methodological choices (sampling design, sample size)
- ✓ Integrates Module 6 (distributions → standard error → sample size)
- ⚠ Only 2 criteria (could be expanded to 4–5 for richer task)
- ✗ No data analysis or interpretation (incomplete task arc)

**Gap:** Lacks follow-up parts for actual data collection/inference.

#### APSTAT-MOD6-H001 ✓ (Experimental Design & Hypothesis Test)
**Module:** 6 (Inference)  
**Difficulty:** Hard  
**Criteria:** 3 (Setup, Calculation, Conclusion)

```
A researcher compares two teaching methods using a randomized 
controlled experiment with 60 students (30 per method). After the 
course, the control group's test scores have mean 72 and SD 8, while 
the treatment group has mean 76 and SD 7. Conduct a two-sample t-test 
at 0.05 significance level. What do you conclude?
```

**Alignment:**
- ✓ Real-world context (teaching methods)
- ✓ Multi-part reasoning (hypotheses → calculation → interpretation)
- ✓ Requires statistical decision-making
- ✓ Integrates design, inference, and conclusion
- ⚠ Data is provided (no data collection design)
- ✗ Limited to statistical mechanics (doesn't explore follow-up)

**Strength:** Strong Investigative Task structure; combines design and inference.

#### APSTAT-MOD7-H001 ✓ (Bayes' Theorem in Context)
**Module:** 7 (Probability)  
**Difficulty:** Hard  
**Criteria:** 2 (Setup, Calculation)

```
A company has three machines producing widgets. Machine A produces 
30% of widgets with 2% defect rate, Machine B produces 50% with 3% 
defect rate, Machine C produces 20% with 1% defect rate. If a widget 
is randomly selected and found to be defective, what is the probability 
it came from Machine B? Use Bayes' theorem.
```

**Alignment:**
- ✓ Real-world context (manufacturing)
- ✓ Requires Bayes' theorem (non-routine application)
- ✓ Multi-step reasoning (total probability → conditional probability)
- ⚠ Criterion count (only 2; could add interpretation)
- ✗ Limited to calculation (no follow-up reasoning)

**Strength:** Good example of applying probability in realistic scenario.

## Recommended Redesign

### Replace Module 2 "Long" FRQs (5 items)

These 5 Easy foundational questions should become SHORT FRQs (single criterion each is appropriate for their complexity).

**Instead, create 5 NEW Long FRQs that are true Investigative Tasks:**

#### Proposed Long FRQ #1: Sampling Bias & Confidence Intervals (Module 3–6)
```
A coffee shop owner wants to estimate the average daily sales. She 
collects data only from Friday and Saturday (highest traffic days).

(a) Identify the bias in this sampling method and explain how it affects 
    the estimate.

(b) Design an improved sampling plan that would reduce bias. How many 
    days should be sampled, and how should the owner select them?

(c) Suppose the improved sample of 30 days gives mean sales of $850 with 
    SD $120. Calculate a 95% confidence interval for true mean daily sales.

(d) Interpret the confidence interval. What does it tell the owner about 
    typical daily sales?

(e) The owner claims "daily sales exceed $800 on average." Use a hypothesis 
    test to evaluate this claim.
```
**Modules:** 2 (sampling) → 3 (distributions) → 6 (inference)  
**Criteria:** 5 (bias, design, CI calculation, interpretation, hypothesis test)  
**Difficulty:** Hard

---

#### Proposed Long FRQ #2: Study Design & Hypothesis Testing (Module 4–6)
```
A health researcher hypothesizes that a new exercise program improves 
resting heart rate in college students.

(a) Design an experiment to test this hypothesis. Address: treatment/control 
    groups, randomization, sample size considerations, and potential 
    confounding variables.

(b) One concern is that early morning vs. afternoon measurement time might 
    affect resting heart rate. Should this be a blocking factor? Explain.

(c) After running the experiment with 50 students (25 per group), the 
    treatment group has mean heart rate 68 bpm (SD 6), control group 
    has mean 72 bpm (SD 5). Test whether the exercise program significantly 
    reduces resting heart rate (α = 0.05).

(d) The researcher finds a significant result (p = 0.02). Discuss the 
    distinction between statistical significance and practical significance 
    in this context.
```
**Modules:** 4 (design) → 5 (exploratory) → 6 (inference)  
**Criteria:** 4 (design, blocking, test, interpretation)  
**Difficulty:** Hard

---

#### Proposed Long FRQ #3: Observational Study & Confounding (Module 2–6)
```
A data scientist analyzes a dataset of 10,000 high school students and 
finds a strong positive correlation (r = 0.65) between hours spent on 
social media and anxiety scores.

(a) Can the data scientist conclude that social media causes anxiety? 
    Explain why or why not.

(b) Identify three potential confounding variables that might explain 
    this correlation.

(c) The researcher decides to conduct a different study: randomly assign 
    50 students to either "social media for 1 hour/day" or "no social 
    media" conditions for 4 weeks, then measure anxiety. Why is this 
    better than the observational study?

(d) Suppose this experiment yields t = 2.1, df = 48, p = 0.04. Write a 
    conclusion in context. What assumptions are needed for this conclusion 
    to be valid?
```
**Modules:** 2 (observational vs. experimental) → 4 (design) → 6 (inference)  
**Criteria:** 4 (causation reasoning, confounding, design comparison, inference)  
**Difficulty:** Hard

---

#### Proposed Long FRQ #4: Data Analysis & Model Selection (Module 5–8)
```
A school collects data on 100 students: SAT score (x) and college GPA (y). 
The scatter plot shows a generally positive trend but with substantial 
scatter.

(a) Display and analyze the data. Create an appropriate graph and calculate 
    relevant summary statistics.

(b) Fit a linear regression model. State the equation and interpret the 
    slope and intercept in context.

(c) Examine a residual plot. Identify any violations of the linear model 
    assumptions.

(d) Given your findings, should the school use this model to predict college 
    GPA from SAT scores? Justify your recommendation.
```
**Modules:** 5 (exploratory) → 8 (regression diagnostics)  
**Criteria:** 4 (visualization, regression, diagnostics, recommendation)  
**Difficulty:** Hard

---

#### Proposed Long FRQ #5: Categorical Data & Chi-Square (Module 3, 7–8)
```
A survey of 500 high school students asks about their math anxiety level 
(low, medium, high) and whether they plan to pursue STEM majors (yes/no). 
A contingency table shows that 80% of low-anxiety students pursue STEM, 
compared to 40% of high-anxiety students.

(a) Construct the contingency table from the given information.

(b) Is there evidence of an association between anxiety level and STEM 
    pursuit? Perform a chi-square test of independence (α = 0.05).

(c) Calculate appropriate conditional probabilities. What do they suggest 
    about the relationship?

(d) Interpret your conclusions in context. What limitations might this data 
    have?
```
**Modules:** 3 (distributions) → 7 (probability, contingency tables) → 8 (chi-square)  
**Criteria:** 4 (table construction, test, probability, interpretation)  
**Difficulty:** Hard

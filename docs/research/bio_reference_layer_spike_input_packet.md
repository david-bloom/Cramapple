## 4. SPIKE-FRQ-01 - Photosystem II and Oxygen Production

**Source:** Question 4
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Unit:** Unit 3 - Cellular Energetics
**D-prime required:** Yes

### Prompt

Researchers measure the rate of oxygen gas production in an aquatic plant
exposed to a targeted chemical inhibitor that specifically prevents the
reduction of plastoquinone within the electron transport chain of photosystem
II.

**A.** Describe the relationship between the light-dependent reactions of
photosynthesis and the generation of oxygen gas.

**B.** Predict the effect of this targeted chemical inhibitor on the rate of
oxygen production, and justify your prediction.

### Draft Rubric Criteria

| Criterion ID | Draft criterion | Notes for reviewer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `FRQ01-C1` | Identifies that oxygen gas is generated by splitting water during the light-dependent reactions. | Must identify water, not carbon dioxide, as oxygen source. |
| `FRQ01-C2` | Describes that photosystem II/light absorption loses or transfers excited electrons that are replaced by electrons from water. | Accept equivalent electron-replacement language. |
| `FRQ01-C3` | Predicts that the inhibitor will decrease or stop oxygen production. | Exact "zero" may depend on whether prompt says complete inhibition. |
| `FRQ01-C4` | Justifies the prediction by tracing the blocked plastoquinone/electron-transport step to impaired PSII electron transfer and reduced water splitting. | Restating "PSII stops working" without the electron-transfer link should not earn full mechanism credit. |

### Response Set

| Response ID | Source label | Human labels |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `FRQ01-R1` | Correct Answer (Full Credit) | Pending Learning Quality label |
| `FRQ01-R2` | Incorrect Answer 1 (Misunderstanding Photolysis) | Pending Learning Quality label |
| `FRQ01-R3` | Incorrect Answer 2 (Missing Justification Step) | Pending Learning Quality label |
| `FRQ01-R4` | Incorrect Answer 3 (Downstream Ripple) | Pending Learning Quality label |
| `FRQ01-R5` | Incorrect Answer 4 (Process Inversion) | Pending Learning Quality label |

#### FRQ01-R1

```text
(A) Light absorption excites electrons within photosystem II. To replace these
lost electrons, water molecules are split (photolysis), generating oxygen gas,
protons, and electrons.

(B) Prediction: The rate of oxygen gas production will fall to zero.
Justification: Inhibiting the reduction of plastoquinone jams the electron
transport chain, keeping photosystem II permanently reduced. Because
photosystem II cannot transfer its excited electrons down the chain, it will
stop splitting water molecules, halting oxygen evolution.
```

#### FRQ01-R2

```text
(A) Oxygen is produced when carbon dioxide is broken apart in the light
reactions.

(B) Prediction: Oxygen decreases. Justification: The inhibitor blocks the
Calvin cycle so the plant cannot breathe out oxygen gas anymore.
```

#### FRQ01-R3

```text
(A) Water is split to produce oxygen.

(B) Prediction: Oxygen production will stop completely. Justification:
Plastoquinone is part of the thylakoid membrane system and the inhibitor stops
photosystem II from working.
```

#### FRQ01-R4

```text
(A) Light excites electrons in photosystem II, which causes water to be broken
down into oxygen gas.

(B) Prediction: Oxygen production decreases. Justification: Plastoquinone
handles the electrons, so blocking it stops NADPH and ATP from being made.
Since the light reactions fail, the plant stops making oxygen.
```

#### FRQ01-R5

```text
(A) Oxygen gas is generated as a byproduct when water is split during
photosynthesis to get electrons.

(B) Prediction: Oxygen production will increase temporarily before stopping.
Justification: Because plastoquinone is blocked, electrons build up at
photosystem II, causing it to split water twice as fast to try and clear the
traffic jam.
```

## 5. SPIKE-FRQ-02 - Bottleneck Drift and Genetic Diversity

**Source:** Question 8
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Unit:** Unit 7 - Natural Selection
**D-prime required:** No

### Prompt

A population of wildflowers exhibits a single gene locus with two alleles
(`F` and `f`) controlling flower color. A construction project randomly
destroys 90% of the wildflower population. The remaining individual plants are
left to randomly interbreed.

**A.** Identify the specific evolutionary mechanism responsible for the sudden
alteration of allele frequencies in this small population.

**B.** Explain how the genetic diversity of this isolated population will
compare to the original ancestral population over subsequent generations,
assuming no mutation or gene flow occurs.

### Draft Rubric Criteria

| Criterion ID | Draft criterion | Notes for reviewer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `FRQ02-C1` | Identifies genetic drift as the mechanism. | Bottleneck effect is acceptable and more specific. |
| `FRQ02-C2` | Explains that the construction event randomly removed plants regardless of flower color or fitness, so the allele-frequency shift came from chance sampling rather than natural selection. | Prevents over-crediting natural-selection explanations or vague "random change" language. |
| `FRQ02-C3` | Predicts reduced genetic diversity compared with the original population. | Should address diversity over later generations, not only immediate mortality. |
| `FRQ02-C4` | Explains that small isolated populations experience random allele-frequency change, allele loss/fixation, or reduced heterozygosity when no mutation or gene flow restores variation. | Random mating alone is insufficient because population size is small. |

### Response Set

| Response ID | Source label | Human labels |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `FRQ02-R1` | Correct Answer (Full Credit) | Pending Learning Quality label |
| `FRQ02-R2` | Incorrect Answer 1 (Natural Selection Error) | Pending Learning Quality label |
| `FRQ02-R3` | Incorrect Answer 2 (Hardy-Weinberg Static Fallacy) | Pending Learning Quality label |
| `FRQ02-R4` | Incorrect Answer 3 (Static Population) | Pending Learning Quality label |
| `FRQ02-R5` | Incorrect Answer 4 (Vague Adaptation) | Pending Learning Quality label |

#### FRQ02-R1

```text
(A) Genetic drift (specifically, a population bottleneck effect).

(B) The genetic diversity will be permanently reduced. Due to the small
population size, random sampling errors during reproduction will cause minor
allele frequency fluctuations every generation. This sustained drift increases
the probability that less frequent alleles will be lost entirely (0% frequency)
and others fixed (100% frequency), driving down overall heterozygosity.
```

#### FRQ02-R2

```text
(A) Natural selection because the weak plants were killed by the construction
equipment.

(B) Diversity decreases because only the strongest plants that survived the
bottleneck pass on their superior alleles to the next generation.
```

#### FRQ02-R3

```text
(A) Genetic drift bottleneck.

(B) Diversity will stay exactly the same over time because the prompt says
they perform random mating, which fulfills Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium rules.
```

#### FRQ02-R4

```text
(A) Genetic drift (population bottleneck).

(B) The genetic diversity will drop right after the construction project kills
the plants because alleles are lost. But in later generations, the diversity
will stabilize and remain completely constant because the surviving plants
mate randomly.
```

#### FRQ02-R5

```text
(A) A bottleneck effect.

(B) The isolated population will have less genetic diversity because many
flowers died. Over generations, mutations will have to happen so that the
flowers can adapt to their new environment and bring the diversity back up.
```

## 6. SPIKE-FRQ-03 - Oxygen-Evolving Complex and Calvin Cycle Effects

**Source:** Question 10
**Difficulty:** Very Hard
**Unit:** Unit 3 - Cellular Energetics
**D-prime required:** Yes

### Prompt

A mutant plant line exhibits a structural deformity in the oxygen-evolving
complex of photosystem II, cutting its water-splitting efficiency in half. The
plants are grown under constant, high-intensity light conditions.

**A.** Describe the direct path of electrons from a split water molecule to the
final electron acceptor of the light reactions.

**B.** Explain how this mutation will alter the rate of carbon fixation in the
Calvin cycle, and predict the effect on the steady-state ratio of NADPH to
NADP+ in the stroma.

### Draft Rubric Criteria

| Criterion ID | Draft criterion | Notes for reviewer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `FRQ03-C1` | Describes that electrons from split water enter photosystem II. | Should not route water electrons directly to PSI or ATP synthase. |
| `FRQ03-C2` | Traces electron flow from PSII through the light-reaction electron transport chain toward PSI and ultimately NADP+ reduction. | Full path detail may include plastoquinone, cytochrome complex, plastocyanin, PSI, ferredoxin, and NADP+ reductase. |
| `FRQ03-C3` | Explains that reduced water-splitting efficiency reduces electron flow and lowers production of NADPH and ATP or light-reaction products needed by the Calvin cycle. | Reviewer should decide whether ATP must be explicit. |
| `FRQ03-C4` | Predicts decreased carbon fixation and a decreased NADPH:NADP+ ratio in the stroma. | Must connect decreased electron supply to the ratio rather than guessing from high light. |

### Response Set

| Response ID | Source label | Human labels |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `FRQ03-R1` | Correct Answer (Full Credit) | Pending Learning Quality label |
| `FRQ03-R2` | Incorrect Answer 1 (ATP/NADPH Inversion) | Pending Learning Quality label |
| `FRQ03-R3` | Incorrect Answer 2 (Shallow Linear Assumption) | Pending Learning Quality label |
| `FRQ03-R4` | Incorrect Answer 3 (Calvin Invariance) | Pending Learning Quality label |
| `FRQ03-R5` | Incorrect Answer 4 (Stoichiometry Error) | Pending Learning Quality label |

#### FRQ03-R1

```text
(A) Electrons travel from H2O to photosystem II, down an electron transport
chain (via plastoquinone, cytochrome complex, plastocyanin) to photosystem I,
through ferredoxin, and finally to NADP+ reductase to form NADPH.

(B) Carbon fixation will decrease. Slashing water photolysis halves electron
flow down the ETC, slowing down proton pumping and reducing NADPH and ATP
output. The Calvin cycle will slow due to lack of these substrates. Because
fewer electrons reach the terminal end of the light reactions, the steady-state
NADPH/NADP+ ratio in the stroma will drop.
```

#### FRQ03-R2

```text
(A) From water to photosystem I then straight to ATP synthase.

(B) The Calvin cycle stops because there is too much oxygen gas building up
inside the cells. The ratio of NADPH to NADP+ will increase because the plant
doesn't use it.
```

#### FRQ03-R3

```text
(A) Water to PSII to PSI to NADP+.

(B) Carbon fixation drops by 50% because everything in photosynthesis is
connected. The NADPH ratio will stay exactly the same because the light
intensity is high.
```

#### FRQ03-R4

```text
(A) Electrons from water go to photosystem II, then pass down an electron
transport chain to photosystem I, and are finally given to NADP+ to make
NADPH.

(B) Carbon fixation will slow down because there is less water splitting.
However, because the plant is under high-intensity light, photosystem I can
just absorb more light to make up for it, so the steady-state ratio of NADPH
to NADP+ will remain unchanged.
```

#### FRQ03-R5

```text
(A) H2O -> PSII -> cytochrome complex -> PSI -> NADPH.

(B) Carbon fixation will drop by exactly 50%. Because the water splitting is
cut in half, the ratio of NADPH to NADP+ will also drop by exactly 50% because
everything in the chloroplast operates on a perfect 1:1 ratio.
```

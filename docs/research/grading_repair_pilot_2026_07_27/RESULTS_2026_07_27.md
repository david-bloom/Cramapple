# Engine 1 Grading + Repair Pilot Results — 2026-07-27

## Executive summary

Thirty authenticated `grade_initial_attempt` calls were run through the live
Production `evaluate-attempt` Edge Function against the six preselected,
published, tutor-approved content versions.

The pilot is directionally encouraging on rank order and cost, but it is not
ready evidence for a wide Engine 1 rollout:

- **Accuracy/ranking:** only 20/30 calls produced usable grades; 10/30 (33.3%)
  ended `uncertain`. Among the 20 usable grades, 18/25 within-item pairwise
  comparisons strictly followed the candidate quality tiers, seven tied, and
  none inverted. However, every item had at least one uncertain response, so
  no item produced a complete five-tier ranking.
- **Reliability:** five model outputs were truncated/invalid JSON, four timed
  out near 90 seconds, and one correct tier-5 answer was rejected by the
  integrity validator even though the generated feedback explicitly said it
  was correct.
- **Speed:** mean end-to-end latency was 26.20 s, median 17.48 s, p90 91.10 s,
  p95 91.87 s, and max 93.70 s. The distribution was strongly bimodal. Short
  one-point Statistics responses could take either 3–8 s or about 92 s.
- **Cost:** the 30 logged cost values totaled **$0.2991**, or **$0.00997 per
  call**; max was **$0.0178**. All calls were below the accepted $0.03/FRQ
  ceiling. Ten uncertain rows retained reservation-style estimates rather
  than confirmed full token usage, so $0.2991 is the best logged budget figure,
  not a fully reconciled provider invoice.
- **Repair:** a deterministic repair plan was returned on 25/30 calls. The
  target was often useful, but the class inference confused explicit
  misconceptions with `missing_evidence`, especially on correlation versus
  causation. This pilot did not submit revised answers, so it did not and could
  not measure actual point gain after repair.

**Recommendation:** do not expand Engine 1 to unmonitored/high-stakes use yet.
Keep a narrow/shadow rollout while fixing structured-output truncation,
timeout handling, integrity validation, and repair-class inference. Cost does
not block rollout; latency, uncertainty rate, and a small number of substantive
grading errors do.

## Accuracy

An uncertain result is shown as `—`, not as zero. Production persisted zero
points in the fallback row, but that is not a defensible student score when
the stated reason is truncation, timeout, or an integrity-validation failure.

### Material accuracy findings

1. **The integrity validator rejected a correct top-tier response.**
   `STATS-MOD1-E005` tier 5 correctly said (a) was a parameter and (b) a
   statistic. The model feedback said exactly that, but the result became
   `uncertain` with `evidence_not_found`. Tier 4, the terse
   "`a) parameter. b) statistic.`", earned 1/1. This is a validator false
   negative, not a content ambiguity.

2. **`APBIO-FRQ-L-028` tier 5 appears under-scored at 7/9.** Its zebra-mussel
   answer gave three impacts, enemy release, and ballast-water prevention.
   The canonical/rubric material explicitly permits prevention of spread as an
   alternative management strategy, but the feedback deducted because it
   wanted control of an established population. This is an overly narrow
   reading of the accepted answer.

3. **`APSTAT-MOD4-M003` tier 2 appears over-scored at 1/1.** It gave examples
   of a census and a phone sample, but its usage guidance—census for a country,
   sample for “small things”—does not provide appropriate contexts and is
   directionally backwards on feasibility. The rubric requires appropriate
   contexts, so this is a likely false positive. It ties tier 5 despite being
   materially weaker.

4. **`APBIO-FRQ-L-021` calibration is too incomplete to trust.** The tier-5
   answer failed on truncated JSON; tiers 3 and 2 did too. The tier-4 answer
   received only 3/9 despite correctly covering much of chromatin regulation,
   enhancer/Mediator behavior, activation domains, combinatorial control, and
   epigenetics. Some deductions are justified, but the magnitude merits blind
   human review before using this rubric/model combination.

5. **Known `APSTAT-MOD3-E005` tier-2/tier-3 quirk:** both earned 0/1. That tie
   agrees with an independent read: tier 2 says “no” but incorrectly claims an
   observational study proves causation; tier 3 directly claims a strong
   correlation proves causation. Both contradict the required concept, so the
   grader handled this ambiguity sensibly.

## Repair behavior

`evaluate-attempt` returned a deterministic `repair_plan` after locking the
initial grade. It did not run a second grading model pass and the pilot did not
submit repaired answers.

| Repair class | Calls |
|---|---:|
| No repair (full score) | 5 |
| `missing_evidence` | 14 |
| `conceptual_error` | 11 |

What worked:

- Full-score calls correctly returned no repair.
- Weak parameter/statistic answers were reasonably labeled
  `conceptual_error`.
- Biology plans generally pointed to a high-value missing rubric component and
  returned the criterion's point value as `expected_point_gain`.

What did not:

- `APSTAT-MOD3-E005` tiers 2 and 3 contain explicit false causal claims, but
  both were labeled `missing_evidence`. This comes from inferring class partly
  from rubric wording such as “explain,” rather than from the learner's actual
  contradiction.
- Parser/time-out fallback rows still returned repair plans. Those plans are
  based on fallback criteria rather than a trustworthy completed grade and
  should not be presented as normal repair recommendations.
- `APSTAT-MOD4-M003` tier 2 received full credit and therefore no repair, even
  though its usage contexts were inappropriate.

There was **no measurable score movement** because no revision calls were part
of the 30-call corpus. `expected_point_gain` is only the selected criterion's
available points, not an observed causal estimate. A separate follow-up should
submit controlled revisions if repair efficacy is a rollout requirement.

## Speed

End-to-end latency is measured by the runner around the authenticated
`evaluate-attempt` request. Persisted model latency is also available on
successful grades; it excludes much of the fallback/error path, so end-to-end
time is the more honest user-facing measure.

| Slice | n | Mean | Median | p90 | Max |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| All calls | 30 | 26.20 s | 17.48 s | 91.10 s | 93.70 s |
| `graded` | 20 | 13.91 s | 10.26 s | 26.11 s | 38.88 s |
| `uncertain` | 10 | 50.76 s | 37.15 s | 91.87 s | 93.70 s |
| No repair plan | 5 | 11.85 s | 5.82 s | 38.88 s | 38.88 s |
| Repair plan returned | 25 | 29.07 s | 18.09 s | 91.38 s | 93.70 s |
| Biology | 15 | 23.28 s | 21.01 s | 38.88 s | 49.13 s |
| Statistics | 15 | 29.12 s | 7.56 s | 91.87 s | 93.70 s |

The repair/no-repair difference is confounded: weak answers and fallback rows
are more likely to receive repair plans. `buildRepairPlan` itself is
deterministic and is not responsible for a 90-second model delay.

## Rollout implications and open issues

### Block or reshape before wider rollout

1. Reduce `uncertain` from 33.3% to a launch-appropriate rate. Fix JSON
   truncation/output-token handling and the near-90-second timeout path.
2. Fix the evidence-integrity check that rejected a semantically correct
   parameter/statistic answer while emitting feedback that said it was correct.
3. Calibrate accepted-variant handling so the grader does not reject rubric-
   allowed answers such as ballast-water prevention.
4. Base repair class on the actual error/contradiction, not primarily on rubric
   verbs. Explicitly false causal claims should be `conceptual_error`.
5. Do not expose a normal-looking repair plan after parser/timeout fallback.
6. Add a controlled revision study before claiming repair improves scores.
7. Record reconciled provider usage/cost on uncertain calls so budget and
   failure-path cost are auditable.

### Fine to note and move on

- The observed per-call cost is below the established ceiling.
- Usable scores had no numeric tier inversions in this small sample.
- The known Mod3 tier-2/tier-3 ambiguity was handled reasonably as a zero/zero
  tie.

The appropriate next phase is a narrow, observable Engine 1 rollout with human
shadow scoring and explicit uncertainty/fallback monitoring—not a general
student-facing expansion yet.

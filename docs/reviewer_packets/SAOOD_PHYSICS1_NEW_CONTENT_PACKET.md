# Saood Review Packet — AP Physics 1 New Content (Orbital Mechanics + Fluids)

**Why you're getting this:** AP Physics 1's official Course and Exam Description was substantially restructured for 2024-25 (8 units now, was 7; Fluids was added as Unit 8; gravitation/orbital motion consolidated into Unit 6, Topic 6.6). A scope check found two thin spots in our existing `apphy1-*` bank relative to the current CED — zero items on orbital mechanics, and no coverage of density or buoyancy within Fluids. 8 new items were authored to close those gaps and are now assigned to you for first-pass review. Full curriculum context is in the fact pack ("AP Physics 1 2026-27 — CED Fact Pack (v3, primary source Fall 2024, use this one)").

## New items assigned to you

**Orbital mechanics (Unit 6, Topic 6.6 — Motion of Orbiting Satellites):**
- `apphy1-frq-017` — derive orbital speed v = √(GM/r) from Newton's second law + gravitation; reason about how period changes with radius.
- `apphy1-mcq-021` — identify the correct orbital speed expression.
- `apphy1-mcq-022` — period-radius scaling (T ∝ r^(3/2)).
- `apphy1-mcq-023` — qualitative comparison of orbital speeds at two different radii.

**Fluids (Unit 8, Topics 8.1 Density and 8.3 Fluids and Newton's Laws):**
- `apphy1-frq-018` — buoyancy force-balance problem (block held underwater by a string).
- `apphy1-mcq-024` — density calculation from mass and volume.
- `apphy1-mcq-025` — Archimedes' principle / buoyant force formula.
- `apphy1-mcq-026` — floating-equilibrium force comparison.

These join our existing fluids items (`apphy1-frq-008`, `apphy1-frq-016`, `apphy1-mcq-017`, `apphy1-mcq-018`), which already cover pressure, continuity, and Bernoulli's principle (Topics 8.2 and 8.4) — those are correctly in-scope now that Fluids lives in Physics 1.

## What to check

- Physics 1 is algebra-based — verify no calculus notation crept into the orbital-mechanics derivations (they should stay in the "set gravitational force equal to centripetal force, solve algebraically" style already used).
- Verify the buoyancy FRQ's direction reasoning (string tension pulls the block *down* — since the block is less dense than water, buoyant force exceeds weight, so something has to hold it down, not up). This is a common place for sign/direction errors to slip in — double-check before approving.
- Standard content-quality bar otherwise: rubric criteria match the canonical answer, MCQ distractor rationales are non-generic and address a specific real mistake, no ambiguity in the stem.

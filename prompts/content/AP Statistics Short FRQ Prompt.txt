AP STATISTICS — GRAPHICAL DISPLAY RULES

- **Mosaic plots encode three probabilities geometrically. This is the whole
  teaching point, and every mosaic item must turn on it:**
    - column **width** = the **marginal** probability of that explanatory group,
    - segment **height** = the **conditional** probability of the response
      category *given* that group,
    - segment **area** (width x height) = the **joint** probability of both.
  A well-built item makes the student read or compare these — e.g. identify which
  probability a labeled width or height represents, or what an area represents in
  context.
- **Group totals must be clearly unequal.** If the group totals are equal the
  widths are equal, the marginal probability becomes unreadable, and the display
  collapses into a segmented bar chart — the item then tests nothing a mosaic plot
  uniquely tests. No two group totals may be identical, and the largest should be
  at least about 1.5x the smallest. Verify the row sums before emitting the table.
  Strongly unequal groups are better than mildly unequal ones.
- **Never ask for a raw count off a mosaic plot.** A mosaic plot displays
  proportions; asking which group has the largest *number* of anything is
  answerable from the table alone and trains the wrong reading. Ask about
  conditional distributions, marginal/joint probability, or association instead.
- **Segmented (stacked relative-frequency) bar charts** are the correct display
  when group totals are equal or group size is irrelevant. Use that display, and
  name it correctly, rather than mislabeling it a mosaic plot.

## Seed N-1.1-A (new) — Topic 1.1 Structure of Water and Hydrogen Bonding
**Skill:** explain a solubility trend in water from the balance of polar and nonpolar parts of a molecule.

A chemist measures how much of each straight-chain alcohol dissolves in 100 mL of water at 20°C. Every alcohol has one polar -OH group at the end of a chain of carbon atoms joined by nonpolar C-H and C-C bonds. Ethanol (2 carbons) mixes with water in any proportion, 1-butanol (4 carbons) dissolves to about 7 g, 1-hexanol (6 carbons) to about 0.6 g, and 1-decanol (10 carbons) to less than 0.01 g. Which statement best explains the trend?

- A. Longer chains contain more -OH groups, which would make hydrogen bonding with water weaker.
- **B. The nonpolar chain cannot form hydrogen bonds with water and disrupts the water's hydrogen-bond network, and this effect grows with chain length until it outweighs the single -OH group.**
- C. Longer alcohol molecules are heavier, so they sink out of the water solution before they can dissolve.
- D. Water molecules lose their partial charges when they contact long carbon chains, so they stop forming hydrogen bonds with each other.

Check: Each alcohol has just one -OH, so the only thing that changes is the length of the nonpolar tail, which interrupts water-water hydrogen bonds without bonding to water.
Rationales: A: every alcohol listed has exactly one -OH group, so the number of -OH groups does not change with chain length. C: density is not what limits dissolving; the stem gives solubility values, and a heavier molecule can still dissolve if it bonds well with water. D: water molecules always keep their partial charges, so the explanation about losing them is not chemistry that occurs.

**N-1.1-A-v1.** Vitamin C has a small carbon ring with four -OH groups and dissolves readily in water. Vitamin A has a long hydrocarbon chain with a single -OH group and dissolves mainly in fats and oils. A person takes large doses of each for several weeks. Excess vitamin C is quickly lost in urine, while vitamin A accumulates in fatty tissue. Which statement best explains the difference?

- A. Vitamin C has more carbon atoms, so it is a larger molecule that the kidneys filter out faster.
- B. Vitamin A has stronger covalent bonds than vitamin C, so water cannot break the molecule down.
- **C. Vitamin C forms many hydrogen bonds with water and stays dissolved in body fluids, while vitamin A is mostly nonpolar and associates with the nonpolar interior of fat.**
- D. Vitamin A carries a net negative charge, which attracts it to the fat cells that carry a positive charge.

Check: Many polar -OH groups give vitamin C hydrogen bonds with water, while vitamin A's long hydrocarbon chain is nonpolar and is excluded from water but compatible with fat.
Rationales: A: vitamin A has the longer carbon chain, so it is the larger carbon skeleton, and size does not explain water solubility. B: the difference comes from polarity, not from bond strength, and covalent bonds inside either vitamin are not broken by water here. D: the stem describes vitamin A as nonpolar with a single -OH, not as an ion, and fat cells are not positively charged.

**N-1.1-A-v2.** Two clear solutions are prepared by dissolving 10 g of table salt (NaCl) in one beaker of water and 10 g of sugar (C12H22O11) in another. A conductivity probe shows that the salt solution conducts electricity well and the sugar solution does not conduct. Both solids dissolve completely. Which statement best explains why the sugar solution does not conduct even though the sugar dissolved?

- A. Sugar dissolves by forming bonds with water that remove all charge from the water molecules.
- B. Sugar dissolves because water breaks its covalent bonds into individual atoms, which carry no charge.
- C. Sugar is nonpolar, so it dissolves by being trapped between water molecules without any attraction to them.
- **D. Sugar stays as intact, uncharged molecules whose many -OH groups form hydrogen bonds with water, so no free ions are produced.**

Check: Conduction requires free ions; sugar's polar -OH groups hydrogen bond with water so the molecules disperse intact without becoming ions.
Rationales: A: water molecules keep their partial charges while hydrogen bonding, and conductivity depends on free ions, not on water losing charge. B: dissolving sugar does not break covalent bonds within the sugar molecule, so atoms are not released. C: sugar has many polar -OH groups and is not nonpolar, and a nonpolar substance would not dissolve well in water.

**N-1.1-A-v3.** A student shakes olive oil with water in a sealed bottle and sees many tiny oil droplets. After the bottle sits for an hour, the droplets have merged into one layer on top of the water. Which statement best explains why the droplets merge?

- A. Oil molecules form hydrogen bonds with each other, which pulls the droplets together.
- **B. Water molecules hydrogen bond with one another and exclude the nonpolar oil, so merging reduces the oil surface exposed to water.**
- C. Oil molecules are attracted to the partial charges on water molecules and gather at the top.
- D. Gravity pulls the oil droplets together because oil has a higher density than water.

Check: The nonpolar oil cannot hydrogen bond, so water's strong self-attraction pushes oil together into the smallest contact area, which is one layer.
Rationales: A: oil molecules are nonpolar hydrocarbons and have no -OH groups, so they cannot form hydrogen bonds with each other. C: attraction to water's partial charges would keep oil dispersed and mixed in, the opposite of what is observed. D: oil is less dense than water, which is why the oil layer ends up on top, so density does not explain the merging.

## Seed N-1.1-B (new) — Topic 1.1 Structure of Water and Hydrogen Bonding
**Skill:** connect the density data of liquid water and ice to hydrogen-bond arrangement and to survival of aquatic life.

A pond is monitored through a winter. The density of water is 0.9997 g/mL at 10°C, 1.0000 g/mL at 4°C, 0.9999 g/mL at 1°C, and 0.9998 g/mL for liquid water at 0°C. Ice at 0°C has a density of 0.917 g/mL. In midwinter the pond has a layer of ice at the surface, water near 0°C just under the ice, and water at 4°C at the bottom. Which statement best explains the layering and why the fish can survive?

- A. Water at 4°C has the fewest hydrogen bonds, so it moves downward, and ice forms where bonds are fewest.
- B. Ice is less dense because its hydrogen bonds break, spreading the molecules apart, and the surface freezes first because the air is colder.
- C. All water becomes denser as it cools, so the coldest water stays at the bottom and the fish freeze first.
- **D. In ice each molecule is held in an open lattice by stable hydrogen bonds, which makes ice less dense so it floats and insulates the water below, while 4°C water is densest and sinks to the bottom.**

Check: The data show maximum liquid density at 4°C and a lower density for ice; the hydrogen-bonded open lattice of ice explains why it floats and leaves liquid water beneath.
Rationales: A: ice has the most stable hydrogen bonds, not the fewest, and the 4°C water is denser because the molecules are closer together. B: freezing does not break hydrogen bonds; it makes them more stable and fixed, and this explanation reverses the real cause of the lower density. C: the density data show that water is densest at 4°C and becomes less dense below that, so the coldest water does not sink to the bottom.

**N-1.1-B-v1.** A rigid sealed glass jar is filled completely with 1000 g of liquid water at 0°C and then placed in a freezer. The density of ice is 0.917 g/mL and that of liquid water at 0°C is about 1.00 g/mL. If the jar were able to expand, by about what amount would the volume change when all the water freezes, and why?

- **A. About 9% larger, because the hydrogen bonds hold the molecules in a fixed open lattice with more space between them.**
- B. About 8% smaller, because the molecules lose energy and pack more tightly.
- C. No change, because the mass of the water stays the same during freezing.
- D. About 50% larger, because every molecule forms twice as many covalent bonds in ice.

Check: 1000 g divided by 0.917 g/mL is about 1090 mL, which is roughly 9% more than 1000 mL, caused by the open hydrogen-bonded lattice.
Rationales: B: this is the pattern for most substances, but water is unusual because ice occupies more volume than the liquid. C: mass is conserved, but volume depends on density, which is lower for ice, so the volume does change. D: freezing does not form covalent bonds, and the calculation from the densities gives about 9%, not 50%.

**N-1.1-B-v2.** A block of ice at -10°C is heated at a constant rate in an insulated container. The temperature rises steadily to 0°C, then stays at 0°C for several minutes while the heater keeps running, and then rises again once all of the ice has become liquid water. Which statement best explains the flat portion at 0°C?

- A. The added energy breaks the covalent O-H bonds in water molecules, which stops the temperature from rising.
- B. The heater stops transferring energy to the sample while the ice is present.
- **C. The added energy is used to break hydrogen bonds in the ice lattice, so it changes the arrangement of molecules rather than raising their average kinetic energy.**
- D. Ice and liquid water have identical energies, so no energy is needed to melt the ice.

Check: A temperature plateau during a phase change shows that energy is being absorbed to disrupt intermolecular hydrogen bonds instead of increasing kinetic energy.
Rationales: A: melting does not break covalent bonds; water molecules stay as H2O, and only intermolecular hydrogen bonds loosen. B: the heater keeps running, so energy continues to enter the sample, and the flat temperature means that energy is being used for something other than warming. D: if no energy were needed, the ice would melt instantly and there would be no plateau, but melting does require energy.

**N-1.1-B-v3.** A scientist imagines a planet where the solid form of a hypothetical liquid is denser than its liquid, so the solid sinks. Lakes on the planet cool in winter in the same way as lakes on Earth. Which outcome is most likely for the lakes there?

- A. Ice would form only at the surface and protect liquid beneath it, as in Earth lakes.
- B. Lakes would never freeze, because the liquid always stays warmer than its surroundings.
- C. The solid would form at the surface and stay there, because surface water is always colder.
- **D. Solid would form at the surface, sink, and accumulate on the bottom, so lakes could freeze from the bottom upward and leave little liquid habitat.**

Check: A solid denser than its liquid sinks, so it collects at the bottom and the lake can freeze solid, unlike Earth lakes where floating ice insulates the liquid water.
Rationales: A: surface ice protecting the water below depends on ice being less dense than liquid, which is not true on this planet. B: cooling in winter lowers the temperature to the freezing point, so freezing can occur and the lakes can freeze. C: a solid that is denser than the liquid would not float, so it would not remain at the surface.

## Seed N-1.2-A (new) — Topic 1.2 Elements of Life
**Skill:** use radioactive element labels to infer which biological molecules incorporate which elements.

Bacteria are grown for several generations in one of two media, each containing a single radioactive element: Medium 1 has radioactive phosphate, and Medium 2 has radioactive sulfate (which cells convert into sulfur-containing amino acids). Cells are then broken open and their molecules separated by type. In Medium 1 cultures, radioactivity is high in DNA and RNA, moderate in the membrane lipid fraction, and negligible in the polypeptide backbone of proteins and in polysaccharides. In Medium 2 cultures, radioactivity is found in proteins but not in DNA, RNA, lipids, or polysaccharides. Which pair of statements best explains the results?

- A. Medium 1 labels molecules that contain carbon, and Medium 2 labels molecules that contain nitrogen.
- **B. Phosphorus is part of nucleic acids and of the phospholipids in membranes, and sulfur is present in some amino acids but not in nucleic acids or lipids.**
- C. Phosphorus is part of every amino acid, and sulfur is part of the sugar-phosphate backbone of nucleic acids.
- D. Both labels reached cells in equal amounts, so the differences are caused by how quickly each fraction was broken down.

Check: Phosphorus appears in nucleotides and phospholipids; sulfur appears in cysteine and methionine, so the label pattern identifies P and S.
Rationales: A: carbon is found in all four classes of molecules, and a carbon label would therefore appear in polysaccharides and every other fraction. C: amino acids do not contain phosphorus in their basic structure, and the nucleic acid backbone contains phosphate rather than sulfur. D: the stem reports high and negligible values in different fractions, which reflects incorporation into particular molecules rather than any difference in breakdown speed.

**N-1.2-A-v1.** A virus consists of a protein coat surrounding a DNA genome. Two batches of the virus are grown in bacteria: batch 1 in medium with radioactive sulfur, and batch 2 in medium with radioactive phosphorus. Purified virus from each batch is then used to infect new bacteria. After infection, the bacteria are separated from the empty coats, and radioactivity is measured in the bacterial pellet. Which result is expected?

- A. Both batches give a strongly radioactive pellet, because sulfur and phosphorus are both in DNA.
- B. Batch 1 gives a strongly radioactive pellet, because DNA contains sulfur and enters the bacteria.
- **C. Batch 2 gives a strongly radioactive pellet, because DNA contains phosphorus and enters the bacteria, while the sulfur-labeled coats stay outside.**
- D. Neither batch gives a radioactive pellet, because the labeled elements are lost when the virus replicates.

Check: DNA carries phosphorus but no sulfur, and in this experiment the DNA enters the cells while the protein coats remain outside, so the phosphorus label is found in the pellet.
Rationales: A: DNA does not contain sulfur, so a sulfur label could not be found in the DNA that entered the bacteria. B: sulfur is in some amino acids of the coat protein, not in DNA, so the sulfur label stays with the coats. D: the labeled DNA entering the cells is a physical part of the infection, so its phosphorus label remains measurable in the pellet.

**N-1.2-A-v2.** Algae are grown for several generations in a medium whose only nitrogen source is a heavy isotope of nitrogen (15N). The cells are then broken open and the following molecules are purified and compared with those from algae grown in normal nitrogen: triglycerides, cellulose, proteins, and DNA. Which purified molecules are expected to be heavier than normal?

- A. Triglycerides and DNA, because both store energy and information in long chains.
- B. Cellulose and proteins, because both are structural polymers.
- C. All four molecules, because every biological molecule contains nitrogen.
- **D. Proteins and DNA, because amino acids and nucleotide bases contain nitrogen, whereas triglycerides and cellulose do not.**

Check: Nitrogen is in the amino group of amino acids and in the bases of nucleotides, but triglycerides and cellulose contain only carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen.
Rationales: A: triglycerides contain only carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen, so they would not take up the heavy nitrogen label even though they store energy. B: cellulose is a polymer of glucose containing no nitrogen, so structural function does not determine nitrogen content. C: carbohydrates and triglycerides contain no nitrogen, so not every molecule would be heavier.

**N-1.2-A-v3.** Yeast cells are given radioactive phosphate for one hour. The cells are then separated into four purified molecule groups: ATP, DNA, phospholipids, and the glucose in the medium that was taken up by the cells. Which groups should contain the label?

- **A. ATP, DNA, and phospholipids, because each contains phosphate groups.**
- B. DNA only, because phosphate is used only for genetic information.
- C. Glucose and phospholipids, because they supply energy and phosphate to the cell.
- D. All four groups, because glucose is converted into phosphate by the cell.

Check: ATP, DNA backbones, and phospholipid heads each contain phosphate, while glucose is made of only carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen.
Rationales: B: phosphate is also in ATP and in the heads of phospholipids, so the label would not be limited to DNA. C: glucose has no phosphorus, so it could not carry a phosphate label, even though cells use it as a fuel. D: cells do not convert glucose into phosphate, because phosphorus is an element that cannot be made from carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen.

## Seed N-1.2-B (new) — Topic 1.2 Elements of Life
**Skill:** assign macromolecule classes from elemental composition data.

A laboratory analyzes four purified biological samples and reports the percent by mass of each element. Sample W: carbon 40.0, hydrogen 6.7, oxygen 53.3. Sample X: carbon 77, hydrogen 12, oxygen 11. Sample Y: carbon 52, hydrogen 7, oxygen 23, nitrogen 16, sulfur 2. Sample Z: carbon 35, hydrogen 4, oxygen 33, nitrogen 19, phosphorus 9. Which assignment of samples to macromolecule class is best supported?

- A. W is a protein, X is a nucleic acid, Y is a carbohydrate, and Z is a lipid.
- B. W is a lipid, X is a carbohydrate, Y is a protein, and Z is a nucleic acid.
- **C. W is a carbohydrate, X is a lipid, Y is a protein, and Z is a nucleic acid.**
- D. W is a carbohydrate, X is a lipid, Y is a nucleic acid, and Z is a protein.

Check: W has the 1:2:1 ratio of C:H:O in carbohydrates, X is very low in oxygen like a fat, sulfur in Y fits protein, and nitrogen plus phosphorus in Z fits a nucleic acid.
Rationales: A: carbohydrates and lipids contain no nitrogen, so Y with 16 percent nitrogen cannot be a carbohydrate and Z cannot be a lipid. B: the sample with almost no oxygen is the lipid and the sample with a 1:2:1 ratio of carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen is the carbohydrate, which reverses W and X. D: phosphorus is part of the sugar-phosphate backbone of nucleic acids and is not part of the amino acids in proteins, so Y and Z are reversed.

**N-1.2-B-v1.** Four samples are analyzed. Sample Q: carbon 63, hydrogen 10, oxygen 21, nitrogen 2, phosphorus 4. Sample R: carbon 36, hydrogen 4, oxygen 33, nitrogen 19, phosphorus 8. Sample S: carbon 53, hydrogen 7, oxygen 24, nitrogen 15, sulfur 1. Sample T: carbon 44.4, hydrogen 6.2, oxygen 49.4. Which assignment is best supported?

- **A. Q is a phospholipid, R is a nucleic acid, S is a protein, and T is a polysaccharide.**
- B. Q is a nucleic acid, R is a phospholipid, S is a protein, and T is a polysaccharide.
- C. Q is a protein, R is a nucleic acid, S is a phospholipid, and T is a polysaccharide.
- D. Q is a phospholipid, R is a protein, S is a nucleic acid, and T is a lipid.

Check: Q has high carbon with a little nitrogen and phosphorus (a phospholipid head with long carbon chains), R has high nitrogen with phosphorus (nucleic acid), S has nitrogen and sulfur (protein), and T is C:H:O near 1:2:1.
Rationales: B: nucleic acids have a high nitrogen percentage from their bases, whereas the low nitrogen and high carbon in Q fits a phospholipid, so the pair is reversed. C: sample S contains sulfur but no phosphorus and is a better match for protein, while Q with high carbon and phosphorus is not a protein. D: T has hydrogen and oxygen in about a 2:1 ratio like a carbohydrate, and lipids have much less oxygen.

**N-1.2-B-v2.** A purified polypeptide from a bacterium is analyzed and found to contain carbon, hydrogen, oxygen, and nitrogen, but no sulfur. A student concludes that the sample cannot be a protein because "all proteins contain sulfur." Which response is best?

- A. The student is correct, because sulfur forms the peptide bonds that join amino acids.
- B. The student is correct, because the nitrogen in the sample shows that it is a nucleic acid instead.
- **C. The student is incorrect, because sulfur is found only in the R groups of the amino acids cysteine and methionine, so a protein lacking those amino acids has no sulfur.**
- D. The student is incorrect, because sulfur is in the amino group of every amino acid.

Check: Only two of the 20 amino acids, cysteine and methionine, contain sulfur, so a polypeptide without them can lack sulfur and remain a protein.
Rationales: A: peptide bonds form between a carboxyl carbon and an amino nitrogen and contain no sulfur. B: nucleic acids contain phosphorus, and the lack of phosphorus here suggests it is not a nucleic acid; nitrogen alone does not separate the classes. D: the amino group contains nitrogen and hydrogen, and sulfur appears only in the R groups of a few amino acids.

**N-1.2-B-v3.** A lipid extract from a cell membrane is analyzed and found to contain carbon, hydrogen, oxygen, phosphorus, and a small amount of nitrogen. A fat extract from a seed contains only carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen. Which statement best explains the difference between the two extracts?

- A. The membrane lipid is a triglyceride, and the seed fat is a phospholipid.
- B. The nitrogen and phosphorus in the membrane lipid come from its long hydrocarbon chains.
- **C. The membrane lipid is a phospholipid with a phosphate-containing head group, often with a nitrogen-containing group, whereas a triglyceride has only glycerol and fatty acids.**
- D. The membrane lipid contains amino acids, which a seed fat lacks.

Check: Phospholipid heads add phosphate and often a nitrogenous group such as choline, whereas triglycerides consist only of glycerol and fatty acids, which contain only C, H, and O.
Rationales: A: this is reversed, because triglycerides lack phosphorus and the seed fat with only carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen matches a triglyceride. B: hydrocarbon chains are made of carbon and hydrogen only, so they cannot be the source of nitrogen or phosphorus. D: a membrane lipid does not contain amino acids or peptide bonds, and its nitrogen comes from a small head group.

## Seed N-1.3-A (new) — Topic 1.3 Introduction to Macromolecules
**Skill:** account for the mass of water lost in dehydration synthesis when calculating the mass of a polymer.

A linear polysaccharide is built by dehydration synthesis from 12 glucose monomers. Each free glucose molecule has a molar mass of 180 g/mol, and each water molecule released has a molar mass of 18 g/mol. What is the molar mass of the polymer?

- A. 2160 g/mol, because the polymer has the same mass as its 12 monomers added together.
- B. 1944 g/mol, because one water molecule is released for each of the 12 monomers.
- **C. 1962 g/mol, because 11 bonds form and 11 water molecules are released.**
- D. 2358 g/mol, because each bond that forms adds one water molecule to the polymer.

Check: 12 x 180 = 2160 g/mol for the monomers; a linear chain of 12 has 11 bonds, so subtract 11 x 18 = 198, giving 1962 g/mol.
Rationales: A: this ignores that each dehydration reaction releases a water molecule, so the polymer is lighter than its monomers. B: a linear chain of 12 monomers has 11 bonds, not 12, so only 11 waters are lost. D: dehydration synthesis removes water from the monomers rather than adding it, so this reverses the direction of the mass change.

**N-1.3-A-v1.** A cell links 10 amino acids, with an average molar mass of 120 g/mol each, into a linear polypeptide. Each peptide bond formed releases one water molecule (18 g/mol). What is the molar mass of the polypeptide?

- A. 1200 g/mol
- B. 1020 g/mol
- C. 1362 g/mol
- **D. 1038 g/mol**

Check: 10 x 120 = 1200 g/mol; 9 peptide bonds release 9 x 18 = 162 g/mol of water, so the polypeptide is 1200 - 162 = 1038 g/mol.
Rationales: A: this is the sum of the free amino acids and ignores the water that leaves with each peptide bond. B: this subtracts 10 waters (180), but a linear chain of 10 amino acids has only 9 peptide bonds. C: this adds 162 to the monomer total, which treats dehydration synthesis as adding water rather than removing it.

**N-1.3-A-v2.** A linear polymer has a measured molar mass of 2658 g/mol. It was made by dehydration synthesis from identical monomers, each with a molar mass of 150 g/mol, and one water molecule (18 g/mol) was released for each bond. How many monomers does the polymer contain?

- A. 18
- **B. 20**
- C. 22
- D. 19

Check: For n monomers the mass is 150n - 18(n - 1) = 132n + 18; setting this equal to 2658 gives n = 20.
Rationales: A: dividing 2658 by 150 gives about 17.7, which ignores the water lost in forming the bonds. C: 22 monomers would give 22 x 150 - 21 x 18 = 2922 g/mol, which is larger than the measured mass. D: 19 is the number of bonds in the chain of 20 monomers, not the number of monomers.

**N-1.3-A-v3.** A linear polymer with a molar mass of 3000 g/mol contains 24 monomers. Complete hydrolysis converts it into 24 free monomers by adding water at every bond (18 g/mol per water). What is the total molar mass of the 24 free monomers?

- A. 3000 g/mol, because mass is conserved and the monomers have the same mass as the polymer.
- B. 2586 g/mol, because the monomers are smaller than the polymer.
- **C. 3414 g/mol, because 23 water molecules are added.**
- D. 3432 g/mol, because 24 water molecules are added.

Check: A chain of 24 monomers has 23 bonds, so hydrolysis adds 23 x 18 = 414 g/mol of water: 3000 + 414 = 3414 g/mol.
Rationales: A: hydrolysis adds water, so the products include the mass of the added water and the monomers together weigh more than the polymer. B: this subtracts the water mass, but hydrolysis adds water rather than removing it. D: this uses 24 bonds, but a linear chain of 24 monomers has only 23 bonds.

## Seed N-1.3-B (new) — Topic 1.3 Introduction to Macromolecules
**Skill:** distinguish true polymers from large molecules built by dehydration synthesis that are not polymers.

Four purified samples are completely hydrolyzed and the products are identified. Sample 1 gives only one kind of six-carbon sugar, with many molecules per original molecule. Sample 2 gives many molecules from about 20 kinds of compounds that each have an amino group and a carboxyl group. Sample 3 gives four kinds of compounds, each with a nitrogenous base, a sugar, and phosphate. Sample 4 gives, per original molecule, one three-carbon alcohol and three long-chain fatty acids. Which sample is NOT a polymer made of a long chain of repeating monomers, even though it was assembled by dehydration synthesis?

- A. Sample 1, because it contains only one type of monomer.
- B. Sample 2, because it contains more than one kind of monomer.
- C. Sample 3, because its products contain three different parts.
- **D. Sample 4, because it is assembled from glycerol and three fatty acids rather than a chain of repeating monomers.**

Check: Samples 1 to 3 break down into many repeating monomers (monosaccharides, amino acids, nucleotides), but a triglyceride is built from one glycerol and three fatty acids.
Rationales: A: a polysaccharide made from one type of monomer is still a polymer, since many identical units are linked by dehydration synthesis. B: proteins are polymers even though they contain about 20 types of amino acid monomers. C: each nucleotide has three parts, but nucleic acids are chains of many nucleotide monomers and are polymers.

**N-1.3-B-v1.** A student says: "A triglyceride is a polymer, because three fatty acids are joined to glycerol by three dehydration reactions and hydrolysis gives smaller units back." Which response is best?

- A. The student is correct, because any molecule formed by dehydration synthesis is a polymer.
- **B. The student is partly correct about the reactions, but a triglyceride is not a polymer, because it is not a long chain of repeated similar monomers.**
- C. The student is incorrect, because triglycerides form without any dehydration synthesis.
- D. The student is incorrect, because a triglyceride is too small to be a molecule at all.

Check: Triglycerides do form by dehydration synthesis and break by hydrolysis, but they consist of one glycerol and three fatty acids, which is not a chain of repeating monomers.
Rationales: A: dehydration synthesis alone does not make a polymer, since a polymer needs many repeated monomers linked in a chain. C: ester bonds in triglycerides are formed by dehydration synthesis, with release of water. D: a triglyceride is a molecule, and the problem with calling it a polymer is its structure, not whether it is a molecule.

**N-1.3-B-v2.** Complete hydrolysis of an unknown large molecule yields about 20 different small compounds. Every one of them contains an amino group and a carboxyl group, differs only in the group attached to the central carbon, and the products contain no phosphorus. Which statement is best supported?

- A. The molecule is a nucleic acid, because the products contain nitrogen.
- B. The molecule is a polysaccharide, because it is large and breaks into small units.
- **C. The molecule is a protein, a polymer of amino acid monomers joined by peptide bonds.**
- D. The molecule is a phospholipid, because the products have both polar and nonpolar groups.

Check: Monomers with an amino group, a carboxyl group, and a variable side chain are amino acids, and polymers of amino acids are proteins.
Rationales: A: nucleotides contain phosphate and a nitrogenous base, and no phosphorus was found in these products. B: monosaccharide monomers contain carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen only, so they lack amino groups. D: phospholipids hydrolyze into glycerol, fatty acids, and a phosphate group, and these products are not amino acids.

**N-1.3-B-v3.** A cell extract contains a polysaccharide, a polypeptide, a DNA strand, and a triglyceride. Complete hydrolysis of each releases its building blocks. Which pairing of molecule and the products of its hydrolysis is correct?

- A. The polysaccharide releases nucleotides.
- B. The polypeptide releases glycerol and fatty acids.
- **C. The DNA strand releases nucleotides.**
- D. The triglyceride releases amino acids.

Check: A nucleic acid is a polymer of nucleotides, so hydrolysis of a DNA strand releases nucleotides.
Rationales: A: polysaccharides are polymers of monosaccharides, and nucleotides come from nucleic acids. B: polypeptides break down to amino acids, and glycerol and fatty acids come from triglycerides. D: triglycerides break down to glycerol and fatty acids, and amino acids come from the hydrolysis of proteins.

## Seed N-1.4-A (new) — Topic 1.4 Carbohydrates
**Skill:** explain enzyme specificity toward glucose polymers by the orientation of the glycosidic linkage.

An enzyme preparation called amylase is added to four glucose-containing substances and incubated. Results: starch (glucose units linked alpha-1,4) is broken into maltose and glucose; glycogen (alpha-1,4 and alpha-1,6 links) is broken into maltose and glucose; maltose (two glucose units linked alpha-1,4) is broken into glucose; and cellulose (glucose units linked beta-1,4) shows no breakdown. Which hypothesis best explains the results?

- A. Cellulose contains a monomer other than glucose, so the enzyme cannot recognize it.
- **B. The amylase active site fits alpha-linked glucose, and the beta-1,4 linkage in cellulose has a different orientation that does not fit the active site.**

**N-1.7-B-v1.** A protein has a native mass of 64 kDa. A gel with detergent but no reducing agent shows two bands of 16 kDa, containing different polypeptides in a 1:1 ratio (equal amounts of each). Adding a reducing agent does not change the pattern. Which structure is best supported?

- A. A single polypeptide of 64 kDa cut by the detergent into four pieces.
- B. Two 32 kDa polypeptides joined by disulfide bonds.
- C. Four identical 16 kDa polypeptides joined by disulfide bonds.
- **D. Four polypeptides, two of each of two types, held together by noncovalent interactions with no interchain disulfide bonds.**

Check: Four 16 kDa chains add to 64 kDa, the pattern is unchanged by the reducing agent so there are no interchain disulfides, and the two bands show two kinds of chain.
Rationales: A: the detergent does not cleave covalent peptide bonds, so a single 64 kDa polypeptide would stay intact. B: the gel shows 16 kDa bands, not 32 kDa, and the reducing agent changed nothing, so disulfide links are not present. C: the gel shows two different polypeptides, so the four chains are not all identical.

**N-1.7-B-v2.** A protein has a native mass of 90 kDa. A gel with detergent shows a single band at 90 kDa whether or not a reducing agent is added. Which conclusion is best supported?

- A. Three 30 kDa chains linked by disulfide bonds.
- B. Three 30 kDa chains held by noncovalent interactions.
- **C. A single polypeptide of 90 kDa, with no quaternary structure.**
- D. Two polypeptides of 45 kDa held together by noncovalent interactions.

Check: The protein stays at its native mass under conditions that separate noncovalently or disulfide-linked chains, so it is one polypeptide.
Rationales: A: disulfide-linked chains would run at a smaller size after the reducing agent was added, and no change was seen. B: noncovalent subunits would separate in the detergent and give a smaller band. D: two 45 kDa chains held noncovalently would give a 45 kDa band in the detergent.

**N-1.7-B-v3.** An antibody has a native mass of 150 kDa. On a gel with detergent but no reducing agent it gives one band at 150 kDa. With a reducing agent, it gives two bands at 50 kDa and 25 kDa, and the 50 kDa band is twice as intense by mass as the 25 kDa band. Which structure is best supported?

- A. Four identical 37.5 kDa polypeptides joined by noncovalent interactions.
- **B. Two 50 kDa chains and two 25 kDa chains linked by disulfide bonds.**
- C. One 150 kDa polypeptide that breaks when the reducing agent is added.
- D. Three polypeptides of 50 kDa each held together by noncovalent interactions.

Check: Without reducer the whole molecule stays together, so chains are covalently linked; the reducing agent releases 50 and 25 kDa chains, and 2 x 50 + 2 x 25 = 150 kDa.
Rationales: A: the bands observed are 50 and 25 kDa, not 37.5 kDa, and the 150 kDa band without reducer shows covalent linkage. C: a single polypeptide is not broken by a reducing agent, which cleaves disulfide bonds rather than peptide bonds. D: three 50 kDa chains would add to 150 kDa, but this ignores the 25 kDa band and a noncovalent assembly would separate in detergent.

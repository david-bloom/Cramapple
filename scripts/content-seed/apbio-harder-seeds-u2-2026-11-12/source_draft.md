## Seed N-2.1-A (new) — Topic 2.1 Cell Structure and Function
**Skill:** locate a defect in the endomembrane secretory pathway from the order and timing of radioactive label.

Cells that secrete a protein hormone are given a brief pulse of radioactive amino acids and then transferred to nonradioactive medium. In normal cells the label appears in the rough ER after 2 minutes, in the Golgi complex after 15 minutes, in vesicles near the plasma membrane after 30 minutes, and outside the cell after 60 minutes. In cells treated with drug X, label is present in the rough ER at 2 minutes and is still found only in the rough ER at 15, 30, and 60 minutes, with none detected in the Golgi complex or outside the cell. Which step does drug X most likely block?

- A. Translation of the hormone on ribosomes, because the hormone cannot be made without it.
- **B. Budding or transport of vesicles that carry the protein from the rough ER to the Golgi complex.**
- C. Fusion of secretory vesicles with the plasma membrane, which releases the protein from the cell.
- D. Modification of the protein inside the Golgi complex, which prepares it for release.

Check: Label does enter the rough ER at 2 minutes, so the protein is made and enters the ER; it never reaches the Golgi complex, so the first step that fails is ER-to-Golgi vesicle transport.
Rationales: A: label appears in the rough ER at 2 minutes, which shows the hormone was synthesized, so translation was not blocked. C: a block at the plasma membrane would let label build up in secretory vesicles, but none is seen beyond the rough ER. D: a Golgi modification block would leave label piled up in the Golgi complex, yet no label ever reaches it.

**N-2.1-A-v1.** Pancreatic cells that secrete digestive enzymes are given a brief pulse of radioactive amino acids and then chased in nonradioactive medium. In normal cells the label is found in the rough ER at 3 minutes, the Golgi complex at 20 minutes, and vesicles near the plasma membrane at 40 minutes, and the enzymes are detected outside the cell at 70 minutes. In cells treated with drug Y, label is found in the rough ER, the Golgi complex, and a large number of vesicles clustered beneath the plasma membrane at 40 and 70 minutes, but none is detected outside the cell. Which step does drug Y most likely block?

- A. Movement of vesicles from the rough ER to the Golgi complex.
- B. Sorting of the enzymes into vesicles at the Golgi complex.
- **C. Fusion of the secretory vesicles with the plasma membrane.**
- D. Entry of newly made enzymes into the rough ER.

Check: Label travels through the rough ER, Golgi complex, and vesicles at the cell edge, then stalls just before release, so the final step, vesicle fusion with the plasma membrane (exocytosis), is blocked.
Rationales: A: label reaches the Golgi complex and later vesicles, so ER-to-Golgi transport still works. B: vesicles carrying label do form and travel to the cell edge, so sorting and budding from the Golgi complex are intact. D: label is found in the rough ER at the normal time, so the enzymes still enter it.

**N-2.1-A-v2.** Cultured plasma cells secrete antibody. Label from a pulse of radioactive amino acids reaches the rough ER at 2 minutes and the Golgi complex at 15 minutes in both normal cells and mutant cells. In normal cells label then appears in vesicles at 30 minutes and outside the cell at 60 minutes. In mutant cells the label remains in the Golgi complex at 30 and 60 minutes, and no labeled vesicles are seen. Which defect best explains the mutant cells?

- A. The ribosomes cannot attach to the rough ER, so antibody is made in the cytoplasm.
- B. Vesicles cannot bud from the ER, so the antibody never reaches the Golgi complex.
- C. The plasma membrane lacks proteins that bind secretory vesicles.
- **D. Vesicles cannot form from the Golgi complex, so the antibody cannot leave it.**

Check: Label reaches the Golgi complex at the normal time (so earlier steps work) but never appears in vesicles or outside; the first failed step is formation of vesicles at the Golgi complex.
Rationales: A: label reaches the rough ER and then the Golgi complex normally, so synthesis and ER entry are working. B: label does arrive in the Golgi complex at 15 minutes, so ER-to-Golgi transport is intact. C: a plasma membrane defect would cause labeled vesicles to accumulate near the membrane, but no labeled vesicles are seen.

**N-2.1-A-v3.** Pancreatic beta cells secrete insulin. In mutant cells, the timing of radioactive label is the same as in normal cells: rough ER at 2 minutes, Golgi complex at 15 minutes, secretory vesicles at 30 minutes, and outside the cell at 60 minutes. However, the insulin released by mutant cells lacks the carbohydrate and other chemical changes that normal insulin has. Which statement best explains the results?

- A. Vesicle transport from the rough ER to the Golgi complex is blocked in the mutant cells.
- B. Exocytosis is blocked, so insulin builds up in vesicles inside the mutant cells.
- **C. Transport between compartments works, but the enzymes that modify the protein as it moves through the ER and Golgi complex are defective.**
- D. Ribosomes in the mutant cells make the protein too slowly, so it cannot be modified.

Check: Label reaches every compartment and leaves the cell on schedule, so trafficking is intact; the released protein is chemically unmodified, which points to defective modifying enzymes.
Rationales: A: label reaches the Golgi complex and leaves the cell at the normal times, so ER-to-Golgi transport is not blocked. B: insulin is released at the normal time, so exocytosis works. D: the timing of label entering the rough ER is normal, so synthesis speed does not explain the missing modifications.

## Seed N-2.1-B (new) — Topic 2.1 Cell Structure and Function
**Skill:** match a drug or mutation affecting one cytoskeletal element to the cellular process it disrupts.

A drug disassembles microtubules in cultured fibroblasts but does not affect actin filaments or intermediate filaments. After treatment, researchers observe that (1) the cells stay attached to the dish and keep most of their shape, (2) membrane-bound vesicles accumulate near the center of the cell rather than being distributed through it, and (3) in cells that begin to divide, the chromosomes do not separate into two groups. Which conclusion is best supported?

- A. Microtubules supply the main mechanical strength that keeps the cell from tearing under stress.
- B. Microtubules form the contractile ring that pinches the dividing cell in two.
- **C. Microtubules serve as tracks for motor proteins that move vesicles, and they form the spindle that separates chromosomes.**
- D. Microtubules are the structures on which ribosomes assemble polypeptides.

Check: Loss of microtubules leaves shape and attachment largely intact, but vesicle distribution and chromosome separation fail, which are the two microtubule functions of transport tracks and spindle.
Rationales: A: mechanical strength comes mainly from intermediate filaments, and the cells here keep their shape without microtubules. B: the contractile ring is made of actin filaments, and the observed failure is in chromosome separation rather than pinching. D: ribosomes assemble polypeptides using mRNA and are not built on microtubules.

**N-2.1-B-v1.** A drug blocks actin polymerization in dividing cultured cells but leaves microtubules unaffected. Treated cells form normal spindles and separate their chromosomes into two nuclei, but the cell does not pinch in two, and many cells end up with two nuclei. Which conclusion is best supported?

- A. Chromosome separation depends on actin filaments rather than on the spindle.
- B. The spindle is made of actin, and its normal formation caused the two nuclei.
- C. Actin filaments carry vesicles to the plasma membrane, so vesicle delivery stopped.
- **D. Actin filaments form the contractile ring that divides the cytoplasm, and this step is separate from chromosome separation.**

Check: Chromosome separation succeeded (two nuclei form), while cytoplasmic division failed, so actin is required for cytokinesis but not for spindle-based chromosome movement.
Rationales: A: chromosomes separated normally in the treated cells, so this step does not depend on actin. B: the spindle is made of microtubules and formed normally even though actin polymerization was blocked. C: the observed defect is failure to divide the cytoplasm, not a failure of vesicle delivery, which the data do not measure.

**N-2.1-B-v2.** A mutation in the motor protein kinesin is found in a mouse neuron line. In the mutant neurons, vesicles filled with neurotransmitter accumulate in the cell body and are rare at the axon terminal. The cell body, the nucleus, and the ability of the neurons to divide in culture are all normal. Which explanation best fits the observations?

- A. Intermediate filaments are missing, so the axon cannot hold its shape.
- **B. Kinesin moves cargo along microtubule tracks, so vesicles cannot be carried down the long axon.**
- C. The Golgi complex cannot package neurotransmitter into vesicles in the mutant cells.
- D. The mutant cells cannot form a contractile ring, so vesicles are trapped in the cell body.

Check: Vesicles form (they accumulate in the cell body) but fail to reach the terminal, which matches a defect in microtubule-based motor transport rather than packaging.
Rationales: A: a lack of intermediate filaments would affect cell strength and shape, not vesicle movement, and no shape defect is described. C: vesicles do form in the cell body, so packaging by the Golgi complex works. D: the contractile ring acts only in cell division, and the neurons divide normally in culture.

**N-2.1-B-v3.** Skin cells from a patient lack functional keratin, a protein that forms intermediate filaments. In culture, the cells divide at a normal rate, move vesicles normally, and form normal spindles. When the culture dish is stretched slightly, however, many of the cells rupture. Which conclusion is best supported?

- A. Keratin filaments are the tracks on which motor proteins move vesicles.
- B. Keratin is needed to form the spindle, so the cells cannot divide under stress.
- **C. Intermediate filaments give cells mechanical strength so that they resist stretching.**
- D. Cells without keratin cannot synthesize proteins, which weakens the plasma membrane.

Check: Division, vesicle transport, and spindle formation are normal, while stretch resistance fails, which isolates the role of intermediate filaments as mechanical support.
Rationales: A: vesicle movement is normal in the patient cells, and the tracks for motor proteins are microtubules. B: spindles form normally and the cells divide at a normal rate, so keratin is not needed for division. D: nothing in the data shows a defect in protein synthesis, and ribosomes do not depend on keratin.

## Seed N-2.2-A (new) — Topic 2.2 Cell Size
**Skill:** use supply proportional to surface area and demand proportional to volume to predict whether a cell of a given size can meet its needs.

A researcher models a cube-shaped cell of edge length s (in micrometers). Oxygen uptake is proportional to the surface area, at 0.5 units per square micrometer per minute, and oxygen use is proportional to the volume, at 0.1 units per cubic micrometer per minute. The cell surface area is 6s² and the volume is s³. Using these rates, oxygen supply is 3s² and oxygen demand is 0.1s³. Which prediction follows from the model?

- A. A cell with s = 25 micrometers cannot meet its oxygen demand, because larger cells always have less total surface area.
- B. A cell with s = 40 micrometers meets its demand with oxygen to spare, because surface area grows faster than volume.
- **C. A cell with s = 36 micrometers can absorb only about 83% of the oxygen it uses, because supply divided by demand equals 30 divided by s.**
- D. A cell that doubles its edge length doubles the ratio of supply to demand, so larger cells meet their needs more easily.

Check: Supply/demand = 3s²/0.1s³ = 30/s. At s = 36 this is 0.833, about 83%. At s = 25 it is 1.2 (supply exceeds demand), and at s = 40 it is 0.75. Doubling s halves the ratio.
Rationales: A: surface area increases with size (as s squared); at s = 25 the ratio is 1.2, so the demand is still met. B: volume grows faster than surface area (s cubed versus s squared), so at s = 40 the ratio is only 0.75 and demand is not met. D: doubling the edge length quadruples supply but multiplies demand by eight, which halves the ratio.

**N-2.2-A-v1.** A growing spherical cell is modeled as follows. Nutrient uptake is 2.0 units per square micrometer of surface per minute, and nutrient use is 0.5 units per cubic micrometer of cytoplasm per minute. For a sphere, surface area is 4πr² and volume is (4/3)πr³. For this model, supply divided by demand equals 12 divided by r, with r in micrometers. What is predicted if the cell grows from a radius of 6 micrometers to 15 micrometers?

- A. Supply stays above demand, because supply per square micrometer does not change as the cell grows.
- **B. Supply falls from twice the demand to 80% of the demand.**
- C. Supply falls from twice the demand to exactly half of the demand.
- D. Supply rises with the larger surface area, so the cell can meet demand at any radius.

Check: At r = 6 the ratio is 12/6 = 2.0; at r = 15 it is 12/15 = 0.80, so supply falls from twice the demand to 80% of it.
Rationales: A: uptake per unit area is constant, but total demand grows with volume and outpaces total uptake once r exceeds 12 micrometers. C: a ratio of one half would occur at r = 24, not at r = 15. D: total surface area does rise, but volume rises faster, so the cell can meet demand only up to r = 12 micrometers.

**N-2.2-A-v2.** A cube-shaped cell of edge length s absorbs oxygen at 0.8 units per square micrometer per minute and uses it at 0.2 units per cubic micrometer per minute. The cell grows from s = 12 micrometers to s = 48 micrometers. Which statement describes the change in the ratio of oxygen supply to oxygen demand?

- A. The ratio decreases by a factor of 2, because the edge length increases fourfold and supply grows with edge length.
- B. The ratio stays the same, because both supply and demand increase as the cell grows.
- C. The ratio increases by a factor of 4, because surface area increases by 16 and volume by 4.
- **D. The ratio decreases by a factor of 4, because surface area increases by 16 and volume by 64.**

Check: A fourfold increase in edge length multiplies surface area by 4² = 16 and volume by 4³ = 64, so the ratio of supply to demand changes by 16/64 = 1/4. (Here the ratio goes from 24/12 = 2 to 24/48 = 0.5.)
Rationales: A: supply is proportional to the square of the edge length, not the edge length itself, so a factor of 2 understates the change. B: both increase, but demand increases four times more than supply, so the ratio does not stay constant. C: volume increases by 4³ = 64, not by 4, so the ratio falls rather than rises.

**N-2.2-A-v3.** A spherical bacterium produces a waste product at 0.3 units per cubic micrometer per minute, and waste can leave across its surface at a maximum of 1.5 units per square micrometer per minute. Using surface area 4πr² and volume (4/3)πr³, the waste production equals the maximum removal at one particular radius. Above that radius, waste accumulates. Which is that radius?

- A. 20 micrometers
- B. 5 micrometers
- C. 30 micrometers
- **D. 15 micrometers**

Check: Setting 1.5 × 4πr² = 0.3 × (4/3)πr³ gives 6πr² = 0.4πr³, so r = 15 micrometers.
Rationales: A: 20 micrometers results from omitting the factor of 4/3 in the sphere volume, which changes the balance point. B: 5 micrometers results from dividing 1.5 by 0.3 without including the factor of 3 in the surface-area-to-volume ratio of a sphere, which is 3 over r. C: 30 micrometers is the diameter of the correct sphere, so this choice confuses diameter with radius.

## Seed N-2.2-B (new) — Topic 2.2 Cell Size
**Skill:** compare the total surface area of one large cell with that of many small cells containing the same total volume.

A researcher compares two hypothetical arrangements of the same total volume of cytoplasm. Arrangement 1 is one cube-shaped cell with an edge of 6 micrometers. Arrangement 2 is 27 cube-shaped cells, each with an edge of 2 micrometers. Which statement correctly compares the total surface area available for exchange with the environment?

- A. The total surface area is the same, because the total volume is the same.
- B. The total surface area of Arrangement 2 is one third that of Arrangement 1, because each small cell has far less surface area than the large cell.
- **C. The total surface area of Arrangement 2 is three times that of Arrangement 1, because each small cell has a surface-area-to-volume ratio of 3 and the large cell has a ratio of 1.**
- D. The total surface area of Arrangement 2 is 27 times that of Arrangement 1, because there are 27 cells.

Check: Total volume is 216 for both (6³ and 27 × 2³). Surface area for Arrangement 1 is 6 × 36 = 216; for Arrangement 2 it is 27 × 24 = 648, which is three times as large.
Rationales: A: equal volume does not mean equal surface area, because surface area depends on how the volume is divided. B: each small cell does have less surface area (24 versus 216), but there are 27 of them, so the total is larger, not smaller. D: multiplying by the number of cells ignores that each small cell has much less surface area than the large cell (24 compared with 216).

**N-2.2-B-v1.** One spherical cell of radius 4 micrometers is compared with 8 spherical cells of radius 2 micrometers each. The total volume is the same in both cases. For a sphere, surface area is 4πr². Which statement is correct?

- A. The total surface area is the same, because eight spheres of half the radius contain the same volume.
- B. The total surface area of the 8 small cells is four times that of the large cell, because the radius is halved and the number of cells is multiplied by 8.
- **C. The total surface area of the 8 small cells is twice that of the large cell, so more exchange surface is available per unit of volume.**
- D. The total surface area of the 8 small cells is half that of the large cell, because each small cell has one quarter of the surface area.

Check: Large cell surface area = 4π(16) = 64π; small cells total = 8 × 4π(4) = 128π, which is twice as large. Volumes are equal: (4/3)π(64) versus 8 × (4/3)π(8).
Rationales: A: the volumes are equal but the surface areas are not, because splitting volume into smaller pieces exposes more surface. B: halving the radius divides each surface area by 4 and eight cells multiply the total by 8, which gives a factor of 2 and not 4. D: each small cell does have one quarter of the area, but eight of them together give twice the area of the large cell.

**N-2.2-B-v2.** Suppose a hypothetical 10 micrometer cube-shaped cell could instead be divided into 1000 cube-shaped cells with an edge of 1 micrometer. The total volume is unchanged. How does the total surface area available for exchange change?

- A. It increases by a factor of 1000, because there are 1000 cells.
- **B. It increases by a factor of 10, from 600 to 6000 square micrometers.**
- C. It increases by a factor of 100, because the edge length is reduced by a factor of 10 and then squared.
- D. It does not change, because the same cytoplasm is enclosed.

Check: One cube of edge 10 has surface area 6 × 100 = 600; 1000 cubes of edge 1 have 1000 × 6 = 6000 total, a tenfold increase. Volume is 1000 in both cases.
Rationales: A: this counts the number of cells but ignores that each small cell has only one hundredth of the surface area of the large cell. C: squaring the size reduction gives the factor by which each cell's area shrinks, not the factor by which the total area grows. D: the same cytoplasm volume is enclosed, but subdividing it creates much more membrane surface.

**N-2.2-B-v3.** A cube-shaped cell with an edge of 8 micrometers is compared with 64 cube-shaped cells with an edge of 2 micrometers (the same total volume). Oxygen use is proportional to volume, and oxygen uptake is proportional to surface area. How does the ratio of total oxygen supply to total oxygen demand compare for the two arrangements?

- A. The ratio is the same, because oxygen use and uptake both scale with the amount of cytoplasm.
- B. The ratio is 4 times lower for the small cells, because they contain more membrane that must be maintained.
- C. The ratio is 8 times higher for the small cells, because there are 8 times as many cells along each dimension.
- **D. The ratio is 4 times higher for the small cells, because total demand is unchanged while total surface area is 4 times larger.**

Check: Total volume is 512 in both cases. Surface area is 6 × 64 = 384 for the large cell and 64 × 24 = 1536 for the small cells, a fourfold increase. Demand depends on volume, which is unchanged.
Rationales: A: uptake scales with surface area, not with volume, so the ratio changes when the surface area changes. B: maintenance of extra membrane is not part of the model, and more surface area increases supply rather than decreasing it. C: the number of cells along each dimension is 4, not 8, and the surface area ratio is 4, not 8.

## Seed N-2.3-A (new) — Topic 2.3 Plasma Membrane
**Skill:** interpret a cell-fusion or fluorescence experiment as evidence about lateral movement of membrane components.

A mouse cell and a human cell are fused. Before fusion, the mouse cell surface proteins are labeled with a red fluorescent antibody and the human cell surface proteins with a green fluorescent antibody. Immediately after fusion, the red and green labels occupy separate halves of the hybrid cell. After 40 minutes at 37°C the colors are evenly mixed over the whole surface. When the experiment is repeated at 15°C, the halves remain separate after 40 minutes. Which conclusion is best supported?

- A. Membrane proteins flip from one side of the bilayer to the other, which mixes the colors.
- B. The mixing occurs because the hybrid cell synthesizes new proteins that spread over the surface.
- **C. Membrane proteins can move laterally in the fluid bilayer, and the rate of movement depends on temperature.**
- D. Membrane proteins are fixed in position by the cytoskeleton at all temperatures.

Check: Mixing at 37°C shows proteins moving sideways in the membrane, and the failure to mix at 15°C shows that this movement slows when the bilayer is colder and less fluid.
Rationales: A: flipping between layers would not mix the colors across the surface, and the labels sit on the outer surface throughout. B: newly made proteins would not be labeled with the antibodies added before fusion, and the effect depends on temperature rather than synthesis. D: if proteins were permanently anchored, no mixing would occur at 37°C either.

**N-2.3-A-v1.** In a photobleaching experiment, a small spot on the surface of a living cell is bleached with a laser so that fluorescently tagged membrane proteins in that spot no longer glow. Over the next 60 seconds, fluorescence in the spot recovers to 80% of its original level for protein P and to only 5% for protein Q. Both proteins are in the same membrane. Which conclusion is best supported?

- A. The membrane is not fluid, because the recovery is incomplete for protein Q.
- B. The cell destroyed protein Q more quickly than it destroyed protein P.
- C. Both proteins are anchored to the cytoskeleton, and recovery for P is caused by new synthesis.
- **D. Protein P moves laterally in the membrane, but movement of protein Q is restricted, for example by attachment to the cytoskeleton.**

Check: Unbleached P molecules diffuse into the spot, restoring fluorescence, while Q is not mobile on this time scale; in the same membrane this points to anchoring or restriction of Q.
Rationales: A: the high recovery of P in the same membrane shows that the bilayer is fluid; Q behaves differently for reasons specific to Q. B: faster breakdown would lower fluorescence everywhere and does not explain why P recovers while Q does not. C: if P were anchored, it could not recover quickly, and synthesis of new protein in 60 seconds would not restore the spot selectively.

**N-2.3-A-v2.** A fluorescent tag is attached to a phospholipid in the outer layer of a membrane. Researchers find that tagged phospholipids spread laterally across the outer layer within a few seconds but take several hours to appear in the inner layer. Which conclusion is best supported?

- A. Phospholipids in a bilayer are fixed in place, which is why the tag takes hours to spread.
- B. Phospholipids move rapidly in both directions, so each layer quickly acquires the same composition.
- C. Phospholipids cannot move laterally unless transport proteins carry them.
- **D. Phospholipids move quickly sideways within a layer but only rarely change layers, so the two layers can keep different compositions.**

Check: Rapid spreading within the outer layer shows lateral mobility; hours to reach the inner layer shows that flipping between layers is rare, so the layers can remain different.
Rationales: A: the tag spread across the outer layer in seconds, so the phospholipids are not fixed. B: movement between layers takes hours, so it is not rapid in that direction. C: lateral movement was seen within seconds without any transport protein being mentioned, so the lipids move freely sideways.

**N-2.3-A-v3.** In a repeat of a cell-fusion experiment, researchers add sodium azide, which stops ATP production, to the fused cells before the experiment. Labeled mouse and human membrane proteins still mix evenly across the surface after 40 minutes at 37°C. Which conclusion is best supported?

- A. Protein movement in the membrane requires ATP, which the cells had already stored.
- B. Mixing is caused by the fusion event itself, not by movement of proteins.
- **C. Lateral movement of membrane proteins is a passive process that does not require cellular energy.**
- D. The cells produce new proteins using energy from the dye, which replaces the lost ATP.

Check: Mixing still occurs without ATP production, so movement of proteins in the bilayer does not require cellular energy; it results from the fluidity of the membrane.
Rationales: A: stored ATP would be depleted by cellular activity when production is blocked, and lateral movement is driven by the membrane fluidity rather than energy use. B: mixing takes 40 minutes after fusion and depends on temperature, so it is a result of ongoing protein movement. D: the dye is an antibody label and has no ability to supply energy or drive protein synthesis.

## Seed N-2.3-B (new) — Topic 2.3 Plasma Membrane
**Skill:** determine which side of a membrane a protein domain faces from enzyme or antibody accessibility data.

A protease that cannot cross the plasma membrane is added to two preparations of red blood cells: intact cells and cells that have been made leaky so the enzyme can reach the cytoplasm. Protein P is digested in both preparations. Protein Q is digested only in the leaky preparation. Both proteins are integral membrane proteins. Which conclusion is best supported?

- **A. Protein P has an exposed region on the outer surface of the cell, and the exposed region of protein Q faces the cytoplasm.**
- B. Protein Q is not part of the membrane, because the protease cannot reach it in intact cells.
- C. Protein P is a peripheral protein, because the protease can digest it from outside the cell.
- D. Protein Q is deeper in the hydrophobic core than protein P, so it is protected from the protease on both sides.

Check: In intact cells the enzyme can reach only the outer surface, so digestion of P in intact cells places a domain of P outside; Q is digested only when the enzyme enters the cytoplasm, so its accessible region faces the cytoplasm.
Rationales: B: the problem states that Q is an integral membrane protein, and its digestion in leaky cells shows it is exposed on the inner surface. C: digestion from outside shows an exposed outer domain, but both proteins are integral, so this does not make P peripheral. D: Q is digested when the enzyme reaches the cytoplasm, which shows an accessible inner domain rather than complete protection.

**N-2.3-B-v1.** An enzyme that removes sugar groups cannot cross the plasma membrane. When it is added to intact cultured cells, the cells lose their ability to be recognized and bound by a particular immune cell, and sugars can be detected in the surrounding fluid. Which conclusion is best supported?

- A. The sugar groups involved in recognition are on the surface facing the cytoplasm.
- **B. The sugar groups involved in recognition are on glycoproteins or glycolipids exposed on the outer surface of the plasma membrane.**
- C. The sugar groups are the main component of the membrane that forms its hydrophobic core.
- D. The enzyme enters the cells and removes sugars from the nucleus, which blocks recognition.

Check: An enzyme that cannot enter the cell changed recognition by an outside cell, so the sugars it removed are on the outer surface and take part in cell recognition.
Rationales: A: an enzyme that cannot cross the membrane cannot reach sugars on the cytoplasmic surface. C: the hydrophobic core is formed by the fatty acid tails of phospholipids, not by sugars. D: the problem states that the enzyme cannot cross the membrane, so it cannot reach the nucleus.

**N-2.3-B-v2.** Two antibodies are made against different ends of membrane protein R. Antibody 1 binds the amino end of R and antibody 2 binds the carboxyl end. Neither antibody can cross the membrane. In intact cells, only antibody 1 binds. After the cells are treated to make their membranes permeable, both antibodies bind. Which model of protein R is best supported?

- A. Protein R lies entirely on the outer surface of the membrane.
- B. Protein R lies entirely on the cytoplasmic surface of the membrane.
- C. Protein R spans the membrane with the carboxyl end on the outside and the amino end inside.
- **D. Protein R spans the membrane with the amino end on the outside and the carboxyl end in the cytoplasm.**

Check: Antibody 1 reaches its site in intact cells, so the amino end is outside; antibody 2 binds only after permeabilization, so the carboxyl end is in the cytoplasm; each end being on opposite sides means R spans the bilayer.
Rationales: A: if both ends were outside, antibody 2 would also bind intact cells. B: if both ends were inside, antibody 1 would not bind intact cells. C: this reverses the orientation, which would make only antibody 2 bind intact cells.

**N-2.3-B-v3.** A protease that cannot cross the membrane is added to intact red blood cells, and the size of protein V is measured. The undigested protein is 100 kilodaltons (kDa). After treatment of intact cells, the protein is 70 kDa. After treatment of leaky cells, where the enzyme reaches both sides, the protein is 40 kDa. Which conclusion is best supported?

- A. Protein V is entirely outside the cell, because it is digested in intact cells.
- B. Protein V is entirely inside the cell, because it is digested to a smaller piece in leaky cells.

D: the fraction making ATP with oxygen is mitochondria, so the two membrane fractions are swapped.

## Seed N-2.10-A (new) — Topic 2.10 Origins of Cell Compartmentalization
**Skill:** interpret molecular sequence, antibiotic, or genome data as evidence for the endosymbiotic origin of mitochondria and chloroplasts.

A researcher compares the gene for ribosomal RNA found in the mitochondria of a yeast cell with three other sequences. The mitochondrial gene is 80% identical to the equivalent gene of a purple bacterium, 55% identical to the equivalent gene of an archaeon, and 52% identical to the yeast's own nuclear gene for ribosomal RNA. Which conclusion is best supported?

- A. Mitochondria arose from the nucleus by infolding of the nuclear envelope.
- **B. Mitochondrial genes are more similar to those of bacteria than to those of the host's nucleus, which supports an origin from an engulfed bacterium.**
- C. Mitochondria are archaeal in origin, because 55% identity is the highest similarity to an archaeon.
- D. The similarity is the result of the bacterium having been derived from mitochondria.

Check: The mitochondrial sequence is much closer to a bacterial sequence (80%) than to the host nuclear sequence (52%) or an archaeal sequence (55%), the pattern predicted if mitochondria descended from a bacterium taken up by a host cell.
Rationales: A: a nuclear origin would predict mitochondrial genes most similar to nuclear genes, but the similarity to the nucleus is the lowest of the three. C: 55% is a lower similarity than the 80% shared with the bacterium, so the data do not point to an archaeal origin of mitochondria. D: the bacterium is a free-living cell, and the data cannot show that it came from an organelle; the usual direction is the reverse.

**N-2.10-A-v1.** The antibiotic chloramphenicol blocks protein synthesis by bacterial ribosomes but does not block protein synthesis by the cytosolic ribosomes of eukaryotic cells. When added to isolated mitochondria from rat liver, it stops mitochondrial protein synthesis, while protein synthesis in the cytosol of the same cells is unaffected. Which conclusion is best supported?

- A. Mitochondria are made in the nucleus, which is why they respond to the antibiotic.
- B. Mitochondria do not carry out protein synthesis, so the observed change must be due to ATP depletion.
- **C. Mitochondrial ribosomes are similar to bacterial ribosomes, which is consistent with a bacterial ancestor.**
- D. Cytosolic ribosomes of the rat cells are bacterial in origin, since they are unaffected.

Check: Mitochondrial ribosomes share the drug sensitivity of bacterial ribosomes but not of the cell's cytosolic ribosomes, supporting a bacterial origin for mitochondria.
Rationales: A: where mitochondria are made does not explain why their ribosomes are drug sensitive, and a nuclear origin would predict the same behavior as cytosolic ribosomes. B: the experiment measures protein synthesis in mitochondria and shows that they carry it out, because it is blocked by the drug. D: the cytosolic ribosomes are unaffected, which shows they differ from bacterial ribosomes rather than resembling them.

**N-2.10-A-v2.** Chloroplast DNA from a spinach leaf is a single circular molecule not bound to histones, and chloroplasts multiply by dividing in two inside the cell. The nuclear DNA of the same cells is in linear chromosomes bound to histones. Which conclusion is best supported?

- **A. The chloroplast genome resembles a bacterial chromosome, which is consistent with a bacterial origin.**
- B. The chloroplast genome shows that chloroplasts arose from the nucleus by infolding of the nuclear envelope.
- C. Circular DNA shows that chloroplasts were originally viruses that infected the cell.
- D. The data show that chloroplasts encode every gene needed to make their own proteins.

Check: A circular, histone-free chromosome and reproduction by binary fission resemble prokaryotes, supporting an origin from an engulfed bacterium.
Rationales: B: a nuclear origin would predict a chromosome like the nuclear one, but the chloroplast genome is circular and has no histones. C: nothing in the data suggests a virus, and circular DNA that is organized like a bacterial chromosome points to a bacterium. D: the data describe the form of the DNA and say nothing about whether it contains every gene needed, and many chloroplast proteins are encoded in the nucleus.

**N-2.10-A-v3.** Many proteins found in mitochondria are encoded by genes in the nucleus. Sequence comparison shows that these nuclear genes are more similar to genes of a group of free-living bacteria than to most other nuclear genes. Which conclusion is best supported?

- A. Mitochondria are still fully independent of the nucleus.
- B. The nuclear genes were acquired recently from the bacteria in the cell's food.
- **C. Some genes from the bacterial ancestor of mitochondria moved to the host nucleus over evolutionary time.**
- D. Mitochondria did not originate from bacteria, because most of their proteins are made from nuclear genes.

Check: Nuclear genes whose closest relatives are bacterial genes are consistent with transfer of genes from the endosymbiont's genome to the nucleus, which explains the host dependence of mitochondria.
Rationales: A: mitochondria import many proteins encoded in the nucleus, so they depend on the nucleus and are not fully independent. B: no information about food is given, and the bacterial-type genes are specifically those encoding mitochondrial proteins, consistent with transfer from the organelle's ancestor. D: transfer of genes to the nucleus is expected from an endosymbiont and does not argue against a bacterial origin.

## Seed N-2.10-B (new) — Topic 2.10 Origins of Cell Compartmentalization
**Skill:** sort organelles by proposed origin (endosymbiosis or membrane infolding) from a table of features.

A biologist lists features of four organelles from a plant cell. Organelle W has two membranes, a circular DNA molecule, ribosomes similar in size to bacterial ribosomes, and divides by splitting in two. Organelle X has a single membrane that is continuous with the nuclear envelope, has no DNA, and has ribosomes attached to its outer surface. Organelle Y has two membranes, a circular DNA molecule, bacterial-sized ribosomes, and a pigment, and divides by splitting in two. Organelle Z has a single membrane, no DNA, an acidic interior with hydrolytic enzymes, and buds from the Golgi complex. Which statement best matches current models of how these organelles arose?

- **A. W and Y arose from engulfed bacteria, while X and Z arose from the cell's own membrane system.**
- B. All four arose by endosymbiosis, because all are surrounded by membranes.
- C. X and Z arose by endosymbiosis, because they contain ribosomes or enzymes, while W and Y arose by infolding.
- D. W and X arose by endosymbiosis, because both have ribosomes, while Y and Z arose by infolding.

Check: Two membranes together with their own circular DNA, bacterial-sized ribosomes, and fission-type division indicate an endosymbiotic origin (W and Y). A single membrane, continuity with the nuclear envelope, and no genome indicate organelles derived from the cell's own endomembrane system (X and Z).
Rationales: B: being surrounded by a membrane is common to all organelles and is not evidence for endosymbiosis; the other features are what distinguish W and Y. C: this reverses the assignment, because the features of an independent genome and division by splitting are those of W and Y. D: the ribosomes on X are attached to its surface and are the cell's cytosolic ones, and X is continuous with the nuclear envelope, so it is not endosymbiotic.

**N-2.10-B-v1.** A student claims that the nucleus must have arisen by endosymbiosis because it is surrounded by two membranes. Which observation about the nucleus best argues against the claim?

- A. The nuclear envelope has pores, which bacteria do not have.
- B. The nucleus contains DNA, which mitochondria and chloroplasts also contain.
- **C. The nuclear envelope is continuous with the endoplasmic reticulum, and the nucleus has no independent genome or machinery for dividing by fission.**
- D. The nucleus is larger than a typical bacterial cell.

Check: Continuity with the ER suggests derivation from the cell's own membranes by infolding, and an endosymbiont would be expected to retain its own genome and divide independently; the nucleus does neither.
Rationales: A: pores are a feature of the nuclear envelope but do not indicate whether it formed by infolding or engulfment of a bacterium. B: DNA is present in both the nucleus and in organelles of endosymbiotic origin, so its presence does not help refute the claim. D: size alone does not show origin, and some bacteria are large.

**N-2.10-B-v2.** A single-celled eukaryote contains a cyanobacterium-like body inside its cytoplasm. The body has a circular genome of about one million base pairs, one third the size of its free-living relatives' genomes, and cannot survive outside the host. The host nucleus encodes some of the body's proteins, and the body divides in step with the host cell. Which statement best fits these observations?

- A. The body is a pathogen that has evolved a larger genome than its relatives.
- B. The body arose from the host's endoplasmic reticulum by infolding.
- **C. The body resembles an organelle in an early stage of becoming integrated, with gene loss, gene transfer to the host, and host-controlled division.**
- D. The body is an example of the origin of mitochondria, because mitochondria also came from cyanobacteria.

Check: A reduced genome, dependence on the host, host-encoded proteins, and synchronized division are expected as an endosymbiont is integrated into a host cell.
Rationales: A: the genome is smaller than those of free-living relatives, not larger, and the body is described as integrated into the host. B: the body has its own circular genome and a bacterial-like form, which would not be expected for a structure derived from host membrane. D: mitochondria are thought to have descended from an ancestor of proteobacteria, whereas chloroplasts descended from cyanobacteria.

**N-2.10-B-v3.** A plant cell has the following structures: mitochondria, chloroplasts, rough endoplasmic reticulum, a Golgi complex, and a large central vacuole. Which statement about the origin of these structures best matches current models?

- A. Only the chloroplasts arose by endosymbiosis, because mitochondria arose by infolding of the plasma membrane.
- B. Mitochondria, chloroplasts, and the central vacuole arose by endosymbiosis, because all contain fluid.
- **C. Mitochondria and chloroplasts arose by endosymbiosis, while the endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi complex, and vacuole are part of the cell's own endomembrane system.**
- D. All five arose by endosymbiosis, because all are specialized compartments inside the cell.

Check: Mitochondria and chloroplasts have their own genomes and bacteria-like features, matching an endosymbiotic origin, whereas the ER, Golgi complex, and vacuole belong to the interconnected endomembrane system that is attributed to infolding.
Rationales: A: mitochondria also have a double membrane, a circular genome, and bacterial-type ribosomes, so they are included in the endosymbiotic group. B: a vacuole has a single membrane and no genome, and containing fluid is not evidence of an endosymbiotic origin. D: being a compartment does not indicate endosymbiosis, since the endomembrane compartments lack their own genomes.

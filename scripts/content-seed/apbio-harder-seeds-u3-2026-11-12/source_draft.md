## Seed N-3.1-A (replaces 122 family) — Topic 3.1 Enzymes
**Skill:** tell competitive from noncompetitive inhibition using rate data at different substrate concentrations.

An enzyme is assayed with and without a reversible inhibitor. Enzyme concentration, temperature and pH are the same in every tube. Initial rates (relative units per minute) are:

Substrate (mM) | No inhibitor | With inhibitor
2 | 33 | 14
4 | 50 | 25
10 | 71 | 45
40 | 91 | 77
200 | 98 | 94

Which conclusion is best supported by the data?

- A. The inhibitor is noncompetitive: it binds away from the active site and lowers the maximum rate at every substrate concentration.
- B. The inhibitor permanently denatures a fraction of the enzyme molecules, so fewer enzyme molecules are available at every substrate concentration.
- **C. The inhibitor is competitive: it competes with the substrate for the active site, so a high substrate concentration nearly overcomes it.**
- D. The inhibitor binds to the substrate and removes it from solution, so the rates fall by the same amount at every substrate concentration.

Check: the rate gap shrinks from 19 units at 2 mM to 4 units at 200 mM and both curves approach the same maximum, which is the signature of competition for the active site.
Rationales: A: a noncompetitive inhibitor lowers the maximum rate, so the gap would remain large at 200 mM, but here the rates nearly converge (94 versus 98). B: loss of active enzyme molecules would lower the maximum rate at saturating substrate too, and the rates would not converge. D: the gap is not constant, since it falls from 19 to 4 units as substrate rises, which a fixed loss of substrate would not produce.

**N-3.1-A-v1.** A second inhibitor is tested on the same enzyme under identical conditions. Initial rates (relative units per minute) are:

Substrate (mM) | No inhibitor | With inhibitor
2 | 33 | 17
4 | 50 | 25
10 | 71 | 36
40 | 91 | 45
200 | 98 | 49

A student proposes raising the substrate from 40 mM to 200 mM to restore the uninhibited rate. Which statement best evaluates the proposal?

- **A. It will fail: the rate with inhibitor stays near half of the uninhibited rate at every substrate concentration, which fits an inhibitor that binds outside the active site and lowers the maximum rate.**
- B. It will succeed, because every inhibitor loses its effect once substrate is added in large excess.
- C. It will fail only because the enzyme is denatured at high substrate concentration, which is why the rate stops rising.
- D. It will succeed, because the rates at 2 mM and 4 mM show the inhibitor is weaker at higher substrate.

Check: the inhibited rate is about 50 percent of the uninhibited rate at every concentration (17/33, 25/50, 36/71, 45/91, 49/98), so substrate cannot overcome it, as expected for a noncompetitive inhibitor.
Rationales: B: only a competitive inhibitor is overcome by excess substrate, and this data shows a constant fractional reduction. C: the uninhibited curve also levels off at high substrate because the enzyme is saturated, not denatured. D: the ratio of inhibited to uninhibited rate does not rise with substrate, so the inhibitor is not weaker at higher substrate.

**N-3.1-A-v2.** A drug has a shape and charge distribution similar to an enzyme's substrate and binds reversibly to the active site. In an assay containing 1 mM substrate and the drug, the rate is 30 percent of the uninhibited rate. A researcher raises the substrate concentration to 100 mM while keeping the drug concentration the same. Which prediction and explanation are best?

- A. The rate stays at about 30 percent, because the drug lowers the enzyme's maximum rate regardless of substrate.
- B. The rate stays at about 30 percent, because the drug changes the shape of the active site by binding elsewhere on the enzyme.
- C. The rate rises toward the uninhibited rate, because extra substrate lowers the activation energy of the reaction.
- **D. The rate rises toward the uninhibited rate, because substrate and drug compete for the same active sites and more substrate occupies more of them.**

Check: a reversible inhibitor that resembles the substrate and binds the active site is competitive, so its effect is reduced when substrate greatly outnumbers it.
Rationales: A: lowering the maximum rate regardless of substrate describes noncompetitive inhibition, whereas this drug competes for the active site. B: binding elsewhere describes an allosteric or noncompetitive inhibitor, but the stem says the drug binds the active site. C: substrate concentration does not change the activation energy, since only the enzyme and its structure do that.

**N-3.1-A-v3.** Inhibitors X and Y each reduce the activity of the same enzyme. Initial rates (relative units per minute) at two substrate concentrations are:

Condition | 10 mM substrate | 100 mM substrate
No inhibitor | 60 | 94
Inhibitor X | 45 | 89
Inhibitor Y | 30 | 47

Which interpretation is best supported?

- A. Both are competitive, because both lower the rate at 10 mM substrate.
- **B. X is likely competitive because 100 mM substrate nearly restores the rate, while Y is likely noncompetitive because the rate stays about half of normal.**
- C. X is likely noncompetitive because it lowers the rate at 10 mM, while Y is likely competitive because it lowers the rate more.
- D. Both are noncompetitive, because neither reaches the uninhibited rate at 100 mM.

Check: with X the rate recovers from 75 percent to 95 percent of normal as substrate rises, while with Y it stays near 50 percent, so X behaves competitively and Y noncompetitively.
Rationales: A: every inhibitor lowers the rate at some concentration, so that observation cannot distinguish the types; the 100 mM data can. C: the larger drop at low substrate does not identify a type, and the rate with Y does not recover, which is the noncompetitive pattern. D: X comes within about 5 percent of the uninhibited rate at 100 mM, which is consistent with competition and not with a fixed loss of capacity.

## Seed N-3.1-B (replaces 123 family) — Topic 3.1 Enzymes
**Skill:** choose the control that isolates enzyme activity in an experiment.

A student grinds potato tissue and filters the liquid. When she adds 1 mL of the extract to hydrogen peroxide, bubbles form quickly. She claims that an enzyme in the extract causes the bubbling. Which additional treatment would best test whether the bubbling depends on the enzyme's native, folded structure rather than on some other component of the extract?

- A. Add 1 mL of extract to water in place of hydrogen peroxide.
- **B. Add 1 mL of the same extract that was first heated in boiling water and then cooled, to the same hydrogen peroxide solution.**
- C. Add 2 mL of the untreated extract to the same hydrogen peroxide solution.
- D. Add 1 mL of the untreated extract to a hydrogen peroxide solution at a lower concentration.

Check: boiled extract contains the same chemical components but has denatured proteins, so if bubbling stops the difference must be due to the folded protein.
Rationales: A: this shows whether the extract alone releases gas, but it does not separate enzyme activity from other extract components when peroxide is present. C: doubling the amount is a dose comparison that changes a variable and cannot show that the activity depends on protein structure. D: changing the peroxide concentration tests a substrate effect and does not distinguish an enzyme from other components of the extract.

**N-3.1-B-v1.** A student adds pancreatic extract to milk containing an indicator that turns from pink to colorless as fatty acids form. The color fades within minutes, and she claims lipase in the extract is responsible. Which control best shows that the change depends on an active, properly folded enzyme?

- **A. Use the same amount of extract that was heated to denature its proteins and then cooled to the same temperature.**
- B. Use the same amount of extract with the milk adjusted to a different pH.
- C. Use ice-cold milk with the same amount of untreated extract.
- D. Use the same amount of extract with a larger volume of milk.

Check: the heated extract keeps every other component but loses folded protein, so persistence of the pink color would show the change depends on active enzyme.
Rationales: B: changing the pH introduces a second variable and tests a pH effect, not the dependence on enzyme structure. C: lowering the temperature slows an enzyme without destroying it, so a slow color change would not show that the protein structure is responsible. D: changing the milk volume changes the substrate amount and says nothing about whether the active enzyme is required.

**N-3.1-B-v2.** An inhibitor is dissolved in the solvent DMSO. When 20 microliters of the inhibitor solution is added to an assay, the rate of the reaction falls from 50 to 20 units per minute. Which control best shows that the decrease is caused by the inhibitor and not by the solvent?

- A. An assay with the inhibitor solution but with no substrate.
- B. An assay with twice the amount of inhibitor solution.
- C. An assay with no enzyme but with the inhibitor solution.
- **D. An assay with 20 microliters of DMSO that contains no inhibitor.**

Check: the solvent-only assay matches everything except the inhibitor, so if the rate stays near 50 the inhibitor and not the solvent caused the drop.
Rationales: A: without substrate there is no reaction to measure, so this assay cannot show what the solvent does to the rate. B: doubling the dose tests whether the effect increases with dose, but it still contains the solvent, so it cannot separate solvent from inhibitor. C: without the enzyme the reaction rate says nothing about how the solvent affects the enzyme.

**N-3.1-B-v3.** A student uses the iodine test, which turns blue-black with starch, to detect amylase activity in saliva. Before testing saliva she wants to confirm that her assay can detect starch breakdown at all. Which tube is the best positive control?

- A. Starch solution with boiled saliva, then iodine.
- **B. Starch solution with a commercial amylase solution known to be active, then iodine.**
- C. Starch solution with water instead of saliva, then iodine.
- D. Water with saliva, with no starch, then iodine.

Check: a positive control uses a treatment known to produce the effect; active amylase with starch should lose the blue-black color if the assay works.
Rationales: A: boiled saliva is a negative control because its enzyme is denatured and no starch breakdown is expected. C: starch with water is a negative control that shows what no breakdown looks like. D: there is no starch in this tube, so no breakdown is possible and the tube does not test the assay's ability to detect it.

## Seed N-3.1-C (new) — Topic 3.1 Enzymes
**Skill:** predict the effect of allosteric feedback inhibition in a metabolic pathway.

In a bacterial biosynthetic pathway, enzyme E1 converts substrate A to B, E2 converts B to C, and E3 converts C to the end product D. D binds reversibly to a site on E1 that is separate from the active site. A researcher adds excess D to a cell extract that contains all three enzymes and plenty of A. Which prediction and explanation are best?

- A. The rate of conversion of A to B increases, because D is a product of the pathway and more D pulls the pathway forward.
- B. The rate of conversion of A to B stays the same, because D can act only on E3, the enzyme that makes it.
- C. The rate of conversion of A to B falls, because D competes with A for the active site, so raising A would fully restore the rate.
- **D. The rate of conversion of A to B falls, because D binds a site other than the active site and changes the shape of E1, which reduces its activity.**

Check: feedback inhibition by an end product that binds an allosteric site shuts down the first step, which prevents the cell from wasting resources on excess D.
Rationales: A: products of the pathway inhibit the early enzyme rather than stimulating it, which is how the cell avoids overproduction. B: the stem says D binds E1, so D acts on the first step and not only on E3. C: D binds a site separate from the active site, so it does not compete with A, and added A would not fully restore the rate.

**N-3.1-C-v1.** In the same pathway (E1 makes B from A, E2 makes C from B, E3 makes D from C, and D inhibits E1 allosterically), a cell is growing with a moderate level of D that partly inhibits E1. A drug then completely and irreversibly blocks E2. Which change is most likely over the next few minutes?

- A. A accumulates and B falls, because the drug blocks E1.
- **B. D falls, so inhibition of E1 is relieved and B accumulates because E2 can no longer remove it.**
- C. C accumulates, because C is made by E2 even when E2 is blocked.
- D. All intermediates fall, because blocking one enzyme shuts down every reaction in the pathway at once.

Check: blocking E2 stops the production of C and D, so the inhibitor D falls and E1 speeds up, while B is made faster and not removed.
Rationales: A: the drug acts on E2, and E1 becomes more active as D falls, so A would be consumed and not accumulate. C: C is produced only by E2, so with E2 blocked its supply stops and it does not accumulate. D: the product of the unblocked first enzyme, B, builds up, so not every intermediate falls.

**N-3.1-C-v2.** A mutant bacterium has an E1 whose active site is unchanged but which can no longer bind the end product D at its regulatory site. Compared with the normal strain growing under the same conditions, which prediction is best?

- A. The mutant makes less D, because the mutation weakened the active site of E1.
- B. The mutant makes the same amount of D, because regulatory sites never affect enzyme activity.
- **C. The mutant accumulates much more D, because E1 stays active even when D is abundant.**
- D. The mutant stops making D, because without regulation the pathway cannot run.

Check: with the regulatory site unable to bind D, feedback is lost, so E1 continues to work at a high rate and D builds up.
Rationales: A: the stem states the active site is unchanged, and the lost feature is the regulatory binding site. B: binding at a regulatory site normally lowers the enzyme's activity, so losing it changes the amount of D made. D: the pathway still runs because the active site works, and the loss of feedback lets it run unchecked.

**N-3.1-C-v3.** An enzyme that catalyzes the first step of a pathway has an activity of 100 units in the absence of the end product P and 20 units when P is present. After the sample is dialyzed to remove P, the activity returns to 95 units. Which conclusion is best supported?

- **A. P binds the enzyme reversibly and alters its activity without permanently damaging the enzyme.**
- B. P permanently denatures the enzyme, which is why activity falls.
- C. P is used up as a substrate by the enzyme, which is why activity falls.
- D. P covalently modifies the enzyme and the dialysis removes the modification.

Check: activity recovered to 95 percent once P was removed, which means that P binds reversibly and does not destroy the enzyme.
Rationales: B: permanent denaturation would prevent the activity from returning when P is removed. C: if P were consumed as a substrate, activity would rise and not fall when P is present. D: dialysis removes small free molecules and cannot reverse covalent changes, so the recovery points to reversible binding.

## Seed N-3.2-A (new) — Topic 3.2 Environmental Impacts on Enzyme Function
**Skill:** interpret rate-versus-temperature data for two enzymes with different optima.

A researcher measures the initial rate of a similar reaction catalyzed by an enzyme from a human cell and an enzyme from a hot-spring bacterium. Substrate concentration and pH are identical in all tests.

Temperature (°C) | Human enzyme rate | Hot-spring enzyme rate
20 | 6 | 1
37 | 20 | 3
60 | 2 | 12
80 | 0 | 20
95 | 0 | 4

Which statement is best supported by the data?

- A. The hot-spring enzyme is denatured at 20°C and 37°C, which is why its rates there are low.
- B. The human enzyme's rate falls above 37°C because the substrate is used up at higher temperatures.
- **C. Each rate rises with temperature up to the enzyme's optimum and then falls as heat disrupts the active site, and the hot-spring enzyme's optimum is much higher than the human enzyme's.**
- D. The hot-spring enzyme has a higher rate at every temperature tested because it comes from an extremophile.

Check: the human enzyme peaks near 37°C and the hot-spring enzyme near 80°C; below the optimum low rates reflect low kinetic energy, above it they reflect loss of active-site shape.
Rationales: A: cool temperatures slow molecular motion and collisions but do not denature a protein, so the low rate at 20°C does not indicate denaturation. B: the stem holds the substrate concentration the same, and the decline at high temperature comes from loss of the active-site shape. D: the human enzyme has higher rates at 20°C and 37°C (6 and 20 versus 1 and 3), so the claim of a higher rate at every temperature is false.

**N-3.2-A-v1.** The activities of pepsin (found in the stomach) and trypsin (found in the small intestine) are measured at several pH values with temperature and substrate constant.

pH | Pepsin rate | Trypsin rate
2 | 10 | 0
5 | 2 | 3
8 | 0 | 10
11 | 0 | 1

Which explanation best accounts for these results?

- A. Pepsin cannot function at pH 8 because there are no hydrogen ions available to act as its substrate.
- B. The two enzymes have the same optimum, and the differences between them are measurement error.
- C. Trypsin is inactive at pH 2 because the acid hydrolyzes its peptide bonds and permanently cuts the polypeptide.
- **D. pH changes the ionization of amino acid side chains, which alters the interactions that maintain the active site, so each enzyme works best near the pH of its own environment.**

Check: pepsin peaks at pH 2 and trypsin at pH 8, matching the stomach and intestine; pH affects R-group charge and therefore the shape and binding of the active site.
Rationales: A: hydrogen ions are not the substrate of these enzymes, and pepsin loses activity at high pH because of altered structure. B: the peaks are far apart (pH 2 versus pH 8) and the pattern is clear, so the difference reflects different structures and not error. C: a brief exposure to pH 2 changes side-chain charge and shape, but it does not break peptide bonds, so the explanation by hydrolysis is incorrect.

**N-3.2-A-v2.** The initial rate of an enzyme at pH 7 and several temperatures is measured:

Temperature (°C) | Rate (relative units)
10 | 4
20 | 8
30 | 15
40 | 3

Which prediction for 25°C and 50°C is best supported?

- A. At 25°C the rate is between 3 and 8, and at 50°C it is above 15.
- **B. At 25°C the rate is between 8 and 15, and at 50°C it is below 3 because even more of the enzyme is denatured.**
- C. At 25°C the rate is above 15, and at 50°C it is between 8 and 15.
- D. At 25°C the rate is between 8 and 15, and at 50°C it stays near 15 because kinetic energy keeps rising.

Check: the rate rises from 20°C to 30°C, so 25°C lies between 8 and 15; the rate already collapses at 40°C from heat damage, so more damage at 50°C gives an even lower rate.
Rationales: A: the rate at 25°C must fall between the values at 20°C and 30°C, and it cannot be below 8, while the high rate at 50°C ignores the collapse seen at 40°C. C: the rate at 25°C cannot exceed the 30°C value of 15 when the trend is rising, and 50°C would not recover to a mid-level rate. D: increasing kinetic energy does not help once the active site is distorted, as the fall at 40°C shows.

**N-3.2-A-v3.** An enzyme is assayed at two temperatures and two substrate concentrations. Enzyme amount and pH are identical.

Temperature | 1 mM substrate | 50 mM substrate
25°C | 10 | 40
55°C | 1 | 3

A student proposes to rescue the activity at 55°C by adding more substrate. Which evaluation is best?

- **A. It will not help much: at 55°C the maximum rate is lowered because much of the enzyme has lost its active-site shape, and more substrate cannot restore the lost enzyme.**
- B. It will fully restore activity, because substrate concentration alone limits every enzyme.
- C. It will help because higher substrate concentrations make the active site refold.
- D. It will not help, because substrate cannot bind a heated enzyme at all.

Check: even at 50 mM substrate the rate at 55°C is only 3 compared with 40 at 25°C, so the limit is the amount of functional enzyme and not the substrate supply.
Rationales: B: raising substrate helps only if substrate is limiting, and the rate at 55°C stays low even at 50 mM, which shows the enzyme is the limit. C: substrate does not cause a denatured protein to refold, and the data show no recovery. D: the rate rises from 1 to 3 with more substrate, so some functional enzyme still binds substrate, and the issue is that most of it is lost.

## Seed N-3.2-B (replaces 125 family) — Topic 3.2 Environmental Impacts on Enzyme Function
**Skill:** identify and fix a design flaw in an experiment on environmental effects on enzymes.

A student measures catalase activity in potato at 5, 20, 35 and 60°C by placing a potato disc in hydrogen peroxide and counting oxygen bubbles for one minute. Because she ran short, she used 3 percent hydrogen peroxide at 5°C and 20°C but 5 percent at 35°C and 60°C. All other conditions were identical. Which change would most improve the validity of her conclusion about temperature?

- **A. Use the same hydrogen peroxide concentration in every tube so that temperature is the only variable that differs.**
- B. Use a different potato at each temperature so that differences between potatoes average out.
- C. Increase the peroxide concentration further as temperature rises to compensate for the faster collisions.
- D. Use a different buffer in each tube so that pH rises with the temperature.

Check: the substrate concentration differs between tubes, so a rate difference between 20°C and 35°C could come from the concentration change and not from temperature.
Rationales: B: using a different potato at each temperature adds a second variable and cannot be averaged out with one trial per temperature. C: adjusting concentration with temperature creates an even larger confounded difference between tubes. D: changing the pH across the tubes adds another variable and makes the effect of temperature harder to isolate.

**N-3.2-B-v1.** A student measures the product formed after 30 minutes by an enzyme at five temperatures, starting with 100 micromoles of substrate in every tube.

Temperature | Product at 30 min (micromoles)
5°C | 20
20°C | 100
35°C | 100
50°C | 100
70°C | 40

She concludes that the enzyme works equally well from 20 to 50°C. Which change would best improve the experiment?

- A. Run all tubes for 60 minutes so the lower temperatures can catch up.
- B. Remove the 70°C tube because it does not fit the trend.
- **C. Measure product at several early time points and compare initial rates, before the substrate is used up.**
- D. Double the starting substrate in every tube so that the reaction lasts longer in each tube.

Check: the three tubes all converted every micromole of substrate, so the end point hides rate differences; initial rates would reveal which temperature is fastest.
Rationales: A: a longer run makes the end-point problem worse, because even more tubes would be at the same completed endpoint. B: removing data that does not fit is poor practice, and the 70°C tube carries real information about heat damage. D: the problem is that a single late measurement hides the rates, and doubling the substrate still leaves one end-point reading that does not reveal which temperature is fastest.

**N-3.2-B-v2.** A student tests the effect of pH on an enzyme. Tubes at pH 4, 7 and 10 are prepared. The pH 4 tube is kept on ice until the assay, the pH 7 tube is at room temperature, and the pH 10 tube is warmed to 37°C, because that is what was convenient. Initial rates are 3, 15 and 6. Which change would best allow a valid conclusion about pH?

- A. Use a larger number of different pH values and keep the original temperatures.
- B. Use a fresh enzyme sample at each pH and keep the original temperatures.
- **C. Bring all tubes to the same temperature before adding the enzyme so that pH is the only variable that differs.**
- D. Run only the pH 7 tube at 37°C because that is closest to body temperature.

Check: temperature differs between tubes, so rate differences cannot be assigned to pH until all tubes share one temperature.
Rationales: A: more pH values do not fix the confounding because each would still carry a different temperature. B: fresh enzyme at each pH does not change that the tubes differ in temperature, and the confounding remains. D: testing only one tube at 37°C does not give a comparison across pH and does not remove the differing temperatures from the other tubes.

**N-3.2-B-v3.** A student measures the rate of an enzyme at 30°C and at 35°C using one tube at each temperature. The rates are 12.0 and 12.8 units. She concludes that the enzyme is more active at 35°C. Which change would best strengthen the conclusion?

- A. Increase the temperature difference to 3°C by repeating the 35°C tube at 38°C.
- B. Use only the 35°C tube, since it had the higher rate.
- C. Switch to a higher substrate concentration in the 35°C tube only.
- **D. Run several replicate tubes at each temperature and compare the means and the spread of the replicates.**

Check: a difference of 0.8 units from single tubes could be within normal variation, and replicates allow the difference to be judged against the spread.
Rationales: A: changing the 35°C condition to 38°C does not address whether the original difference was real, and it changes the variable under test. B: dropping the 30°C tube removes the comparison that the claim depends on. C: raising substrate only in one tube adds a second variable and makes the comparison invalid.

## Seed N-3.3-A (replaces 126 family) — Topic 3.3 Cellular Energy
**Skill:** use free-energy changes to predict whether coupled reactions proceed.

A cell converts compound X to compound Y in a reaction with a free-energy change of +20 kJ/mol. The hydrolysis of ATP to ADP and phosphate has a free-energy change of −30 kJ/mol. An enzyme couples the two by transferring the terminal phosphate of ATP to X. Which prediction is best?

- A. The coupled reaction has a free-energy change of −50 kJ/mol, because the two magnitudes add.
- **B. The coupled reaction has a free-energy change of −10 kJ/mol, so it can proceed spontaneously.**
- C. The coupled reaction keeps a free-energy change of +20 kJ/mol, so it still does not proceed.
- D. The coupled reaction has a free-energy change of +50 kJ/mol, because the ATP hydrolysis adds a cost.

Check: the free-energy changes of coupled reactions add algebraically, +20 + (−30) = −10 kJ/mol, which is negative, so the overall process is spontaneous.
Rationales: A: the values must be added with their signs, and adding magnitudes treats an energy cost as a gain. C: coupling changes the overall free-energy change because the ATP hydrolysis is part of the coupled reaction. D: ATP hydrolysis is exergonic and contributes a negative value, so it lowers the total and does not raise it.

**N-3.3-A-v1.** A reaction has a free-energy change of +35 kJ/mol and is coupled by an enzyme to the hydrolysis of one ATP (−30 kJ/mol) under the same conditions. Which prediction is best?

- A. The coupled reaction proceeds, because any exergonic reaction drives any endergonic reaction.
- B. The coupled reaction has an overall change of −30 kJ/mol, because the more negative step determines the result.
- C. The coupled reaction is spontaneous because the enzyme lowers the activation energy.
- **D. The coupled reaction has an overall change of +5 kJ/mol and would not proceed, but coupling to two ATP (−60 kJ/mol) would give −25 kJ/mol.**

Check: +35 − 30 = +5 kJ/mol, which is positive; adding a second ATP hydrolysis gives +35 − 60 = −25 kJ/mol, which is negative.
Rationales: A: the coupled process proceeds only if the sum of the free-energy changes is negative, which is not the case here. B: the sum is +35 − 30, and the larger value does not simply dominate by being more negative. C: an enzyme lowers activation energy but does not change the free-energy change, so it cannot make a positive net value spontaneous.

**N-3.3-A-v2.** In a cell, the formation of ATP from ADP and phosphate has a free-energy change of +30 kJ/mol. An enzyme couples this to the breakdown of phosphoenolpyruvate, which releases −62 kJ/mol. Which statement is best?

- **A. The coupled reaction has a free-energy change of −32 kJ/mol, so ATP formation can proceed.**
- B. The coupled reaction has a free-energy change of +92 kJ/mol, so ATP cannot form.
- C. The coupled reaction has a free-energy change of −62 kJ/mol, because the breakdown of phosphoenolpyruvate is unaffected by the coupling.
- D. The coupled reaction has a free-energy change of −30 kJ/mol, because ATP formation always releases energy.

Check: −62 + 30 = −32 kJ/mol, which is negative, so ATP synthesis driven by this reaction can proceed.
Rationales: B: the +92 value comes from subtracting the sign of an exergonic reaction, whereas it contributes a negative term. C: the two free-energy changes add to give a single value, and the reaction with positive value contributes to it. D: ATP formation from ADP and phosphate requires an energy input and has a positive free-energy change.

**N-3.3-A-v3.** An enzyme is added to a solution of X, which converts to Y in a reaction with a free-energy change of +20 kJ/mol. No ATP is present. Which prediction about the reaction after a long time is best?

- A. The enzyme makes the reaction spontaneous because it lowers the activation energy, so most X becomes Y.
- B. The enzyme speeds the approach to equilibrium and shifts the equilibrium toward Y, so at equilibrium most of the material is Y.
- **C. The enzyme speeds the approach to equilibrium, but at equilibrium most of the material is still X because the enzyme does not change the free-energy change.**
- D. The enzyme supplies energy from its own bonds, so the reaction proceeds until all X is gone.

Check: a catalyst changes the rate and not the free-energy change or the equilibrium position, so the reaction with ΔG = +20 kJ/mol still favors X.
Rationales: A: lowering activation energy changes speed only, and the free-energy change that sets the equilibrium is unchanged. B: an enzyme does not shift the equilibrium, because it speeds the forward and reverse reactions equally and leaves the free-energy difference between X and Y unchanged. D: enzymes are not consumed and do not donate energy from their own bonds to drive reactions.

## Seed N-3.3-B (replaces 030 apoptosis) — Topic 3.3 Cellular Energy
**Skill:** explain ATP cycling: ATP use and regeneration.

In skeletal muscle, the concentration of ATP is about 5.0 mM at rest and about 4.8 mM during intense contraction, even though the rate of ATP hydrolysis is about 20 times higher during contraction. Which explanation best accounts for the nearly constant ATP concentration?

- B. ATP hydrolysis releases energy that creates new ATP molecules directly, so the pool is replaced by hydrolysis itself.
- C. Contraction uses energy directly from glucose and not from ATP, so ATP hydrolysis hardly affects the pool.

## Seed N-4.1-A (replaces 026 family) — Topic 4.1 Cell Communication
**Skill:** classify a signaling type (juxtacrine, paracrine, autocrine, endocrine) from experimental results.

Researchers study how cell type X signals to target cells T in culture. (1) When X cells are mixed with T cells and touch them, T cells turn on gene Y. (2) When medium in which X cells grew for 24 hours is added to a separate dish of T cells, gene Y stays off. (3) When X and T cells share a dish but are separated by a porous membrane that lets proteins pass but prevents cell contact, gene Y stays off. (4) When T cells are treated with an antibody that blocks their receptor, touching X cells no longer turns on gene Y. Which best classifies the signaling from X to T?

- A. Paracrine signaling, because X releases a diffusible signal that acts on nearby T cells.
- **B. Contact-dependent (juxtacrine) signaling, because a signal on the surface of X binds the receptor on T only when the cells touch.**
- C. Endocrine signaling, because X releases a signal that travels through the fluid to distant T cells.
- D. Autocrine signaling, because X cells respond to a signal they make themselves.

Check: the response needs direct contact (results 1 to 3), and a receptor on T is required (result 4), so a membrane-bound signal on X binds a receptor on T.
Rationales: A: a diffusible signal would have crossed the porous membrane and would have worked in the conditioned medium, and neither of those turned on gene Y. C: a signal carried in fluid would also have worked in the conditioned medium and across the porous membrane. D: autocrine signals act on the cell that makes them, but here it is the T cells that respond and only when touched.

**N-4.1-A-v1.** In a developing tissue, a small group of cells secretes protein P. Cells within about 100 µm of the group turn on gene G, including cells that do not touch the group. Cells farther than 200 µm away do not respond. Protein P is broken down by enzymes in the extracellular fluid within minutes. When the group of cells is transplanted to a new site, cells within 100 µm of the graft turn on gene G. Which best classifies this signaling?

- **A. Paracrine signaling, because a short-lived secreted signal acts only on nearby cells.**
- B. Endocrine signaling, because a secreted protein travels to target cells in other parts of the body.
- C. Contact-dependent signaling, because the responding cells must be in direct membrane contact with the group.
- D. Autocrine signaling, because the cells that secrete P are the ones that respond to it.

Check: nearby cells respond without touching, the response falls to zero with distance, and P is degraded quickly, which describes a local diffusible signal.
Rationales: B: endocrine signals are carried by the bloodstream to distant tissues, and here P is degraded locally and acts over 100 µm only. C: cells that do not touch the group still respond, so direct contact is not required. D: the responding cells are neighbors of the signaling group, so the signal acts on other cells and not just on the cells that make it.

**N-4.1-A-v2.** A tumor cell line makes growth factor G and has receptors for it. Single cells plated one per well, far from any other cell, still divide in medium with no added growth factor. Adding an antibody that binds and neutralizes G to the medium stops their division, and so does an antibody that blocks the G receptor. Which best classifies the signaling that drives division in these cells?

- A. Paracrine signaling, because G acts on nearby cells.
- B. Contact-dependent signaling, because G is attached to the membrane of the neighboring cell.
- C. Endocrine signaling, because G is carried through fluid to target cells.
- **D. Autocrine signaling, because the cell makes a signal that binds its own receptors.**

Check: each well holds only one cell, so the signal can only come from the cell itself and act on the same cell's receptor; blocking either G or its receptor stops division.
Rationales: A: paracrine signaling needs a different nearby cell to receive the signal, and each well has only one cell. B: there is no neighboring cell to touch, since each cell is alone in its well. C: endocrine signaling involves a distant target cell reached by the bloodstream, and no such cell exists in a culture well.

**N-4.1-A-v3.** A gland is removed from a rat, and the rat's liver cells stop breaking down glycogen. When a piece of the gland is transplanted under the skin of the ear, 20 cm from the liver, glycogen breakdown resumes. Skin cells next to the graft, which lack the receptor for the gland's hormone, show no response. Which statement best explains these results?

- A. The hormone acts as an autocrine signal, so only the gland cells in the graft respond to it.
- B. The hormone acts as a paracrine signal, so the response depends on how close the target cells are to the graft.
- **C. The hormone acts as an endocrine signal: it travels in the blood to distant cells, and only cells with the receptor respond.**
- D. The hormone acts through direct cell-to-cell contact, so the graft must have grown into the liver.

Check: the graft is far from the liver yet restores the response, so the signal travels through the blood; neighboring skin cells without the receptor do not respond, so the receptor determines which cells respond.
Rationales: A: the response is seen in liver cells far from the graft, so the signal acts on cells other than the ones that make it. B: the liver is 20 cm from the graft and still responds, and nearby skin cells do not, so distance from the source does not decide the response. D: the graft is under the skin of the ear and responds across 20 cm, so contact between the graft and liver cells is not possible.

## Seed N-4.1-B (new) — Topic 4.1 Cell Communication
**Skill:** separate ligand binding from the cell's response using a table of results.

Ligand L binds a single receptor protein, R, which an antibody test shows is present on the surface of cell types 1, 2 and 4. Cell type 3 does not make R. A researcher adds L at the same concentration to each type.

Cell type | Labeled L bound | Response to L
1 | high | breaks down glycogen
2 | high | opens an ion channel
3 | none | none
4 | high | none

Which conclusion is best supported by all four rows?

- A. The structure of L alone determines the response, so L should cause the same response in every cell that binds it.
- B. Cell type 4 must lack receptor R, because a cell that binds a ligand but does not respond has no receptor.
- **C. Binding to R is necessary but not sufficient: the response depends on the transduction proteins and effectors each cell type has, so the same ligand and receptor can give different responses or none.**
- D. Cell types 1 and 2 must have different receptors for L, because they respond to L in different ways.

Check: type 3 shows no binding and no response; types 1, 2 and 4 all bind L to the same receptor R yet respond in three different ways, so the response must depend on the cell's downstream components.
Rationales: A: cell types 1 and 2 bind the same ligand yet respond differently, so the ligand's structure does not fix the response. B: the antibody test shows type 4 has R and it binds L strongly, so the receptor is present and a downstream defect is the more likely cause. D: the stem states that the same receptor protein R is present on both types, so different responses cannot be explained by different receptors.

**N-4.1-B-v1.** A researcher engineers three cell lines to make the same receptor protein, R, at the same level. Each line is exposed to the same concentration of ligand L.

Cell line | Origin | Response to L
1 | liver | breaks down glycogen
2 | neuron | opens an ion channel
3 | skin fibroblast | begins dividing

Which statement best explains why one ligand and one receptor produce three responses?

- **A. Each cell line has its own set of signal transduction proteins and effectors, so the same receptor activation leads to different responses.**
- B. L enters each cell and decides which genes to turn on, because the response depends on the structure of the ligand.
- C. The receptor R changes its structure in each cell line so that it binds a different part of L.
- D. The cells differ in the number of L molecules that reached them, since only the amount of ligand determines the type of response.

Check: ligand, receptor and ligand concentration are identical in the three lines, so the differences must lie downstream, in each cell's own transduction components and effectors.
Rationales: B: L acts by binding the receptor and the stem gives no evidence that it enters the cells, and the same ligand structure gives three different responses. C: the same receptor protein is made in all three lines, so no new binding site or structure is needed to explain the differences. D: the same concentration of L was added to every line, so the amount of ligand does not differ among them.

**N-4.1-B-v2.** Cell line K binds labeled ligand L as strongly as a normal cell line does, yet shows no response. K lacks protein P, a cytoplasmic kinase that normally acts after receptor activation. When the gene for P is added to K, the cells respond to L. Which conclusion is best supported?

- A. P is the receptor for L, because the response appeared only after P was supplied.
- B. K could not bind L, so the original lack of response was caused by missing ligand binding.
- C. P makes the ligand L, so adding P supplied the signal that K had been missing.
- **D. The receptor in K works, but the cells lacked a downstream component, P, needed to relay the signal to a response.**

Check: normal binding shows the receptor and reception step are intact; supplying P restores the response, so the missing piece was a downstream transduction component.
Rationales: A: K already bound L normally before P was added, so a receptor was present, and P is a cytoplasmic kinase and not a surface receptor. B: the stem says K binds L as strongly as a normal line does, so binding was not the problem. C: L was supplied by the researcher in every test, and P is a signaling kinase that does not make ligands.

**N-4.1-B-v3.** Three cell lines were tested with ligand L. In a second test, a membrane-permeable drug that directly activates the downstream kinase of the pathway was added in place of L.

Cell line | Labeled L bound | Response to L | Response to the drug
1 | high | high | high
2 | none | none | high
3 | high | none | none

Which statement is best supported?

- A. Cell line 3 lacks the receptor, since it does not respond to L.
- **B. Cell line 2 lacks a functional receptor for L but has an intact downstream pathway, while cell line 3 has a receptor but a defect downstream.**
- C. Cell line 2 has a defect in transduction, since it gives no response to L.
- D. Cell line 1 shows that receptors are never needed, since the drug alone can produce its response.

C: line 2 responds to the drug, so its transduction machinery works, and the missing step is binding of L. D: the drug is an artificial bypass, and in a normal cell the pathway is turned on by ligand binding to the receptor, as the response of line 1 to L shows.

## Seed N-4.2-A (replaces 027 and 047 families) — Topic 4.2 Introduction to Signal Transduction
**Skill:** use an experiment to decide where a ligand is received and whether the response needs new gene expression.

Ligand M is a 30-kDa protein. When M is added to the medium around liver cells, glycogen breakdown begins within 20 seconds. When M is microinjected directly into the cytoplasm of a liver cell, there is no response. The protein-synthesis inhibitor cycloheximide does not change the response to M added outside the cell, but a PKA inhibitor abolishes the response. Which conclusion is best supported?

- **A. M binds a receptor on the cell surface, and the response is relayed by proteins already in the cytoplasm, such as PKA, without new protein synthesis.**
- B. M enters the cell, binds an intracellular receptor, and acts as a transcription factor that produces new proteins within seconds.
- C. M binds a membrane receptor, but the response needs newly synthesized proteins, and cycloheximide did not block them because it acts too slowly.
- D. M enters the cell through a channel and directly activates PKA, which then breaks down glycogen.

Check: a response to outside M but not to injected M places the receptor at the plasma membrane; cycloheximide has no effect, so no new proteins are needed; the PKA inhibitor blocks the response, so PKA relays the signal.
Rationales: B: injecting M into the cytoplasm gave no response, which an intracellular receptor would not predict, and the response needs no new protein synthesis. C: the response arises within 20 seconds and cycloheximide had no effect on it, so no newly made proteins are needed. D: if M acted directly on PKA inside the cell, injecting it would have caused a response, and it did not.

**N-4.2-A-v1.** Hormone S is a small nonpolar molecule. After S is added to cultured cells, the amount of a certain enzyme rises only after 3 hours. The transcription inhibitor actinomycin D prevents the rise. When S is attached to large beads that cannot cross the plasma membrane, there is no response. Which conclusion is best supported?

- A. S binds a membrane receptor and activates a kinase cascade that acts within seconds.
- B. S acts at the cell surface, because the beads show that S can reach the cell from outside.
- C. S must first be broken down in the cell to an active form, and transcription is not involved in the delay.
- **D. S crosses the membrane and binds an intracellular receptor, and the complex changes gene expression, which explains the delay.**

Check: beads that cannot enter give no response, so S must get inside; actinomycin D blocks the response, so it needs transcription; new mRNA and protein take hours.
Rationales: A: a membrane receptor cascade would respond within seconds to the beads, and the response here is delayed and needs transcription. B: the beads that stay outside the cell give no response, so a surface receptor is not enough for S. C: actinomycin D blocks the response, so transcription is involved, and the stem gives no evidence of a conversion step.

**N-4.2-A-v2.** Neurotransmitter N is applied to a muscle fiber and depolarizes the membrane within 5 milliseconds. Replacing the Na⁺ outside the cell with a large ion that cannot pass through channels abolishes the depolarization. Kinase inhibitors and protein-synthesis inhibitors do not change the response. Which conclusion is best supported?

- A. N crosses the membrane and binds an intracellular receptor that changes gene expression.
- B. N binds a receptor tyrosine kinase that phosphorylates transcription factors within milliseconds.
- **C. N binds a receptor that is itself an ion channel, so binding opens the channel and ions enter, with no second messenger or new gene expression needed.**
- D. N causes IP₃ to release stored Ca²⁺ from the ER, and the Ca²⁺ then depolarizes the membrane.

Check: a 5 ms response that needs extracellular Na⁺ but not kinases or new proteins fits a ligand-gated ion channel opened directly by N.
Rationales: A: gene expression takes much longer than 5 ms and protein-synthesis inhibitors had no effect. B: kinase inhibitors did not change the response, so a kinase-based pathway is not needed. D: stored Ca²⁺ release would not depend on the Na⁺ outside the cell, and replacing the Na⁺ abolished the response.

**N-4.2-A-v3.** In a cell line, ligand L normally raises cAMP and turns on gene G. When drug Z is present, L no longer raises cAMP or turns on gene G, and binding of labeled L drops by 95%. In drug-treated cells, a membrane-permeable cAMP analog turns on gene G strongly. Which stage of signaling does Z most likely block?

- A. Transduction, because Z prevents the cell from making cAMP after L binds.
- **B. Reception, because Z stops L from binding its receptor, while transduction and the response are intact.**
- C. The response, because Z stops gene G from being turned on even when cAMP is high.
- D. All three stages, because Z prevented L from turning on gene G.

Check: Z cuts labeled L binding by 95% (reception), and cAMP analog restores gene G expression (transduction after cAMP and the response work).
Rationales: A: Z reduces the binding of L, so reception fails first, and the cell can still respond to cAMP, so making cAMP is not shown to be the blocked step. C: gene G turns on strongly when cAMP analog is added, so the response step is intact. D: the cAMP analog restores the response, so the later stages work and only reception is shown to be blocked.

## Seed N-4.2-B (new) — Topic 4.2 Introduction to Signal Transduction
**Skill:** calculate how amplification through a cascade scales the final output when one step changes.

In a cell, each hormone-bound receptor activates 20 G proteins. Each active G protein activates one adenylyl cyclase, and each activated adenylyl cyclase makes 50 cAMP molecules before it is inactivated. A mutation lowers the number of G proteins activated per hormone-bound receptor from 20 to 5; all other steps are unchanged. If 300 receptors bind hormone, which statement describes the cAMP produced?

- **A. 75,000 cAMP molecules, one fourth of the normal amount, because a smaller yield at the first step is carried through the later multiplying steps.**
- B. 150,000 cAMP molecules, half of the normal amount, because the mutation removes half of the G proteins.
- C. 1,500 cAMP molecules, because only the 5 G proteins per receptor make cAMP.
- D. 300,000 cAMP molecules, unchanged, because the later steps of the cascade make up for the loss.

Check: normal output is 300 × 20 × 50 = 300,000 cAMP; with the mutation it is 300 × 5 × 50 = 75,000, which is one fourth.
Rationales: B: going from 20 to 5 G proteins is a fourfold decrease and not a twofold decrease, so the output falls to one fourth. C: 300 × 5 = 1,500 counts activated G proteins and leaves out the 50 cAMP made by each adenylyl cyclase. D: the later steps only multiply what they receive and cannot restore a lower number of activated G proteins.

**N-4.2-B-v1.** In a kinase cascade, each active kinase K1 activates 10 molecules of K2, each active K2 activates 10 molecules of K3, and each active K3 phosphorylates 10 molecules of a transcription factor. A drug changes K2 so that each active K2 activates only 5 K3 molecules. If 40 K1 molecules are active, which statement describes the number of phosphorylated transcription factor molecules?

- A. 40,000, unchanged, because the later step adds 10 for each K3 and so corrects the loss.
- B. 10,000, one fourth of normal, because the change at the second step affects two steps.
- **C. 20,000, half of the normal 40,000, because halving the output of one step halves the final output.**
- D. 200, because only the 5 K3 per K2 matter for the final amount.

Check: normal is 40 × 10 × 10 × 10 = 40,000; with the drug it is 40 × 10 × 5 × 10 = 20,000, which is half.
Rationales: A: each step only multiplies the number it receives, so fewer K3 means fewer phosphorylated factors. B: only one step changed and it was halved from 10 to 5, so the output halves and does not fall to a quarter. D: 40 × 5 = 200 ignores the multiplication by the other steps of the cascade.

**N-4.2-B-v2.** Two hypothetical pathways are compared. In pathway X, each of 100 ligand-bound receptors directly activates one transcription factor. In pathway Y, each of 100 ligand-bound receptors activates 5 kinase molecules, and each kinase activates 5 transcription factors. Which statement is correct?

- A. Pathway Y activates 500 transcription factors, because each receptor activates 5 molecules.
- B. Pathway Y activates 1,000 transcription factors, because the 5 and 5 are added at each receptor.
- C. Pathway X and Y activate the same number, because the number of receptors sets the number of active transcription factors.
- **D. Pathway Y activates 2,500 transcription factors, which is 25 times as many as pathway X, because each step multiplies the signal.**

Check: pathway X gives 100; pathway Y gives 100 × 5 × 5 = 2,500, which is 25 times as large.
Rationales: A: 100 × 5 = 500 counts only the first step and leaves out the 5 transcription factors activated by each kinase. B: the amplification at the steps is multiplied (5 × 5 = 25 per receptor), not added (5 + 5 = 10). C: the number of receptors is the same, but extra steps with several targets per enzyme give a larger output in Y.

**N-4.2-B-v3.** A researcher measures how much of a cell's maximum response is produced when different fractions of its receptors are occupied by ligand.

Receptors occupied | Response (percent of maximum)
5% | 40%
20% | 85%
100% | 100%

Which interpretation is best supported by the data?

- A. The response is directly proportional to the fraction of receptors occupied, so 5% occupancy gives 5% of the maximum.
- **B. Occupying a small fraction of the receptors gives a large share of the maximum response, as expected when downstream steps amplify the signal.**
- C. Occupancy above 20% harms the cell, so the response levels off.
- D. The ligand must enter the cell to produce a response, so the limit on the response is how fast the ligand is transported.

Check: 5% occupancy yields 40% of the maximum, a much larger fraction than the occupancy, which is the signature of amplification after reception.
Rationales: A: the data show 5% occupancy giving 40% of the maximum, which is not proportional. C: the response levels off because the downstream machinery nears its maximum, and the data give no sign of damage to the cell. D: the experiment concerns receptor occupancy and the stem gives no evidence that the ligand enters the cell or that transport limits the response.

## Seed N-4.3-A (replaces 028 family) — Topic 4.3 Signal Transduction Pathways
**Skill:** predict the effect of a constitutively active component and of blocking different steps.

In a signaling pathway, growth factor binding activates receptor R, which activates Ras, then Raf, then MEK, then ERK. Active ERK turns on genes for cell proliferation. A cell line has a mutation that keeps Raf active even without input from Ras. The cells are grown with no growth factor. Which result is predicted?

- A. The proliferation genes stay off until growth factor is added, because Raf cannot act without Ras.
- B. The proliferation genes are on, and an antibody that blocks receptor R turns them off.
- **C. The proliferation genes are on, and a MEK inhibitor turns them off, but an antibody that blocks receptor R does not.**
- D. The proliferation genes are on, and a MEK inhibitor does not turn them off, because active Raf can bypass MEK.

Check: Raf is downstream of the receptor and Ras, so its constant activity keeps MEK and ERK active without ligand; blocking the receptor acts upstream of the mutation and has no effect, but blocking MEK downstream of it cuts the signal.
Rationales: A: the mutation makes Raf active without Ras, so the downstream steps proceed without growth factor. B: the receptor acts upstream of Raf, and blocking it cannot switch off a Raf that is active without any input. D: in this pathway Raf activates ERK only through MEK, so blocking MEK stops the signal.

## Seed N-4.3-B (new) — Topic 4.3 Signal Transduction Pathways
**Skill:** work out the order of proteins in a pathway from rescue experiments.

Three proteins, X, Y and Z, act in a single linear pathway between a receptor and a gene response. Researchers made cell lines that each lack one protein, then tested whether adding a constitutively active form of another protein (CA) turns on the response when no ligand is present.

Cell line | Response to ligand | CA-X, no ligand | CA-Y, no ligand | CA-Z, no ligand
Lacks X | none | not tested | response | no response
Lacks Y | none | no response | not tested | no response
Lacks Z | none | response | response | not tested

Which order of action, from the receptor to the response, fits the data?

- A. X, then Y, then Z.
- B. Z, then Y, then X.
- C. Y, then X, then Z.
- **D. Z, then X, then Y.**

Check: an active protein can rescue only a line that lacks something upstream of it. Lines lacking Z are rescued by both CA-X and CA-Y, so Z is first. A line lacking X is rescued by CA-Y but not CA-Z, so Y is after X. The line lacking Y is rescued by neither, so Y is last.
Rationales: A: if X came first, CA-Z and CA-Y would rescue the line lacking X, and CA-Z did not rescue it. B: if Y came before X, CA-X would rescue the line lacking Y, and it did not. C: if Y came first, CA-X would rescue a line lacking Y, and the table shows it did not.

**N-4.3-B-v1.** A pathway in a cell has three kinases, KA, KB and KC. After the ligand is added, the researchers add a drug that blocks one kinase at a time and measure which kinases become phosphorylated (active).

Drug target | KA phosphorylated | KB phosphorylated | KC phosphorylated
KA blocked | no | no | no
KB blocked | yes | no | no
KC blocked | yes | yes | no

Which order of action fits the data?

- A. KC, then KB, then KA.
- **B. KA, then KB, then KC.**
- C. KB, then KA, then KC.
- D. The three kinases act in parallel, each one switched on directly by the receptor.

Check: blocking a kinase prevents activation of the kinases downstream of it but not those upstream; blocking KA stops all three, blocking KB stops KB and KC, and blocking KC stops only KC.
Rationales: A: if KC acted first, blocking KC would stop KB and KA, but KA and KB were phosphorylated with KC blocked. C: if KB acted before KA, blocking KB would stop KA, and KA was still phosphorylated. D: in a parallel pathway, blocking one kinase would leave the others active, and blocking KA stopped all three.

**N-4.3-B-v2.** Four proteins, F, G, H and J, act in a linear pathway from a receptor to a gene response. Each cell line lacks one protein. In each line, no ligand is given and a constitutively active form of one of the other proteins is added.

Cell line | Active forms that turn on the response
Lacks F | H, J, G
Lacks G | none
Lacks H | J, G
Lacks J | G

Which order of action, from the receptor to the response, fits the data?

- A. J, then H, then F, then G.
- B. G, then J, then H, then F.
- **C. F, then H, then J, then G.**
- D. F, then J, then H, then G.

## Seed N-5.1-A (new) — Topic 5.1 Meiosis

**Skill:** infer the meiotic division and chromosome structures from microscopy and predict the immediate consequence



A diploid cell has completed DNA replication. In a micrograph, homologous chromosomes are paired at the cell equator, and the two sister chromatids of each chromosome remain attached. If the spindle pulls each homolog toward a different pole, which event and immediate outcome are shown?



- **A. Metaphase I followed by separation of homologs; each resulting cell has one replicated chromosome from each pair.**

- B. Metaphase II followed by separation of sister chromatids; each resulting cell has both homologs.

- C. Mitosis followed by separation of homologs; each resulting cell has half the original chromosome number.

- D. Anaphase II followed by separation of homologs; each resulting cell has two chromatids from every pair.



Check: Paired homologs at one plate identify meiosis I. Anaphase I moves homologs, not sisters, yielding two haploid cells whose chromosomes remain replicated.

Rationales: B: Meiosis II begins with no homologous pairs in one cell; sisters separate there, not paired homologs. C: Mitosis separates sister chromatids and maintains chromosome number per daughter cell. D: Anaphase II separates sisters after homologs were divided in meiosis I; it does not retain two chromatids per resulting chromosome.



**N-5.1-A-v1.** A researcher marks the centromere of each sister chromatid in one diploid cell. After the first cytokinesis, two cells contain one homolog from each original pair, but each chromosome still consists of two attached chromatids. Which next step directly creates four cells with one chromatid per chromosome?



- **A. Separation of sister chromatids during anaphase II, followed by the second cytokinesis.**

- B. Pairing of homologs during prophase I, followed by the first cytokinesis.

- C. Separation of homologs during anaphase I, followed by the second cytokinesis.

- D. Another round of DNA replication, followed by chromosome separation in mitosis.



Check: The observed two cells are after meiosis I. Meiosis II separates attached sisters and the second cytokinesis yields four haploid cells.

Rationales: B: Homolog pairing precedes the first division and cannot convert the observed two cells into four cells with single chromatids. C: Homologs have already separated; repeating that step would not split attached sister chromatids. D: Meiosis II has no intervening DNA replication; replication would add rather than remove attached chromatids.



**N-5.1-A-v2.** In a cell from a sexually reproducing diploid organism, spindle fibers pull the two sister chromatids of each chromosome toward opposite poles. The cell already contains only one member of each homologous pair. Which interpretation fits both observations?



- **A. The cell is in anaphase II; homologs separated in the previous division and sister chromatids are now separating.**

- B. The cell is in anaphase I; homologous pairs are being pulled apart for the first time.

- C. The cell is in metaphase I; homologous pairs are aligned but no chromosomes are moving.

- D. The cell is in mitosis; its two daughters will necessarily be diploid like the original parent cell.



Check: One homolog per pair indicates the reductional division has occurred; active sister separation is anaphase II.

Rationales: B: Anaphase I moves homologs while sister centromeres remain attached. C: Metaphase I aligns paired homologs in a diploid cell and does not pull sisters apart. D: Mitosis also separates sisters, but this cell is already haploid after the first meiotic division, so diploid daughters do not follow.



**N-5.1-A-v3.** Four nuclei form after a diploid germ cell divides twice, with DNA replicated only before the first division. In the first division, sister chromatids stay together while homologs separate. Which description of the second division is consistent with the final four nuclei?



- **A. Each of the two haploid cells separates sister chromatids, producing four haploid nuclei with unduplicated chromosomes.**

- B. The original diploid cell separates homologs again, producing four diploid nuclei with replicated chromosomes.

- C. Each haploid cell replicates DNA again and then separates homologous pairs, producing four diploid nuclei.

- D. The two haploid cells fuse before separating sister chromatids, producing four genetically identical diploid nuclei.



Check: Meiosis I reduces homolog number in each cell; without another replication, meiosis II separates sisters and produces four haploid nuclei.

Rationales: B: Homolog separation has already occurred and the original cell no longer exists after the first division. C: No replication occurs between meiotic divisions, and each haploid cell has only one homolog per pair. D: Fusion would restore diploidy and is not part of the second meiotic division.

## Seed N-5.1-B (new) — Topic 5.1 Meiosis

**Skill:** track chromosome and chromatid counts across the two meiotic divisions



A germ cell has 2n=6 chromosomes. DNA replicates once before meiosis, and no chromosomes are lost. Immediately after meiosis I, what does each of the two cells contain, and what will each contain after meiosis II? Count a chromosome by its centromere.



- **A. After meiosis I: 3 chromosomes and 6 chromatids; after meiosis II: 3 chromosomes and 3 chromatids.**

- B. After meiosis I: 6 chromosomes and 6 chromatids; after meiosis II: 3 chromosomes and 3 chromatids.

- C. After meiosis I: 3 chromosomes and 3 chromatids; after meiosis II: 3 chromosomes and 6 chromatids.

- D. After meiosis I: 6 chromosomes and 12 chromatids; after meiosis II: 6 chromosomes and 6 chromatids.



Check: Homolog separation halves chromosome number from 6 to 3 but keeps sisters joined (6 chromatids). Sister separation yields 3 unduplicated chromosomes per gamete.

Rationales: B: This counts both post-I cells together rather than each one; one post-I cell has only three centromeres. C: Meiosis I does not separate sisters, and meiosis II does not replicate DNA. D: This retains the diploid chromosome number in every product despite homolog separation.



**N-5.1-B-v1.** A diploid organism has 2n=8. A student counts chromosomes by centromeres and measures DNA as chromatids. Compare one cell at metaphase I with one cell at metaphase II, assuming normal separation and no additional replication. Which pair of counts is expected?



- **A. Metaphase I: 8 chromosomes, 16 chromatids; metaphase II: 4 chromosomes, 8 chromatids.**

- B. Metaphase I: 16 chromosomes, 16 chromatids; metaphase II: 8 chromosomes, 8 chromatids.

- C. Metaphase I: 8 chromosomes, 8 chromatids; metaphase II: 4 chromosomes, 4 chromatids.

- D. Metaphase I: 8 chromosomes, 16 chromatids; metaphase II: 8 chromosomes, 16 chromatids.



Check: Replication doubles chromatids, not centromeres. Meiosis I halves centromeres per cell but leaves each chromosome duplicated.

Rationales: B: This treats each chromatid as a chromosome at metaphase I and fails to account for attached sisters at metaphase II. C: Both metaphase stages occur before sister separation, so each chromosome has two chromatids. D: The first division separates homologs; a metaphase-II cell is haploid, with four chromosomes rather than eight.



**N-5.1-B-v2.** A diploid cell with 2n=10 enters meiosis after DNA replication. A technician counts five chromosomes per cell immediately after the first division. If the second division proceeds normally, which count and cell number should the technician record at the end?



- **A. Four cells, each with five chromosomes containing one chromatid each.**

- B. Four cells, each with ten chromosomes containing one chromatid each.

- C. Two cells, each with five chromosomes containing two chromatids each.

- D. Four cells, each with five chromosomes containing two chromatids each.



Check: Meiosis I produces two haploid cells with five duplicated chromosomes each; meiosis II separates sisters, making four cells with five unduplicated chromosomes each.

Rationales: B: Meiosis II does not restore the original diploid chromosome count per cell. C: This describes the state after meiosis I, not the end of meiosis II. D: Sister chromatids separate during meiosis II, so the final chromosomes each have one chromatid.



**N-5.1-B-v3.** A diploid plant has 2n=12. A premeiotic cell has completed S phase. Its four final meiotic products are analyzed separately. Assuming normal meiosis, which change in chromosome and chromatid count occurs from the premeiotic cell to any one final product?



- **A. From 12 chromosomes/24 chromatids to 6 chromosomes/6 chromatids.**

- B. From 24 chromosomes/24 chromatids to 12 chromosomes/12 chromatids.

- C. From 12 chromosomes/12 chromatids to 6 chromosomes/12 chromatids.

- D. From 12 chromosomes/24 chromatids to 12 chromosomes/12 chromatids.



Check: S phase duplicates chromatids without changing 12 centromeres; meiosis I halves homolog count, and meiosis II separates sisters, leaving six single-chromatid chromosomes.

Rationales: B: A replicated chromatid is not a separate chromosome while sisters share a centromere; each gamete is haploid. C: The premeiotic cell has already undergone S phase, and final gametes have separated sister chromatids. D: Final meiotic products have half the original chromosome count, not the diploid count.

## Seed N-5.1-C (new) — Topic 5.1 Meiosis

**Skill:** distinguish mitosis and meiosis from chromosome behavior and product observations



A diploid cell line is filmed through division. In trial X, paired homologs align at one plate; after two divisions there are four haploid cells. In trial Y, individual duplicated chromosomes align; after one division there are two diploid cells. Which inference best explains the difference?



- **A. X is meiosis because homologs segregate before sisters; Y is mitosis because sisters segregate in one division without reducing ploidy.**

- B. X is mitosis because homolog pairing yields identical daughters; Y is meiosis because one division makes diploid gametes.

- C. Both are meiosis because every division uses a spindle; the number of products depends only on cell size.

- D. Both are mitosis because chromosomes align at a plate in each trial.



Check: Paired homolog alignment and reduction plus a second division diagnose meiosis; one sister-separation division preserving ploidy diagnoses mitosis.

Rationales: B: Homolog pairing is characteristic of meiosis I, and mitosis does not make four haploid products. C: Both use spindles, but spindle use alone does not explain reductional versus equational outcomes. D: Alignment occurs in both processes, but homolog pairing and product ploidy distinguish them.



**N-5.1-C-v1.** A researcher disables a protein needed to keep sister centromeres joined through the first division of meiosis. In a normal control, homologs separate first and sisters separate in the second division. What observation would most directly reveal that the mutant failed to follow the normal meiotic sequence?



- **A. Sister chromatids separate during the first division instead of remaining attached while homologs move apart.**

- B. Homologous chromosomes pair during prophase I before they separate.

- C. Two haploid cells are present after the first cytokinesis, each with duplicated chromosomes.

- D. Four haploid cells are present after two divisions, each with unduplicated chromosomes.



Check: The protein's specified role is sister cohesion in meiosis I; losing it permits premature sister separation, violating the observed order.

Rationales: B: Homolog pairing is an expected normal event and does not reveal loss of sister cohesion. C: Attached sisters after meiosis I indicate the protein's role was maintained, not lost. D: Four unduplicated haploid products describe the normal endpoint and do not directly show premature sister separation.



**N-5.1-C-v2.** A diploid parent cell makes two daughter cells in culture. Each daughter has the same number of chromosome sets as the parent and received one chromatid from each replicated chromosome. A separate germ cell makes four cells, each with one chromosome set. Which event explains the set-number difference?



- **A. The germ cell separated homologous chromosomes in its first division, whereas the cultured cell separated sister chromatids in a single mitotic division.**

- B. The cultured cell separated homologs in mitosis, whereas the germ cell separated sisters in meiosis I.

- C. The germ cell replicated DNA between its two divisions, restoring two chromosome sets in each product.

- D. The cultured cell paired homologs and crossed them over, while the germ cell skipped chromosome separation.



Check: Homolog segregation reduces chromosome sets in meiosis I; sister segregation alone in mitosis preserves the original set number.

Rationales: B: Mitosis segregates sisters; meiosis I segregates homologs, the reverse of this choice. C: No S phase occurs between meiosis I and II, and replication would not explain haploid products. D: Meiotic products require chromosome separation; homolog pairing is associated with meiosis I, not ordinary mitosis.



**N-5.1-C-v3.** A microscopy study compares two divisions of the same germ-cell lineage. After division 1, each cell has one homolog from every pair but its sister chromatids are still joined. After division 2, sister chromatids are separate. Why can division 2 occur without another S phase and still produce gametes?



- **A. Each chromosome already has two chromatids after division 1, so division 2 partitions those sisters into haploid cells with unduplicated chromosomes.**

- B. Division 1 has already separated sister chromatids, so division 2 only replicates the homologs.

- C. Each post-division-1 cell is diploid, so division 2 must remove an entire homolog pair from each cell.

- D. Gametes are made by fusing the two cells from division 1 before division 2 begins.



Check: Meiosis I leaves sisters attached in haploid cells. Meiosis II distributes existing chromatids; no new DNA synthesis is required.

Rationales: B: The observation explicitly says sisters remain joined after division 1; replication is not division 2's role. C: One homolog per pair means the post-I cells are haploid, and meiosis II separates sisters rather than homolog pairs. D: Cell fusion would restore diploidy and is not part of meiosis II.

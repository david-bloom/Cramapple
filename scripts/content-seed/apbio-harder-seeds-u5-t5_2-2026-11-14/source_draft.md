## Seed N-5.2-A (new) — Topic 5.2 Meiosis and Genetic Diversity

**Skill:** combine independent assortment probability with its cellular mechanism



An organism has 3 homologous chromosome pairs. Ignore crossing over and assume each pair orients independently at meiosis I. What is the chance that one gamete gets a specified maternal-or-paternal homolog from every pair, and which meiotic event creates these possible combinations?



- **A. 1/8; random orientation of homologous pairs at metaphase I creates 8 equiprobable whole-chromosome assortments.**

- B. 1/3; sister chromatids separate at anaphase II to create 3 equiprobable assortments.

- C. 1/16; random fertilization doubles the possible homolog assortments within one gamete.

- D. 1/8; crossing over in prophase I is required to create the 8 whole-chromosome assortments.



Check: Each of the 3 pairs has two independent maternal/paternal choices, giving 2^3=8 combinations and probability (1/2)^3=1/8 for one specified combination. The source of these whole-chromosome combinations is independent assortment at meiosis I; crossing over is excluded.

Rationales: B: Meiosis II separates sisters; the number of whole-homolog configurations is exponential in homolog-pair count, not equal to that count. C: Fertilization combines two gametes but does not change the probability for a specified assortment in one gamete. D: The numerical probability is right, but the mechanism is wrong: independent orientation creates these combinations without crossing over.

## Seed N-5.2-B (new) — Topic 5.2 Meiosis and Genetic Diversity

**Skill:** infer recombination products and meiotic stage from linked markers



Two linked markers are on one homolog as AB and on the other as ab. After meiosis, a researcher identifies gametes with AB, ab, Ab, and aB. Which gametes require an exchange between nonsister chromatids in the interval between the markers, and when can that exchange occur?



- **A. Ab and aB; crossing over between nonsister chromatids during prophase I can create these new combinations.**

- B. AB and ab; crossing over during prophase I is required to preserve the original combinations.

- C. Ab and aB; separation of sister chromatids during meiosis II creates the new combinations without exchange.

- D. All four gamete classes; random fertilization creates their allele combinations inside a single gamete.



Check: The original chromosome arrangements are AB and ab. A crossover between the loci on nonsister chromatids during prophase I can exchange the second marker, producing Ab and aB. Independent separation alone preserves the linked arrangements.

Rationales: B: Those are the parental marker combinations, which can be inherited without recombination. C: Sister-chromatid separation sorts existing chromatids; it does not exchange linked alleles between homologs. D: Fertilization combines already formed gametes and cannot create new allele arrangements inside one gamete.

## Seed N-5.2-C (new) — Topic 5.2 Meiosis and Genetic Diversity

**Skill:** diagnose division-specific nondisjunction from all four gamete counts



A diploid cell normally makes gametes with n=4 chromosomes. Four gametes from one meiosis have chromosome counts 5, 5, 3, and 3. Assuming a single nondisjunction event, which division failed, and how does the pattern support the inference?



- **A. Nondisjunction of one homologous pair in meiosis I; all four gametes are abnormal, two n+1 and two n−1.**

- B. Nondisjunction of sister chromatids in meiosis II; two gametes should still have 4 chromosomes.

- C. Crossing over in prophase I; exchanging chromosome segments creates the count differences.

- D. Normal independent assortment; random orientation of homologs creates the count differences.



Check: Failure of homologs to separate in meiosis I puts both homologs in one daughter cell and neither in the other. Normal meiosis II then yields two 5-chromosome and two 3-chromosome gametes, matching all four observations.

Rationales: B: Meiosis II nondisjunction leaves two normal gametes, which are absent here. C: Crossing over changes allele combinations, not the number of chromosomes in each gamete. D: Normal meiosis gives four n-chromosome gametes, not the observed two high and two low counts.



**N-5.2-C-v1.** A diploid cell normally makes gametes with n=6 chromosomes. Four gametes from one meiosis have chromosome counts 6, 6, 7, and 5. Assuming a single nondisjunction event, which division failed, and how does the pattern support the inference?



- **A. Nondisjunction of sister chromatids in meiosis II; two gametes are normal and the others are n+1 and n−1.**

- B. Nondisjunction of homologous chromosomes in meiosis I; all four gametes should have abnormal counts.

- C. Crossing over in prophase I; exchanging chromosome segments creates the count differences.

- D. Normal independent assortment; random orientation of homologs creates the count differences.



Check: Normal homolog separation in meiosis I creates two n-chromosome cells. If sister chromatids fail to separate in one meiosis II cell, its gametes are 7 and 5; the other cell produces two normal 6-chromosome gametes.

Rationales: B: Meiosis I nondisjunction produces no normal gametes from that meiosis, but two are observed. C: Crossing over changes genetic combinations without this n±1 chromosome-count pattern. D: Normal meiosis gives four n-chromosome gametes, not one high and one low count.

**N-5.2-C-v3.** A diploid organism normally makes gametes with n=10 chromosomes. One abnormal meiosis produced gametes with 10, 10, 11, and 9 chromosomes. If each gamete separately fuses with a normal 10-chromosome gamete, which zygote chromosome counts result, and which division most likely had one nondisjunction event?



- **A. 20, 20, 21, and 19; a meiosis II sister-chromatid nondisjunction leaves two normal gametes and one n+1/n−1 pair.**

- B. 20, 20, 21, and 19; a meiosis I homolog nondisjunction leaves two normal gametes.

- C. 10, 10, 11, and 9; fertilization does not change chromosome number.

- D. 21, 21, 19, and 19; meiosis II nondisjunction makes all four gametes abnormal.



Check: Normal fertilization adds 10 chromosomes to each gamete, yielding 20, 20, 21, and 19. The two normal gametes and one high/low pair identify nondisjunction of sister chromatids in one meiosis II cell, not failure of homologs in meiosis I.

Rationales: B: The zygote counts are right, but meiosis I nondisjunction would make all four gametes abnormal. C: The normal partner contributes 10 more chromosomes to each zygote. D: The two normal gametes rule out a single meiosis I nondisjunction and yield two normal 20-chromosome zygotes.

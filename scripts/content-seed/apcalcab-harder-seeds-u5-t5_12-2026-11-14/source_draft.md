## Seed N-5.12-A (new) — Topic 5.12 Exploring Behaviors of Implicit Relations

**Skill:** find both horizontal critical points of an implicit relation



For the implicit relation x²+xy+y²=3, find all points where its tangent is horizontal. Which reasoning confirms that the derivative is defined at those points?



- **A. (1,−2) and (−1,2); y'=−(2x+y)/(x+2y), whose denominator is nonzero at both points.**

- B. (2,−1) and (−2,1); these make the numerator of y' zero.

- C. (1,2) and (−1,−2); these make the numerator of y' zero.

- D. (1,−2) only; the negative-x point cannot have a horizontal tangent.



Check: Implicit differentiation gives y'=−(2x+y)/(x+2y). Horizontal tangents require y=−2x and x+2y≠0. Substitution gives 3x²=3, so x=±1 and y=∓2. The denominator equals −3x, nonzero at both points.

Rationales: B: These are the vertical-tangent points where the denominator is zero, not horizontal tangents. C: The stated points do not satisfy the relation or the zero-numerator condition. D: The equation has both signs of x, and the derivative denominator is nonzero at each resulting point.

## Seed N-5.12-B (new) — Topic 5.12 Exploring Behaviors of Implicit Relations

**Skill:** differentiate an implicit circle twice and infer concavity



On the upper branch of x²+y²=25, evaluate d²y/dx² at (3,4) and use its sign to describe the branch's local concavity there.



- **A. d²y/dx²=−25/64; the upper branch is concave down.**

- B. d²y/dx²=+25/64; the upper branch is concave up.

- C. d²y/dx²=−1/4; the upper branch is concave down.

- D. d²y/dx²=−3/4; the upper branch is concave down.



Check: From 2x+2yy'=0, y'=−x/y. Differentiating again gives 2+2(y')²+2yy''=0, hence y''=−(x²+y²)/y³=−25/64 at (3,4). Because y>0, the branch is concave down.

Rationales: B: The second derivative is negative on the upper half of the circle; this sign is reversed. C: Differentiating y' requires the (y')² term, which produces the circle-radius numerator rather than just 1. D: This is the first derivative y', not the second derivative y''.

## Seed N-5.12-C (new) — Topic 5.12 Exploring Behaviors of Implicit Relations

**Skill:** use an implicit second derivative to classify a branch extremum



Near (0,1), the ellipse x²+4y²=4 defines an upper-branch function y(x). Find y'' at x=0 and use it to classify the point (0,1) on that branch.



- **A. y''(0)=−1/4; (0,1) is a local maximum of the upper branch.**

- B. y''(0)=+1/4; (0,1) is a local minimum of the upper branch.

- C. y''(0)=−1/1; (0,1) is a local maximum of the upper branch.

- D. y''(0)=0; (0,1) is neither a maximum nor a minimum.



Check: Differentiate to get 2x+8yy'=0, so y'(0)=0. Differentiate again: 2+8[(y')²+yy'']=0. At (0,1), y''=−2/(8·1)=−1/4<0, making this stationary point a local maximum on the upper branch.

Rationales: B: The sign is negative, so the upper branch has a local maximum, not a minimum. C: This misses the factor 4 multiplying y² in the original relation. D: A horizontal tangent can still be a maximum; the implicit second derivative is nonzero and negative.

## Seed N-5.4-A (new) — Topic 5.4 Using the First Derivative Test

**Skill:** classify two stationary points from a factored derivative



A differentiable function f has f'(x)=1(x-(-2))(x-(1)) for all real x, with -2<1. Which classification of its local extrema follows from the first derivative test?



- **A. f has a local maximum at x=-2 and a local minimum at x=1.**

- B. f has a local minimum at x=-2 and a local maximum at x=1.

- C. Both x=-2 and x=1 are local maxima because f' is zero at both.

- D. Neither zero of f' can be a local extremum because f is differentiable there.



Check: For x<-2, between -2 and 1, and x>1, the derivative signs are positive, negative, positive. Thus x=-2 is a positive-to-negative switch and x=1 a negative-to-positive switch.

Rationales: B: This reverses the derivative sign changes: positive-to-negative makes a maximum and negative-to-positive a minimum. C: Zeros are candidates; the sign changes around each root determine opposite extremum types. D: Differentiability does not prevent local extrema; these derivative sign reversals establish them.

## Seed N-5.4-B (new) — Topic 5.4 Using the First Derivative Test

**Skill:** interpret a derivative sign representation with a stationary non-extremum



A graph of a continuous derivative f' crosses or touches the x-axis only at x=-2, 0, and 3. On the four successive intervals its signs are +,-,-,+. Which conclusion about f follows?



- **A. f has a local maximum at x=-2, a local minimum at x=3, and neither type of extremum at x=0.**

- B. f has a local minimum at x=-2, a local maximum at x=3, and neither at x=0.

- C. All three zeros are local extrema because f' is zero at each.

- D. No local extrema can be inferred because the numerical values of f at the zeros are unknown.



Check: Read derivative signs in x order: +,-,-,+. A + to - switch marks a maximum, - to + marks a minimum, and no sign switch at x=0 marks neither.

Rationales: B: The sign-switch directions are reversed: positive-to-negative is a local maximum, not a minimum. C: A derivative zero without a sign reversal need not be a local extremum; inspect all adjacent intervals. D: The first derivative test uses signs of f', so explicit function values are unnecessary to classify local extrema.

## Seed N-5.4-C (new) — Topic 5.4 Using the First Derivative Test

**Skill:** distinguish even- and odd-multiplicity derivative zeros



A differentiable function g satisfies g'(x)=1(x-(1))²(x-(-2)) for all real x. Which statement about the stationary points x=1 and x=-2 is correct?



- **A. x=-2 is a local minimum; x=1 is stationary but not a local extremum because g' keeps its sign there.**

- B. x=1 is a local minimum; x=-2 is not an extremum because the factor at -2 has odd multiplicity.

- C. x=-2 is a local maximum, and x=1 is also a local extremum because both factors vanish.

- D. Neither point can be a local extremum because the derivative is a polynomial and therefore continuous.



Check: The squared factor at x=1 does not flip sign. Away from that point the sign of g' is the sign of 1(x-(-2)), which changes negative to positive at x=-2.

Rationales: B: The squared factor gives no derivative sign reversal at its root, while the simple factor does reverse sign at the other root. C: The simple factor changes sign, but its extremum type is reversed here; the repeated root stays stationary without an extremum. D: A continuous polynomial derivative can change sign at a simple root and create a local extremum.

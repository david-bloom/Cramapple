## Seed N-5.6-A (new) — Topic 5.6 Determining Concavity of Functions over Their Domains

**Skill:** test second-derivative signs at a repeated and a simple zero



A twice-differentiable function f on all real numbers has f''(x)=1(x−(-2))²(x−(1)), where -2<1. Which conclusion about concavity and inflection is justified?



- **A. The graph changes from concave down to concave up at x=1; x=-2 is not an inflection point.**

- B. Both x=-2 and x=1 are inflection points because f'' is zero at each.

- C. Neither x=-2 nor x=1 can be an inflection point because f'' is zero there.

- D. The graph changes from concave up to concave down at x=1, and x=-2 is not an inflection point.



Check: The squared factor is nonnegative and does not change sign at -2. The simple factor changes sign at 1; f'' has sign negative then positive across that point.

Rationales: B: A zero of the second derivative alone is insufficient; its sign does not change at the squared-factor root. C: A sign change of f'' at the simple-factor root establishes an inflection point even though f'' is zero there. D: This reverses the sign of f'' on either side of the simple-factor root.

## Seed N-5.6-B (new) — Topic 5.6 Determining Concavity of Functions over Their Domains

**Skill:** infer concavity of f from monotonicity of f' without confusing sign and trend



A function f is differentiable on all real numbers and f' is continuous. A graph of f' shows that f' is decreasing on (−∞,-2), increasing on (-2,1), and decreasing on (1,∞). No numerical values of f' are supplied. Which conclusion follows?



- **A. The graph of f is concave down, then up, then down on those three intervals; its concavity changes at x=-2 and x=1.**

- B. The graph of f is increasing precisely on the intervals where f' is increasing, and decreasing where f' is decreasing.

- C. The graph of f has local extrema at both x=-2 and x=1, because f' changes its increasing/decreasing trend there.

- D. No concavity conclusion is possible without the numerical values of f' at the interval boundaries.



Check: Concavity of f follows the trend of f': increasing means up, decreasing means down. The trend sequence is decreasing, increasing, decreasing; only the stated changes in concavity are inflections.

Rationales: B: Whether f increases depends on the sign of f', not whether f' itself rises or falls. C: A local extremum of f requires a sign switch of f', which is not given by its increasing/decreasing trend alone. D: The direction of change of f' suffices to infer concavity without knowing its numerical values.

## Seed N-5.6-C (new) — Topic 5.6 Determining Concavity of Functions over Their Domains

**Skill:** separate true inflection points from a domain break in a rational second derivative



A function g is twice differentiable for all real x except x=0 and satisfies g''(x)=1(x−(-2))(x−(3))/(x−(0))² on that domain, with -2<0<3. The function has no value at x=0. Which statement correctly describes concavity and inflection points?



- **A. g is concave up for x<-2 and x>3, concave down on (-2,0) and (0,3), and has inflection points at x=-2 and x=3 only.**

- B. g has inflection points at x=-2, x=0, and x=3 because the displayed formula is zero or undefined at all three.

- C. g is concave down outside (-2,3) and concave up between the roots because the squared denominator is negative there.

- D. g has no inflection points because its domain is disconnected at x=0.



Check: The squared denominator is positive wherever defined. The numerator has sign positive, negative, positive across -2,3; the gap at 0 is not a point on the graph.

Rationales: B: An inflection point must lie on the graph; g is undefined at the domain gap. C: A nonzero square is positive, so it does not reverse the numerator's sign pattern. D: The domain gap does not prevent concavity from changing at the included points x=a and x=b.

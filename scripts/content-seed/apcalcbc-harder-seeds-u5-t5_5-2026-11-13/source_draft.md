## Seed N-5.5-A (new) — Topic 5.5 Using the Candidates Test

**Skill:** compare all endpoint and critical-point values, including ties



For f(x)=x³−3x on the closed interval [-3,2], a student finds f'(x)=3x²−3. Which statement gives every absolute maximum and every absolute minimum on the interval?



- **A. The absolute maximum is 2 at x=-1, x=2, and the absolute minimum is -18 at x=-3.**

- B. The absolute maximum is -18 at x=-3, and the absolute minimum is 2 at x=-1, x=2.

- C. Only x=−1 and x=1 need be compared because they are the zeros of f'.

- D. The left endpoint is an absolute maximum and the right endpoint is an absolute minimum.



Check: The candidates are the endpoints -3,2 and derivative zeros −1,1. Their function values in that order are -18,2,2,−2. Compare all values, retaining ties.

Rationales: B: This reverses the meaning of largest and smallest candidate value. C: The candidates test includes endpoints as well as interior critical points; an endpoint determines or ties an extremum here. D: The left endpoint has a smaller value than the right endpoint here, so their proposed extremum types are reversed.

## Seed N-5.5-B (new) — Topic 5.5 Using the Candidates Test

**Skill:** include a nondifferentiable interior point among candidates and compare values



The continuous function g(x)=|x−(1)|+(-0.5)x is restricted to [0,4]. Its derivative is undefined at x=1 and nonzero at all other interior points. Which conclusion follows from the candidates test?



- **A. The absolute minimum is -0.5 at x=1; the absolute maximum is 1 at x=0, x=4.**

- B. The derivative is undefined at the corner, so the absolute minimum must occur at one of the two endpoints.

- C. The corner x=1 is the absolute maximum because a sharp point always exceeds nearby values.

- D. The candidates test cannot be used because g is not differentiable at every interior point.



Check: The only candidates are endpoint x=0, corner x=1, and endpoint x=4; their values are 1,-0.5,1. The function is continuous on the closed interval.

Rationales: B: An interior corner is a critical candidate when the derivative does not exist; it has the smallest evaluated value. C: A corner can be a minimum; here both one-sided slopes point upward away from it. D: Continuity on a closed interval permits the test; nondifferentiability adds the corner to the candidate list.

## Seed N-5.5-C (new) — Topic 5.5 Using the Candidates Test

**Skill:** interpret a finite candidate table with an undefined derivative and ties



A function h is continuous on [0,5]. Its only interior critical points are x=1, where h'=0, and x=3, where h' is undefined; h' is nonzero and defined everywhere else inside the interval. The measured values are h(0)=2, h(1)=6, h(3)=-1, and h(5)=4. Which conclusion is justified?



- **A. The absolute maximum is 6 at x=1, and the absolute minimum is -1 at x=3.**

- B. Both absolute extrema must occur at x=1, the only place where h'=0.

- C. The value at x=3 must be discarded because h' is undefined there.

- D. The absolute extrema cannot be found without the formula for h between the listed points.



Check: All candidates are 0,1,3,5; by hypothesis there are no other interior critical points. Their values are (2, 6, -1, 4). The largest is 6 and the smallest is -1; include ties.

Rationales: B: A derivative zero is only one candidate, and one point cannot simultaneously have the listed largest and smallest values. C: An undefined derivative at an interior point makes it a candidate; the corner has a smallest value here. D: Continuity on the closed interval plus the exhaustive candidate list makes comparison of these four values sufficient.

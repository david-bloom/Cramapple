## Seed N-5.8-A (new) — Topic 5.8 Sketching Graphs of Functions and Their Derivatives

**Skill:** combine f' zeros and f'' sign for a whole-function sketch



A function f has f'(x)=1(x−(-3))(x−(1)) for all real x, with -3<1. Which set of features belongs in an accurate sketch of f?



- **A. A local maximum at x=-3, a local minimum at x=1, and an inflection at x=-1 where f is decreasing.**

- B. A local minimum at x=-3, a local maximum at x=1, and an inflection at x=-1.

- C. Inflection points at both x=-3 and x=1 because f' is zero there, with no local extrema.

- D. No inflection point, because the given first derivative is a quadratic polynomial.



Check: f' has signs positive, negative, positive across -3,1. Also f''=1(2x−(-3)−(1)) changes sign at -1; f'(-1) has sign negative.

Rationales: B: The first derivative sign changes at both zeros are reversed. C: Zeros of f' classify extrema of f; f'' changes sign at the midpoint, not at both derivative zeros. D: The derivative of the quadratic f' is linear and changes sign at its zero, producing an inflection of f.

## Seed N-5.8-B (new) — Topic 5.8 Sketching Graphs of Functions and Their Derivatives

**Skill:** translate sampled slope and concavity data into local sketch features



A table from a twice-differentiable function records f'(-3)=5, f''(-3)=-6, f'(1)=-3, and f''(1)=2. A student is drawing local pieces of the graph near x=-3 and x=1. Which annotations match the table?



- **A. Near x=-3, f is rising and concave down; near x=1, f is falling and concave up.**

- B. f is rising at both points because f'' is nonzero at both.

- C. Near x=-3, f is falling, and near x=1, f is rising; the f' values give the opposite slope signs.

- D. Both sampled points must be local extrema of f because f'' is nonzero at each.



Check: f' signs at -3,1 are 5,-3, setting rise/fall; f'' signs are -6,2, setting concavity. Neither f' value is zero.

Rationales: B: A nonzero second derivative controls concavity, not whether f is rising; one listed f' is negative. C: This reverses the sign interpretation of f' at both sampled points. D: A nonzero f' rules out a differentiable local extremum at these sampled points.



**N-5.8-B-v1.** A twice-differentiable function has f'(-1)=-3, f''(-1)=-2, f'(3)=5, and f''(3)=6. For a sufficiently small neighborhood of each point, compare the graph with its tangent line there. Which pair of sketch notes is justified?



- **A. At x=-1, the tangent slopes down and lies locally above the graph; at x=3, it slopes up and lies locally below the graph.**

- B. The tangent slopes have the same signs as stated, but lie below the graph near x=-1 and above the graph near x=3.

- C. The tangent lies on the stated side of the graph at each point, but its slope signs are the reverse of the listed f' values.

- D. The tangent must cross the graph at both points because both second derivatives are nonzero.



Check: f' gives tangent slopes -3,5. Negative f'' puts a graph below its tangent locally; positive f'' puts a graph above it. The tangent sides are above,below.

Rationales: B: This reverses the local tangent-versus-graph relation implied by each nonzero second derivative. C: The sign of f' directly gives each tangent slope; these slope directions are reversed. D: Nonzero second derivative makes the tangent stay on one local side of the graph rather than cross it immediately.

## Seed N-5.8-C (new) — Topic 5.8 Sketching Graphs of Functions and Their Derivatives

**Skill:** distinguish stationary inflections from extrema in a derivative-based sketch



A differentiable function g has g'(x)=1(x−(-2))²(x−(4)) for all real x. A proposed sketch marks x=-2 as a horizontal tangent and x=4 as another horizontal tangent. Which additional classification is correct?



- **A. x=-2 is a stationary inflection, x=2 is another inflection, and x=4 is a local minimum.**

- B. x=-2 and x=4 are both local minimum points because g' vanishes at both.

- C. x=-2 is a stationary inflection, but x=4 is also an inflection because g' vanishes there.

- D. x=2 is a local minimum of g because it is a zero of g''.



Check: The squared factor makes g' keep its sign at -2; the simple factor changes sign at 4, giving a local minimum. Differentiation gives g''=1(x−(-2))(3x−(6)), whose signs change at -2 and 2.

Rationales: B: A repeated derivative zero does not change g' sign, while the simple zero does. C: The simple zero of g' is an extremum of g, not an inflection; g'' does not vanish there. D: A zero of g'' can signal an inflection of g, not a local extremum unless g' also changes sign.

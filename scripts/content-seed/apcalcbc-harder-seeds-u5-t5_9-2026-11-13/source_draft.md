## Seed N-5.9-A (new) — Topic 5.9 Connecting a Function, Its First Derivative, and Its Second Derivative

**Skill:** connect a local extremum of f' to an inflection of f, including stationary status



Let f(x)=1(x−(-2))³+(0)(x−(-2)) for all real x. Which description correctly links the graphs of f, f', and f'' at x=-2?



- **A. f has a stationary inflection there; f' has a local minimum there, and f'' changes sign through zero.**

- B. f has a local minimum there because f' has a local minimum there.

- C. f' has an inflection there because f'' is zero there, while f has no concavity change.

- D. f'' has a local extremum there because its value is zero, so f cannot have an inflection.



Check: f'=3(1)(x−(-2))²+(0), f''=6(1)(x−(-2)). The linear f'' changes sign at -2; f' is quadratic with local minimum there; f'(-2)=0, so f is stationary.

Rationales: B: An extremum of f' concerns the slope of f; f has an inflection here, and may have a nonzero slope. C: A zero of f'' with sign change makes an inflection of f, while f' has a local extremum, not an inflection, here. D: The linear f'' crosses zero rather than turning around; that crossing marks f's concavity change.

## Seed N-5.9-B (new) — Topic 5.9 Connecting a Function, Its First Derivative, and Its Second Derivative

**Skill:** use f'' signs and f' endpoint values to locate an extremum and inflections



A twice-differentiable function f has f''(x)=2(x−(-1))(x−(2)) for all real x, where -1<2. Also f'(-1)=5 and f'(2)=-4. Which relationship among the three graphs is forced on (-1,2) and at its boundary points?



- **A. f' decreases from 5 to -4 and has exactly one zero inside (-1,2), giving f a local maximum there; f has inflections at x=-1 and x=2.**

- B. f has local extrema at both x=-1 and x=2 because f'' is zero at those points.

- C. f' cannot cross zero between -1 and 2 because f'' has no zero strictly between them.

- D. The interior zero of f' is an inflection of f, while x=a and x=b cannot be inflections because f'' vanishes there.



Check: Between -1 and 2, f'' has sign negative, so f' is strictly monotone. Its endpoint values 5,-4 straddle zero, forcing exactly one zero, where f has a local maximum. f'' changes sign at each boundary root.

Rationales: B: f''=0 can make an inflection of f, but the given f' values at those points are nonzero, so neither is a local extremum of f. C: A monotone continuous f' with endpoint values on opposite sides of zero must cross zero exactly once. D: At the interior zero, f'' is nonzero, giving an extremum of f; f'' changes sign at the two boundary roots, giving inflections there.

## Seed N-5.9-C (new) — Topic 5.9 Connecting a Function, Its First Derivative, and Its Second Derivative

**Skill:** match trig graph landmarks across f, f', and f''



On the interval from x=0 to x=0+2π, let g(x)=1sin(x−(0)). Which interior features of the graphs of g, g', and g'' correspond?



- **A. g has a local maximum at x=0+π/2 and a local minimum at x=0+3π/2; g has an inflection at x=0+π, where g' has a local extremum.**

- B. g has inflections at x=0+π/2 and x=0+3π/2, because g' is zero there; g has an extremum at x=0+π.

- C. g has local extrema at all three listed interior points because either g' or g'' vanishes at each.

- D. g cannot have an inflection at the middle point because g'' is zero there.



Check: g'=1cos(x−(0)), g''=−1sin(x−(0)). The cosine zeros make g's two extrema; g'' changes sign at the middle point, where the cosine graph has an extremum.

Rationales: B: Zeros of g' locate extrema of g, whereas a sign change of g'' locates an inflection of g. C: At the middle point g' is nonzero, so g has no extremum there; g''=0 alone does not mark an extremum of g. D: g'' changes sign at the middle point, which is the defining concavity change for an inflection.

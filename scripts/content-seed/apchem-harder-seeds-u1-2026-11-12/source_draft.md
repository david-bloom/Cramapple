## Seed N-1.1-A (new) — Topic 1.1 Moles and Molar Mass
**Skill:** compare the number of atoms of one element across samples given as a mass, an amount in moles, and a particle count.

Four samples are described. Molar masses (g/mol): H₂O 18.02, CO₂ 44.01.

Sample 1: 20.0 g of H₂O.
Sample 2: 0.100 mol of Ca₃(PO₄)₂.
Sample 3: 30.0 g of CO₂.
Sample 4: 2.40 × 10²³ molecules of SO₃.

Which sample contains the greatest number of oxygen atoms? (Avogadro's number = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹)

- A. Sample 1, because it has the largest amount of substance (1.11 mol) of the four.
- **B. Sample 3, which contains about 1.36 mol of oxygen atoms.**
- C. Sample 2, because each formula unit of Ca₃(PO₄)₂ contains eight oxygen atoms, more than any other formula.
- D. Sample 4, because a count of 2.40 × 10²³ is a number of particles and therefore the largest quantity listed.

Check: Convert each to mol of O atoms: 1.11, 0.800, 2 × (30.0/44.01) = 1.36, and 3 × (2.40 × 10²³/6.022 × 10²³) = 1.20; the largest is sample 3.
Rationales: A: Sample 1 has the most moles of molecules, but each H₂O has only one O atom, so it holds 1.11 mol of O atoms, fewer than sample 3 at 1.36 mol. C: Sample 2 has the most O atoms per formula unit, but only 0.100 mol of formula units, giving 0.800 mol of O atoms, the fewest of the four. D: Sample 4 is 0.399 mol of SO₃ with three O atoms each, which is 1.20 mol of O atoms; this is large but still less than sample 3 at 1.36 mol.

**N-1.1-A-v1.** Four samples are described. Molar masses (g/mol): NH₃ 17.03.

Sample 1: 15.0 g of NH₃.
Sample 2: 0.800 mol of C₂H₅OH.
Sample 3: 0.300 mol of C₆H₁₂O₆.
Sample 4: 1.20 × 10²⁴ molecules of H₂SO₄.

Which sample contains the greatest number of hydrogen atoms? (Avogadro's number = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹)

- **A. Sample 2, which contains 4.80 mol of hydrogen atoms.**
- B. Sample 1, because ammonia has the lowest molar mass, so 15.0 g contains the most molecules.
- C. Sample 3, because glucose has twelve hydrogen atoms per molecule, more than any other formula.
- D. Sample 4, because it is stated as a count of particles and 1.20 × 10²⁴ is the largest number.

Check: Mol of H atoms: 15.0/17.03 × 3 = 2.64; 0.800 × 6 = 4.80; 0.300 × 12 = 3.60; (1.20 × 10²⁴/6.022 × 10²³) × 2 = 3.99; the largest is sample 2.
Rationales: B: The 15.0 g of NH₃ is 0.881 mol of molecules, but at three H atoms each it gives only 2.64 mol of H atoms, less than sample 2. C: Glucose has the most H per molecule, but 0.300 mol gives only 3.60 mol of H atoms, which is less than the 4.80 mol in sample 2. D: The 1.20 × 10²⁴ molecules are 1.99 mol of H₂SO₄ with two H each, so 3.99 mol of H atoms; this is below sample 2 at 4.80 mol.

**N-1.1-A-v2.** Four samples are described. Molar masses (g/mol): CCl₄ 153.82, AlCl₃ 133.34.

Sample 1: 40.0 g of CCl₄.
Sample 2: 0.500 mol of MgCl₂.
Sample 3: 60.0 g of AlCl₃.
Sample 4: 2.00 × 10²³ molecules of PCl₃.

Which sample contains the greatest number of chlorine atoms? (Avogadro's number = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹)

- A. Sample 1, because CCl₄ has four chlorine atoms per molecule, the most of any formula listed.
- B. Sample 2, because it is the only sample stated directly in moles, so no conversion is needed to compare it.
- C. Sample 4, because each PCl₃ molecule contains three chlorine atoms and phosphorus is heavier than aluminum.
- **D. Sample 3, which contains about 1.35 mol of chlorine atoms.**

Check: Mol of Cl atoms: 40.0/153.82 × 4 = 1.04; 0.500 × 2 = 1.00; 60.0/133.34 × 3 = 1.35; (2.00 × 10²³/6.022 × 10²³) × 3 = 0.996; the largest is sample 3.
Rationales: A: Four Cl per molecule is the most, but 40.0 g is only 0.260 mol of CCl₄, giving 1.04 mol of Cl atoms, less than the 1.35 mol in sample 3. B: Sample 2 gives 0.500 mol × 2 = 1.00 mol of Cl atoms; the way a quantity is stated does not make it larger, and sample 3 holds 1.35 mol. C: Sample 4 is 0.332 mol of PCl₃, giving only 0.996 mol of Cl atoms; the mass of the other element does not affect the count of Cl atoms.

**N-1.1-A-v3.** Four samples are described. Molar masses (g/mol): NH₃ 17.03, NaNO₃ 84.99.

Sample 1: 25.0 g of NH₃.
Sample 2: 0.600 mol of NH₄NO₃.
Sample 3: 50.0 g of NaNO₃.
Sample 4: 4.00 × 10²³ molecules of N₂O₄.

Which sample contains the greatest number of nitrogen atoms? (Avogadro's number = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹)

- A. Sample 2, because NH₄NO₃ contains two nitrogen atoms per formula unit and its amount is given directly in moles.
- B. Sample 3, because the 50.0 g is the largest mass of any compound that contains nitrogen.
- **C. Sample 1, which contains about 1.47 mol of nitrogen atoms.**
- D. Sample 4, because N₂O₄ has two nitrogen atoms per molecule and a particle count of the order 10²³.

Check: Mol of N atoms: 25.0/17.03 = 1.47; 0.600 × 2 = 1.20; 50.0/84.99 = 0.588; (4.00 × 10²³/6.022 × 10²³) × 2 = 1.33; the largest is sample 1.
Rationales: A: The 0.600 mol of NH₄NO₃ gives 1.20 mol of N atoms; two N per formula unit does not offset the smaller number of formula units compared with sample 1. B: Sample 3 is 0.588 mol of NaNO₃ with one N atom each, so 0.588 mol of N atoms; a large mass does not mean many atoms when the molar mass is large. D: Sample 4 is 0.664 mol of N₂O₄, giving 1.33 mol of N atoms, which is close to but smaller than the 1.47 mol in sample 1.

## Seed N-1.1-B (new) — Topic 1.1 Moles and Molar Mass
**Skill:** identify an unknown element from the number of atoms of another element in a mass of its compound, using the formula to link atoms, moles and molar mass.

A 5.00 g sample of an unknown alkali-metal carbonate, M₂CO₃, contains 6.54 × 10²² oxygen atoms. Use the molar masses (g/mol) C 12.01, O 16.00, S 32.06, N 14.01, P 30.97, Li 6.94, Na 22.99, K 39.10, Rb 85.47, Mg 24.31, Ca 40.08, Sr 87.62, Ba 137.33. Avogadro's number = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹. What is M?

- A. Li, because lithium is the lightest alkali metal and the sample is small.
- B. Na, because Na₂CO₃ is the most common carbonate and its formula contains three oxygen atoms.
- **C. K, because 6.54 × 10²² O atoms is 0.109 mol of O, so 0.0362 mol of M₂CO₃, a molar mass of 138 g/mol.**
- D. Rb, because the sample must contain more than one mole of metal atoms to account for 10²² oxygen atoms.

Check: 6.54 × 10²²/6.022 × 10²³ = 0.1086 mol O; ÷ 3 = 0.03620 mol M₂CO₃; 5.00/0.03620 = 138 g/mol; 138.2 − 60.01 = 78.2 for 2 M, so M = 39.1, potassium.
Rationales: A: Li₂CO₃ (73.89 g/mol) would give 5.00/73.89 × 3 × 6.022 × 10²³ = 1.22 × 10²³ O atoms, nearly twice the value observed. B: Na₂CO₃ (105.99 g/mol) would give 8.52 × 10²² O atoms in 5.00 g, which is more than the 6.54 × 10²² observed. D: Rb₂CO₃ (230.94 g/mol) would give only 3.91 × 10²² O atoms in 5.00 g; also 10²² atoms is far less than one mole, so no such requirement exists.

**N-1.1-B-v1.** A 6.00 g sample of an unknown alkali-metal sulfate, M₂SO₄, contains 1.02 × 10²³ oxygen atoms. Use the molar masses (g/mol) C 12.01, O 16.00, S 32.06, N 14.01, P 30.97, Li 6.94, Na 22.99, K 39.10, Rb 85.47, Mg 24.31, Ca 40.08, Sr 87.62, Ba 137.33. Avogadro's number = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹. What is M?

- A. Li, because a lighter metal gives a smaller molar mass and therefore more oxygen atoms per gram.
- **B. Na, because the O count gives 0.0423 mol of M₂SO₄, a molar mass of 142 g/mol, so M = 22.99.**
- C. K, because K₂SO₄ is the sulfate most often used as a standard and contains four oxygen atoms per formula unit.
- D. Rb, because a count of 10²³ atoms of oxygen is too large to come from a light metal.

Check: 1.02 × 10²³/6.022 × 10²³ = 0.1694 mol O; ÷ 4 = 0.04235 mol; 6.00/0.04235 = 141.7 g/mol; minus 96.06 for SO₄ gives 45.6 for 2 M, so M = 22.8, sodium.
Rationales: A: Li₂SO₄ (109.94 g/mol) would give 1.31 × 10²³ O atoms in 6.00 g, which is more than the 1.02 × 10²³ observed. C: K₂SO₄ (174.26 g/mol) would give 8.29 × 10²² O atoms in 6.00 g, which is less than the 1.02 × 10²³ observed. D: Rb₂SO₄ (267.00 g/mol) would give only 5.41 × 10²² O atoms in 6.00 g; the number of oxygen atoms depends on the molar mass of the whole formula unit.

**N-1.1-B-v2.** A 8.00 g sample of an unknown alkaline-earth nitrate, M(NO₃)₂, contains 4.55 × 10²² nitrogen atoms. Use the molar masses (g/mol) C 12.01, O 16.00, S 32.06, N 14.01, P 30.97, Li 6.94, Na 22.99, K 39.10, Rb 85.47, Mg 24.31, Ca 40.08, Sr 87.62, Ba 137.33. Avogadro's number = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹. What is M?

- **A. Sr, because 4.55 × 10²² N atoms is 0.0378 mol of M(NO₃)₂, a molar mass of 212 g/mol.**
- B. Mg, because Mg(NO₃)₂ has the smallest formula mass, so the sample holds the fewest atoms.
- C. Ca, because Ca(NO₃)₂ is the nitrate whose count of N atoms is closest to 5 × 10²².
- D. Ba, because dividing the N atoms by two formula units gives a metal mass near 137 g/mol.

Check: 4.55 × 10²²/6.022 × 10²³ = 0.07556 mol N; ÷ 2 = 0.03778 mol; 8.00/0.03778 = 211.8 g/mol; minus 124.02 for 2 NO₃ gives 87.7, strontium.
Rationales: B: Mg(NO₃)₂ (148.31 g/mol) would give 8.00/148.31 × 2 × 6.022 × 10²³ = 6.50 × 10²² N atoms, more than observed; a smaller formula mass gives more atoms, not fewer. C: Ca(NO₃)₂ (164.10 g/mol) would give 5.87 × 10²² N atoms in 8.00 g, which does not match the 4.55 × 10²² observed. D: Ba(NO₃)₂ (261.35 g/mol) would give 3.69 × 10²² N atoms in 8.00 g; the factor of two relates N atoms to formula units, not to the molar mass of the metal.

**N-1.1-B-v3.** A 4.00 g sample of an unknown alkali-metal phosphate, M₃PO₄, contains 4.54 × 10²² oxygen atoms. Use the molar masses (g/mol) C 12.01, O 16.00, S 32.06, N 14.01, P 30.97, Li 6.94, Na 22.99, K 39.10, Rb 85.47, Mg 24.31, Ca 40.08, Sr 87.62, Ba 137.33. Avogadro's number = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹. What is M?

- A. Li, because Li₃PO₄ has the smallest formula mass and four oxygen atoms per formula unit.
- B. Na, because Na₃PO₄ is a common phosphate and three metal atoms balance one phosphate ion.
- C. Rb, because the unknown metal must be heavy to account for the small number of oxygen atoms.
- **D. K, because the O count gives 0.0188 mol of M₃PO₄, a molar mass of 212 g/mol, so M = 39.1.**

Check: 4.54 × 10²²/6.022 × 10²³ = 0.07539 mol O; ÷ 4 = 0.01885 mol; 4.00/0.01885 = 212.2 g/mol; minus 94.97 for PO₄ gives 117.2 for 3 M, so M = 39.1, potassium.
Rationales: A: Li₃PO₄ (115.79 g/mol) would give 4.00/115.79 × 4 × 6.022 × 10²³ = 8.32 × 10²² O atoms, almost twice the observed number. B: Na₃PO₄ (163.94 g/mol) would give 5.88 × 10²² O atoms in 4.00 g, which is more than the 4.54 × 10²² observed. C: Rb₃PO₄ (351.38 g/mol) would give only 2.74 × 10²² O atoms in 4.00 g, fewer than observed.

## Seed N-1.2-A (new) — Topic 1.2 Mass Spectra of Elements
**Skill:** interpret the molecular-ion peaks of a diatomic element, where each element's isotope abundances must be recovered from a 3-peak pattern, and then compute the average atomic mass.

The mass spectrum of a sample of chlorine gas, Cl₂, shows three peaks from singly charged Cl₂⁺ ions: m/z 70 (relative height 9), m/z 72 (relative height 6) and m/z 74 (relative height 1). The only isotopes are ³⁵Cl and ³⁷Cl. Treat isotope masses as equal to their mass numbers. What is the average atomic mass of chlorine in this sample?

- A. 71.0, because it is the weighted average of the three peak positions.
- B. 36.0, because the average of the two isotope masses, 35 and 37, is 36.
- C. 35.0, because the tallest peak at m/z 70 corresponds to the most abundant isotope.
- **D. 35.5, because the 70 : 72 : 74 heights of 9 : 6 : 1 correspond to 75% ³⁵Cl and 25% ³⁷Cl.**

Check: p² = 9/16 so the fraction of ³⁵Cl is 0.75 and of ³⁷Cl is 0.25 (check: 2pq = 6/16); average = 35(0.75) + 37(0.25) = 35.5.
Rationales: A: The weighted average of the peaks is 71.0, but that is the average mass of a Cl₂ molecule; the atomic mass is half of it. B: A simple average of 35 and 37 ignores abundance; the 9 : 6 : 1 pattern shows ³⁵Cl is far more abundant, so the average must be closer to 35. C: The tallest peak is ³⁵Cl₂, but 25% of the atoms are ³⁷Cl, which raises the average above 35.

**N-1.2-A-v1.** The mass spectrum of a sample of bromine, Br₂, shows peaks from singly charged Br₂⁺ ions at m/z 158, 160 and 162 with relative heights 1 : 2 : 1. The only isotopes are ⁷⁹Br and ⁸¹Br. Treat isotope masses as equal to their mass numbers. What is the average atomic mass of bromine in this sample?

- A. 160.0, because it is the position of the tallest peak in the spectrum.
- B. 79.0, because ⁷⁹Br is the lighter isotope and gives the first peak.
- **C. 80.0, because the 1 : 2 : 1 pattern shows the two isotopes are equally abundant.**
- D. 81.0, because the heaviest peak at m/z 162 corresponds to the heaviest isotope, which sets the atomic mass.

Check: p² = 1/4 gives p = 0.50 for each isotope (2pq = 1/2 matches the middle peak); average = 79(0.50) + 81(0.50) = 80.0.
Rationales: A: The tallest peak at 160 is the mass of a Br₂ molecule; the atomic mass is half, so 80.0. B: The first peak at 158 is ⁷⁹Br₂, but equal numbers of ⁸¹Br atoms are present, so the average is not the lighter isotope mass. D: The heaviest peak shows that ⁸¹Br exists, but it is only one of the two equally abundant isotopes, so the average is not 81.

**N-1.2-A-v2.** A sample of chlorine gas enriched in the heavier isotope gives a mass spectrum with peaks from Cl₂⁺ ions at m/z 70, 72 and 74 with relative heights 1 : 4 : 4. The only isotopes are ³⁵Cl and ³⁷Cl. Treat isotope masses as equal to their mass numbers. What is the average atomic mass of chlorine in this sample?

- A. 72.7, because it is the average mass of the Cl₂ molecules.
- **B. 36.3, because ³⁷Cl accounts for two thirds of the atoms.**
- C. 36.0, because the two tallest peaks are equal, so the isotopes are equally abundant.
- D. 35.0, because the lightest peak at m/z 70 is the base for the average.

Check: q² = 4/9 for ³⁷Cl₂ gives ³⁷Cl = 2/3 of the atoms (2pq = 4/9 checks); average = 35(1/3) + 37(2/3) = 36.3.
Rationales: A: The weighted average of the peak positions is 72.7, but this is the mass of a Cl₂ molecule; the atomic mass is half of it. C: The tallest two peaks have equal height, but the peak at 72 counts one atom of each isotope while 74 counts two ³⁷Cl, so ³⁷Cl is more abundant than ³⁵Cl. D: The 70 peak is only ³⁵Cl₂, the smallest peak here; the isotope with mass 37 is the most abundant, so the average is above 36.

**N-1.2-A-v3.** A sample of bromine gas that is depleted in ⁸¹Br gives a mass spectrum with peaks from Br₂⁺ ions at m/z 158, 160 and 162 with relative heights 16 : 8 : 1. The only isotopes are ⁷⁹Br and ⁸¹Br. Treat isotope masses as equal to their mass numbers. What is the average atomic mass of bromine in this sample?

- **A. 79.4, because ⁷⁹Br makes up 80% of the atoms and ⁸¹Br makes up 20%.**
- B. 158.8, because it is the weighted average mass of a Br₂ molecule.
- C. 80.0, because the mean of the isotope masses 79 and 81 is the atomic mass.
- D. 79.0, because the tallest peak at m/z 158 shows that ⁷⁹Br is the only significant isotope.

Check: p² = 16/25 so the fraction of ⁷⁹Br is 0.80 (2pq = 8/25 checks); average = 79(0.80) + 81(0.20) = 79.4.
Rationales: B: The weighted average of the peaks is 158.8, but this is for a Br₂ molecule; the atomic mass is half, 79.4. C: The unweighted mean of 79 and 81 ignores that ⁷⁹Br is four times as abundant as ⁸¹Br in this sample. D: The tallest peak shows ⁷⁹Br dominates, but the 20% ⁸¹Br raises the average above 79.

## Seed N-1.2-B (new) — Topic 1.2 Mass Spectra of Elements
**Skill:** use both the m/z values and the isotope pattern (peak spacing and relative heights) in a mass spectrum to decide how many chlorine or bromine atoms a molecule contains.

A pure compound that contains only carbon, hydrogen and one type of halogen gives a mass spectrum with molecular-ion peaks at m/z 112 (relative height 100) and m/z 114 (relative height 32), and no other peaks in that region. Use the isotope masses ¹²C = 12, ¹H = 1, ³⁵Cl = 35, ³⁷Cl = 37, ⁷⁹Br = 79 and ⁸¹Br = 81, and ignore ¹³C and ²H. Natural chlorine is about 75% ³⁵Cl and 25% ³⁷Cl; natural bromine is about 51% ⁷⁹Br and 49% ⁸¹Br. Which formula is consistent with the spectrum?

- **A. C₆H₅Cl, because its mass is 112 and the 3 : 1 pair separated by two mass units is the signature of one chlorine atom.**
- B. C₈H₁₆, because a hydrocarbon with 8 carbons and 16 hydrogens also has a mass of 112.
- C. C₃H₆Cl₂, because it also has a mass of 112 and contains chlorine.
- D. C₆H₅Br, because any halogen produces a peak two mass units above the molecular ion.

Check: C₆H₅³⁵Cl = 72 + 5 + 35 = 112 and C₆H₅³⁷Cl = 114; the 100 : 32 ratio is about 75 : 25 for ³⁵Cl : ³⁷Cl, so one Cl atom.
Rationales: B: C₈H₁₆ has mass 112 but contains no halogen, so it would give a single peak with no peak at 114 of height 32. C: C₃H₆Cl₂ has mass 112 but two Cl atoms would give peaks at 112, 114 and 116 in a 9 : 6 : 1 ratio, so the 114 peak would be about 2/3 of the base peak, not 32%. D: C₆H₅Br would have its molecular ions at m/z 156 and 158 in a 1 : 1 ratio, not at 112 and 114 in a 3 : 1 ratio.

**N-1.2-B-v1.** A pure compound that contains only carbon, hydrogen and one type of halogen gives a mass spectrum with molecular-ion peaks at m/z 94 and m/z 96 of nearly equal height (100 and 98), and no other peaks in that region. Use the isotope masses ¹²C = 12, ¹H = 1, ³⁵Cl = 35, ³⁷Cl = 37, ⁷⁹Br = 79 and ⁸¹Br = 81, and ignore ¹³C and ²H. Natural chlorine is about 75% ³⁵Cl and 25% ³⁷Cl; natural bromine is about 51% ⁷⁹Br and 49% ⁸¹Br. Which formula is consistent with the spectrum?

- A. C₇H₁₀, because a hydrocarbon of this formula also has a mass of 94.
- B. CH₃Cl, because chlorine also gives a pair of peaks separated by two mass units.
- C. CH₂Br₂, because two peaks of equal height imply two bromine atoms.
- **D. CH₃Br, because its mass is 94 and a 1 : 1 pair two mass units apart is the signature of one bromine atom.**

Check: CH₃⁷⁹Br = 12 + 3 + 79 = 94 and CH₃⁸¹Br = 96; two equal peaks two units apart indicate one Br atom, since ⁷⁹Br and ⁸¹Br are almost equally abundant.
Rationales: A: C₇H₁₀ has mass 94 but contains no halogen, so it would give one peak at 94 and no peak at 96 of comparable height. B: CH₃Cl would give peaks at m/z 50 and 52 in a 3 : 1 ratio; neither the masses nor the ratio match the spectrum. C: CH₂Br₂ would give three peaks at 172, 174 and 176 in a 1 : 2 : 1 ratio; one atom of bromine gives two equal peaks.

**N-1.2-B-v2.** A pure compound that contains only carbon, hydrogen and chlorine gives a mass spectrum with molecular-ion peaks at m/z 118, 120, 122 and 124 with relative heights 100, 96, 31 and 3. Use the isotope masses ¹²C = 12, ¹H = 1, ³⁵Cl = 35, ³⁷Cl = 37, ⁷⁹Br = 79 and ⁸¹Br = 81, and ignore ¹³C and ²H. Natural chlorine is about 75% ³⁵Cl and 25% ³⁷Cl; natural bromine is about 51% ⁷⁹Br and 49% ⁸¹Br. Which formula is consistent with the spectrum?

- A. C₉H₁₀, because a hydrocarbon of this formula has a mass of 118.
- B. CH₂Cl₂, because the pattern of several peaks shows that chlorine is present.
- **C. CHCl₃, because its mass is 118 and four peaks in a ratio near 27 : 27 : 9 : 1 indicate three chlorine atoms.**
- D. CHBr₃, because four peaks are produced by a compound with three halogen atoms.

Check: CH³⁵Cl₃ = 12 + 1 + 105 = 118; the binomial pattern (0.75 + 0.25)³ = 0.42 : 0.42 : 0.14 : 0.016 matches 100 : 96 : 31 : 3 for three Cl atoms.
Rationales: A: C₉H₁₀ has mass 118 but contains no chlorine, so it would show one peak and not four peaks spaced two mass units apart. B: CH₂Cl₂ would have molecular ions at 84, 86 and 88 in a 9 : 6 : 1 ratio, so it gives three peaks at lower mass. D: CHBr₃ also has three halogen atoms but its peaks would be near m/z 250 to 256, and the ratio would be about 1 : 3 : 3 : 1, not 100 : 96 : 31 : 3.

**N-1.2-B-v3.** A pure compound that contains only carbon, hydrogen and bromine gives a mass spectrum with molecular-ion peaks at m/z 172 (relative height 50), 174 (100) and 176 (49). Use the isotope masses ¹²C = 12, ¹H = 1, ³⁵Cl = 35, ³⁷Cl = 37, ⁷⁹Br = 79 and ⁸¹Br = 81, and ignore ¹³C and ²H. Natural chlorine is about 75% ³⁵Cl and 25% ³⁷Cl; natural bromine is about 51% ⁷⁹Br and 49% ⁸¹Br. Which formula is consistent with the spectrum?

- A. C₁₃H₁₆, because a hydrocarbon of this formula also has a mass of 172.
- **B. CH₂Br₂, because its mass is 172 and the 1 : 2 : 1 pattern of three peaks indicates two bromine atoms.**
- C. CH₂Cl₂, because three peaks with a ratio near 1 : 2 : 1 are produced by two chlorine atoms.
- D. CHBr₃, because the three peaks show that the molecule contains three bromine atoms.

Check: CH₂⁷⁹Br₂ = 12 + 2 + 158 = 172, with ⁷⁹Br⁸¹Br at 174 and ⁸¹Br₂ at 176; (0.5 + 0.5)² gives 1 : 2 : 1 for two Br atoms.
Rationales: A: C₁₃H₁₆ has mass 172 but contains no bromine, so it would show a single molecular-ion peak instead of three peaks. C: CH₂Cl₂ is two halogen atoms, but chlorine gives a 9 : 6 : 1 ratio and the peaks would be at 84, 86 and 88. D: CHBr₃ would give four peaks near m/z 250 in a ratio of about 1 : 3 : 3 : 1; the number of peaks is one more than the number of Br atoms, so three peaks mean two Br atoms.

## Seed N-1.3-A (new) — Topic 1.3 Elemental Composition of Pure Substances

## Seed N-1.8-B (new) — Topic 1.8 Valence Electrons and Ionic Compounds
**Skill:** combine electron transfer, ionic charges and formula ratios to count ions and transferred electrons in a sample.

A sample of calcium nitride, Ca₃N₂, contains 6.02 × 10²² nitride ions, N³⁻. Which row correctly gives the number of Ca²⁺ ions in the sample and the total number of electrons transferred from calcium atoms to nitrogen atoms when the sample formed?

- **A. 9.03 × 10²² Ca²⁺ ions; 1.81 × 10²³ electrons.**
- B. 4.01 × 10²² Ca²⁺ ions; 8.03 × 10²² electrons.
- C. 9.03 × 10²² Ca²⁺ ions; 9.03 × 10²² electrons.
- D. 9.03 × 10²² Ca²⁺ ions; 2.71 × 10²³ electrons.

Check: Ca²⁺ = 6.02 × 10²² × 3/2 = 9.03 × 10²²; electrons = 2 × 9.03 × 10²² = 1.81 × 10²³ (equals 3 × 6.02 × 10²² gained by N).
Rationales: B: This inverts the ratio: the formula Ca₃N₂ has three Ca²⁺ for every two N³⁻, so there are 1.5 times as many Ca²⁺ ions as N³⁻ ions. C: This counts only one electron per calcium ion; each Ca atom loses two electrons to become Ca²⁺. D: This gives each Ca atom three electrons, using the charge of the nitride ion; each Ca atom loses only two.

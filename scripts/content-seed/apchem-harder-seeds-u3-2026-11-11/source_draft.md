## Seed N-3.1-A (replaces 029 family) — Topic 3.1 Intermolecular and Interparticle Forces
**Skill:** weigh hydrogen bonding against the total dispersion force of a much larger molecule.

Water (H2O, 18 g/mol) boils at 100°C. Octane (C8H18, 114 g/mol) boils at 126°C even though its molecules cannot hydrogen bond. Which statement best explains why octane has the higher boiling point?

- A. Octane molecules are polar, so dipole-dipole forces add to the dispersion forces between them.
- **B. Octane molecules have many more electrons and a larger surface area, so the total London dispersion attraction between them exceeds the hydrogen bonding in water.**
- C. The C-H bonds in octane are stronger than the O-H bonds in water, so more energy is needed to boil octane.
- D. Octane forms hydrogen bonds through its many H atoms, and there are more of them than in water.

Check: bp octane 126°C, water 100°C; octane is nonpolar, so only London forces act, which grow with electron count and surface area.
Rationales: A: octane is nonpolar because its C-H bonds are nearly nonpolar and the molecule is symmetric, so it has no dipole-dipole forces. C: boiling breaks intermolecular attractions and leaves the covalent bonds inside each molecule intact, so bond strength is not the reason. D: hydrogen bonding needs H bonded to N, O or F, and H atoms bonded to C do not form hydrogen bonds.

**N-3.1-A-v1.** Methanol (CH3OH, 32 g/mol) boils at 65°C, while pentane (C5H12, 72 g/mol) boils at 36°C. Pentane has more electrons and stronger dispersion forces, yet methanol boils higher. Which statement best explains this?

- A. The O-H bond in methanol is stronger than the C-H bonds in pentane, so more energy is needed to boil methanol.
- B. Pentane molecules are less polarizable than methanol molecules, so their dispersion forces are weaker.
- **C. Hydrogen bonding between methanol molecules is strong enough to outweigh the greater dispersion forces of pentane.**
- D. Methanol has stronger London dispersion forces than pentane because oxygen is more electronegative than carbon.

Check: methanol has an O-H group and hydrogen bonds; pentane is nonpolar with only dispersion forces. At these sizes the hydrogen bonds dominate.
Rationales: A: boiling overcomes attractions between molecules and does not break covalent bonds within them. B: pentane has more electrons than methanol and is more polarizable, so its dispersion forces are stronger, not weaker. D: electronegativity creates dipoles and hydrogen bonds, and it does not make dispersion forces stronger.

**N-3.1-A-v2.** The normal boiling points are HF 20°C, HCl -85°C, HBr -67°C and HI -35°C. Which statement best explains this pattern?

- A. The boiling points from HCl to HI rise because the bonds get more polar, and HF is the exception because it is smallest.
- B. HF has the highest boiling point because the H-F bond is the strongest, and the rise from HCl to HI is because those bonds get stronger.
- **C. HF molecules hydrogen bond, which makes it unusually high, while the rise from HCl to HI is due to stronger dispersion forces as the molecules get larger.**
- D. The boiling points follow molar mass, so HI should be the highest and HF the lowest, and the HF value is probably in error.

Check: HF hydrogen bonds; for HCl, HBr, HI the molecules have similar polarity and bp rises with size (dispersion).
Rationales: A: the bonds become less polar from HCl to HI, since electronegativity of the halogen falls, so polarity cannot explain the rise. B: the H-X bonds get weaker down the group, and boiling does not break them anyway. D: the HF value is correct, and the exception is explained by hydrogen bonding that molar mass cannot account for.

**N-3.1-A-v3.** At 25°C, diethyl ether (CH3CH2OCH2CH3) has a vapor pressure of about 530 torr and 1-butanol (CH3CH2CH2CH2OH) about 7 torr. Both have a molar mass of 74 g/mol. Which statement best explains why 1-butanol has the much lower vapor pressure?

- A. 1-butanol has the larger molar mass, so its dispersion forces are much stronger.
- B. Diethyl ether is nonpolar, so its molecules escape the liquid more easily.
- C. The C-O bonds in 1-butanol are stronger than those in diethyl ether, so 1-butanol molecules are harder to vaporize.
- **D. 1-butanol molecules hydrogen bond through their O-H groups, so the attractions are stronger and fewer molecules escape; diethyl ether has no O-H group.**

Check: equal molar mass (74 g/mol) so dispersion is similar; 1-butanol has O-H (hydrogen bonds), ether does not, so butanol's attractions are stronger and its vapor pressure lower.
Rationales: A: both molecules have the same molar mass, so dispersion forces are comparable and cannot account for the difference. B: diethyl ether is polar because of its bent C-O-C arrangement, so it is not nonpolar. C: vaporization does not break covalent bonds, so bond strength inside the molecule is not the reason.

## Seed N-3.1-B (replaces 030 family) — Topic 3.1
**Skill:** compare molecules of similar size by polarity or shape.

Iodine monochloride (ICl, 162 g/mol) boils at 97°C, while bromine (Br2, 160 g/mol) boils at 59°C. Which statement best explains why ICl boils higher?

- **A. ICl is polar, so its molecules attract through dipole-dipole forces in addition to dispersion forces of similar size.**
- B. ICl is ionic, so strong electrostatic attractions act between its ions.
- C. The I-Cl bond is stronger than the Br-Br bond, so more energy is needed to boil ICl.
- D. ICl forms hydrogen bonds because it contains a very electronegative atom, chlorine.

Check: both molecules have about the same number of electrons, so dispersion forces are similar; ICl is polar (Cl more electronegative than I) and Br2 is nonpolar.
Rationales: B: ICl is a molecular compound with a polar covalent bond, and its particles are molecules, not ions. C: boiling overcomes forces between molecules and does not break bonds inside them. D: hydrogen bonding requires H bonded to N, O or F and neither ICl nor Br2 contains hydrogen.

**N-3.1-B-v1.** Pentane and neopentane both have the formula C5H12 (72 g/mol). Pentane (a straight chain) boils at 36°C and neopentane (a compact, nearly spherical molecule) boils at 10°C. Which statement best explains the difference?

- A. Pentane has more electrons than neopentane, so its dispersion forces are stronger.
- B. Neopentane is polar, which weakens attractions between its molecules.
- **C. Pentane molecules have a larger surface area for contact, so London dispersion forces between them are stronger than between compact neopentane molecules.**
- D. Pentane forms hydrogen bonds along its chain, while neopentane cannot.

Check: same formula, same number of electrons; the straight chain has more surface contact so stronger dispersion forces.
Rationales: A: both molecules have the same formula and therefore the same number of electrons. B: neopentane is symmetric and nonpolar, and polarity is not what separates these two isomers. D: neither molecule has H bonded to N, O or F, so neither can hydrogen bond.

**N-3.1-B-v2.** Ammonia (NH3, 17 g/mol) boils at -33°C and phosphine (PH3, 34 g/mol) boils at -88°C. Which statement best explains why NH3 boils higher despite its much smaller molar mass?

- **A. NH3 molecules hydrogen bond with one another, while PH3 molecules do not hydrogen bond to a significant extent.**
- B. The N-H bonds are weaker than the P-H bonds, so NH3 vaporizes at a higher temperature.
- C. PH3 is more polar than NH3, so its dipole-dipole forces are stronger.
- D. NH3 has the larger London dispersion forces because nitrogen is smaller than phosphorus.

Check: NH3 has H bonded to N and hydrogen bonds; PH3 does not hydrogen bond to a significant extent, so the hydrogen bonding of NH3 outweighs the greater dispersion forces of the larger PH3.
Rationales: B: boiling does not break the covalent N-H bonds, and the N-H bond is actually stronger than P-H. C: N is more electronegative than P, so NH3 is the more polar molecule. D: the smaller molecule has fewer electrons and weaker dispersion forces, not stronger ones.

**N-3.1-B-v3.** Propane (CH3CH2CH3, 44 g/mol) boils at -42°C and acetaldehyde (CH3CHO, 44 g/mol) boils at 20°C. Which statement best explains why acetaldehyde has the higher boiling point?

- A. Acetaldehyde molecules hydrogen bond through the H atom on its carbonyl carbon.
- **B. Acetaldehyde molecules are polar, so dipole-dipole attractions add to dispersion forces of about the same size as in propane.**
- C. Acetaldehyde has stronger dispersion forces because it contains oxygen.
- D. Acetaldehyde is a larger molecule than propane, so its dispersion forces are much stronger.

Check: same molar mass so similar dispersion forces; acetaldehyde has a C=O dipole, propane is nonpolar; neither has an O-H or N-H group for hydrogen bonding.
Rationales: A: hydrogen bonding needs H bonded to N, O or F; the H on the carbonyl carbon is bonded to C. C: the two molecules have the same molar mass and similar numbers of electrons, so dispersion forces are similar. D: the two molecules have the same molar mass, so their dispersion forces are about equal, and the difference comes from the dipole of acetaldehyde.

## Seed N-3.2-A (replaces 031 family) — Topic 3.2 Properties of Solids
**Skill:** use Coulomb's law (ion charge and size) to explain ionic solid properties.

Magnesium oxide (MgO) melts at 2852°C, whereas sodium chloride (NaCl) melts at 801°C. Which statement best explains why MgO has the much higher melting point?

- A. MgO has covalent bonds between Mg and O, while NaCl has ionic bonds.
- **B. The Mg2+ and O2- ions have larger charges and are smaller than Na+ and Cl-, so the Coulombic attractions in the lattice are much stronger.**
- C. MgO is a network solid, while NaCl is a molecular solid held by dispersion forces.
- D. The oxide ion has more electrons than the chloride ion, so dispersion forces are much stronger in MgO.

Check: Coulombic force scales with the product of the charges and falls with distance: (2+)(2-) versus (1+)(1-), and the ions are also smaller.
Rationales: A: both are ionic compounds, and the difference is the size of the charges and the ions. C: neither solid is a network or a molecular solid, since both are ionic lattices. D: the oxide ion has fewer electrons than the chloride ion (10 compared with 18), and in any case dispersion forces are minor next to the Coulombic attractions between ions.

**N-3.2-A-v1.** Sodium fluoride (NaF) melts at 993°C and sodium iodide (NaI) melts at 661°C. Which statement best explains why NaF melts at the higher temperature?

- **A. The fluoride ion is smaller than the iodide ion, so the ions are closer together and the Coulombic attraction is stronger.**
- B. The fluoride ion has a larger charge than the iodide ion.
- C. NaI is a molecular solid and NaF is an ionic solid.
- D. NaF has stronger London dispersion forces because fluorine is more electronegative than iodine.

Check: same charges (1+, 1-); the smaller F- gives a smaller interionic distance and stronger attraction.
Rationales: B: both halide ions carry a 1- charge, so charge cannot explain the difference. C: both are ionic solids made of ions in a lattice. D: electronegativity does not set the strength of dispersion forces, and ionic attractions dominate over dispersion forces in these solids.

**N-3.2-A-v2.** Calcium oxide (CaO) melts at 2613°C and potassium chloride (KCl) melts at 770°C. The ionic radii are comparable: K+ 138 pm and Ca2+ 100 pm, Cl- 181 pm and O2- 140 pm. Which statement best explains the large difference?

- **A. The larger ion charges in CaO (2+ and 2-) lead to much stronger Coulombic attractions than the 1+ and 1- charges in KCl.**
- B. CaO contains covalent network bonds and KCl does not.
- C. Ion radii are the only factor, and CaO has the smaller radii, so charge is unimportant.
- D. KCl has more ions per formula unit, so it needs less energy to melt.

Check: the charge product is 4 for CaO and 1 for KCl, which outweighs the modest size difference.
Rationales: B: CaO is an ionic solid, not a covalent network solid. C: radius matters, but the factor of four in charge product is the dominant difference here. D: KCl and CaO each have one cation and one anion per formula unit.

**N-3.2-A-v3.** Potassium chloride (KCl, 770°C) melts lower than sodium chloride (NaCl, 801°C). Which statement best explains why?

- **A. K+ has a larger radius than Na+, so the ions in KCl are farther apart and the Coulombic attraction is weaker.**
- B. K+ has a smaller charge than Na+, so the attraction is weaker.
- C. KCl forms covalent bonds, and NaCl does not.
- D. Cl- is larger in KCl than in NaCl, so the lattice is weaker.

Check: same charges; K+ (138 pm) is larger than Na+ (102 pm), so the interionic distance is greater.
Rationales: B: Na+ and K+ both carry a 1+ charge, so the charge product is the same in both lattices and cannot explain the difference. C: both are ionic compounds, and neither forms covalent bonds between ions. D: the chloride ion is the same ion in both compounds and has the same size.

## Seed N-3.2-B (replaces oht-3.2) — Topic 3.2
**Skill:** classify solids from combined property data, including conductivity in the solid and molten states.

Four solids were tested. W conducts electricity as a solid, melts at 1085°C, and can be hammered flat. X does not conduct as a solid but conducts when molten, melts at 801°C, and shatters when struck. Y does not conduct in either state, melts near 3500°C, and is extremely hard. Z does not conduct in either state, melts at −57°C, and is soft. Which choice correctly identifies the type of each solid in the order W, X, Y, Z?

- **A. Metallic, ionic, covalent network, molecular**
- B. Metallic, molecular, covalent network, ionic
- C. Covalent network, ionic, metallic, molecular
- D. Metallic, ionic, molecular, covalent network

Check: conducting as a solid and malleable means metallic; conducting only when molten and brittle means ionic; very hard with a very high melting point and no conduction means covalent network; low melting point, soft and no conduction means molecular.
Rationales: B: X conducts when molten, which an ionic solid does and a molecular solid does not, and Z with a very low melting point is molecular, not ionic. C: conducting as a solid points to a metal, and an extremely hard solid with a very high melting point is a network solid. D: Y is extremely hard with a melting point near 3500°C, which fits a network solid, while Z melts at −57°C, which fits a molecular solid.

**N-3.2-B-v1.** Solid P conducts electricity as a solid, melts above 3500°C, and is soft and flaky, with layers that slide easily. Solid Q conducts in no state, melts at 1710°C, and is hard and brittle. Which description of P is best supported?

- A. A metallic solid, because it conducts electricity in the solid state.
- **B. A covalent network solid whose electrons are delocalized within its layers, which explains the conduction and the very high melting point.**
- C. A molecular solid, because it is soft and flaky.
- D. An ionic solid, because its melting point is very high.

Check: P is a covalent network solid with layers (graphite): strong covalent bonds in the layers give a very high melting point, delocalized electrons carry current, and weak forces between layers make it soft.
Rationales: A: a metal is malleable and does not flake, and a melting point above 3500°C with soft flaking layers fits graphite, a network solid. C: molecular solids have low melting points, so a melting point above 3500°C rules this out. D: ionic solids do not conduct as solids, since their ions are fixed in the lattice.

**N-3.2-B-v2.** A crystalline solid K melts at 2800°C and shatters when struck. It does not conduct electricity as a solid but does conduct when melted. Which statement best explains why K shatters instead of bending?

- **A. A shift of the layers places ions of the same charge next to each other, and the repulsion splits the crystal.**
- B. Its delocalized electrons cannot move, so the layers lock together and do not slide.
- C. Weak intermolecular forces between molecules allow the crystal to break apart easily.
- D. Its covalent bonds are directional, so the lattice is rigid and cannot bend.

Check: K is ionic (non-conducting solid, conducting melt, high mp); displacement of a layer aligns like charges, which repel.
Rationales: B: ionic solids contain no delocalized electrons, since their ions are held in a lattice and do not conduct as solids. C: a melting point of 2800°C is far too high for a molecular solid held by intermolecular forces. D: K conducts when molten, which a covalent network solid does not do, so K is ionic rather than covalent.

**N-3.2-B-v3.** Brass (copper with zinc atoms of similar size in place of some copper atoms) and steel (iron with small carbon atoms between the iron atoms) are both harder than the pure metals. Which statement is correct?

- **A. Brass is a substitutional alloy and steel is an interstitial alloy; in both, the added atoms disrupt the regular layers and make it harder for them to slide.**
- B. Brass is an interstitial alloy and steel is a substitutional alloy, and both are harder because the bonds become ionic.
- C. Both are substitutional alloys, since each contains more than one element in a metallic lattice.
- D. Both are interstitial alloys, and both are harder because the added atoms form covalent molecules.

Check: Zn replaces Cu (similar size) in brass, so substitutional; C fits between Fe atoms in steel, so interstitial; added atoms hinder sliding of layers.
Rationales: B: this reverses the two types, and alloys are still held by metallic bonding, not ionic bonding. C: steel's carbon atoms are small and sit in gaps, which is interstitial, so not all alloys are substitutional. D: brass's zinc atoms replace copper atoms of a similar size, and neither alloy forms covalent molecules.

## Seed N-3.3-A (replaces oht-3.3, adds depth) — Topic 3.3 Solids, Liquids, and Gases
**Skill:** separate temperature (average kinetic energy) from potential energy during a phase change.

A pure liquid is heated at its normal boiling point, and its temperature stays constant while it boils. Which statement best describes what happens to the added energy at the particle level?

- A. The average kinetic energy of the particles increases, which is why the liquid vaporizes.
- B. The covalent bonds within each molecule break, which produces the gas.
- **C. The energy is used to overcome intermolecular attractions, increasing the potential energy of the particles while their average kinetic energy stays the same.**
- D. The energy is stored in the intermolecular forces, which become stronger as the particles move apart.

Check: temperature is a measure of average kinetic energy; it is constant during the phase change, so energy goes to overcoming attractions.
Rationales: A: temperature stays constant, so the average kinetic energy does not change. B: boiling separates molecules from one another and does not break the bonds inside them. D: moving particles apart weakens the intermolecular attractions rather than strengthening them.

**N-3.3-A-v1.** Ice at 0°C is heated and melts at 0°C, with the temperature remaining at 0°C until it has all melted. Which statement best describes the energy absorbed during melting?

- A. It raises the average kinetic energy of the water molecules.
- B. It breaks the O-H covalent bonds in each molecule.
- **C. It partly overcomes the hydrogen bonds holding the molecules in fixed positions, raising potential energy with no change in average kinetic energy.**
- D. It strengthens the hydrogen bonds so that the molecules can flow.

Check: constant temperature means constant average kinetic energy; the energy goes into overcoming attractions.
Rationales: A: the temperature remains constant during melting, so the average kinetic energy is unchanged. B: the covalent bonds remain intact, since water is still H2O after melting. D: hydrogen bonds are partly broken in liquid water, not strengthened.

**N-3.3-A-v2.** Two liquids, X and Y, are kept at the same temperature. X evaporates much faster than Y. Which statement is best supported?

- A. The molecules of X have a greater average kinetic energy than those of Y.
- **B. X molecules are held by weaker intermolecular forces, so a larger fraction of them have enough energy to escape, even though the average kinetic energy is the same.**
- C. X has stronger intermolecular forces than Y.
- D. Y molecules move faster than X molecules because Y has a lower vapor pressure.

Check: same temperature means the same average kinetic energy; weaker attractions let more molecules escape.
Rationales: A: the liquids are at the same temperature, so their average kinetic energies are equal. C: stronger intermolecular forces would make X evaporate more slowly. D: the speed of a molecule at a given temperature depends on mass, not on its vapor pressure.

**N-3.3-A-v3.** A pure substance is heated at a constant rate from a solid to a gas. The plateau at its boiling point lasts much longer than the plateau at its melting point. Which statement best explains this?

- A. During boiling, the average kinetic energy of the particles is rising, so the temperature is not rising and the stored energy is released as heat.
- **B. Boiling requires nearly complete separation of the particles, which means overcoming more of the intermolecular attraction than melting does, so more energy is absorbed at constant temperature.**
- C. The covalent bonds in each molecule break during boiling but not during melting.
- D. Gas particles have stronger intermolecular forces than solid particles, which takes longer to establish.

Check: constant heating rate means plateau length is proportional to the energy absorbed; vaporization overcomes more attraction than melting, so ΔHvap is greater than ΔHfus.
Rationales: A: the average kinetic energy is constant on a plateau because the temperature is constant, and energy is absorbed rather than released. C: neither process breaks the covalent bonds inside molecules, since both separate whole molecules from one another. D: gas particles are far apart and attract one another much less than particles in a solid.

## Seed N-3.3-B (new) — Topic 3.3
**Skill:** relate temperature, mass and speed, and explain gas behavior with particle models.

Helium and xenon gas are each held at 300 K. Which statement about the particles is correct?

- **A. Helium and xenon have the same average kinetic energy, and the helium atoms move faster on average.**
- B. Helium and xenon have the same average speed, because they are at the same temperature.
- C. Xenon atoms have the greater average kinetic energy, because they are more massive.
- D. Helium atoms have the greater average kinetic energy, because they are lighter.

Check: average kinetic energy depends only on temperature; with KE = (1/2)mv^2 and equal KE, the lighter atoms have the higher speed.
Rationales: B: the average speeds differ, because equal kinetic energy with unequal mass requires unequal speeds. C: average kinetic energy does not depend on mass, only on temperature. D: lighter particles move faster, but they do not have more kinetic energy at the same temperature.

**N-3.3-B-v1.** Two rigid 5.0 L containers are kept at 300 K. One holds 1.0 mol of H2 and the other holds 1.0 mol of CO2. Which statement about the pressures is correct?

- A. The CO2 container has the higher pressure, because its heavier molecules hit the walls harder.
- B. The H2 container has the higher pressure, because its molecules move faster.
- C. The pressures cannot be compared without knowing the molar masses.
- **D. The pressures are equal, because for an ideal gas the pressure depends on the moles, volume and temperature, not on the identity of the gas.**

Check: P = nRT/V, which is the same for both containers (about 4.9 atm); the faster, lighter molecules hit more often and the heavier ones hit harder, and the two effects cancel.
Rationales: A: heavier molecules move more slowly at the same temperature, and the two effects cancel, so mass does not change the pressure. B: lighter molecules are faster, but they hit the wall with less momentum per collision, and the effects cancel. C: the ideal gas law does not involve molar mass, so the pressures can be compared directly.

**N-3.3-B-v2.** Of the gases NH3 and H2, which would be expected to deviate more from ideal behavior at low temperature and high pressure, and why?

- A. H2, because its molecules are smaller and are packed more tightly.
- **B. NH3, because its molecules have stronger intermolecular attractions, which become significant when the molecules are close together and slow.**
- C. H2, because its particles move faster and collide more often.
- D. They deviate equally, because all gases behave ideally at high pressure.

Check: NH3 hydrogen bonds and has a larger volume; at low T and high P attractions and particle volume matter more.
Rationales: A: small molecules with weak attractions behave more ideally, and H2 is the more ideal gas. C: fast-moving particles overcome attractions more easily, so H2 stays closer to ideal. D: real gases deviate most at high pressure and low temperature, so the claim of ideal behavior is false.

**N-3.3-B-v3.** A sample of gas in a rigid container is heated from 300 K to 600 K. Which statement is correct?

- A. The average speed of the particles doubles.
- B. The average kinetic energy of the particles stays the same, because the number of particles is unchanged.
- C. The average speed of the particles increases by a factor of 4.
- **D. The average kinetic energy of the particles doubles, and the average speed increases by a factor of √2.**

Check: average kinetic energy is proportional to the Kelvin temperature, so it doubles; KE = (1/2)mv², so speed rises by √2.
Rationales: A: doubling the temperature doubles the kinetic energy, and the speed rises by only the square root of 2. B: average kinetic energy depends on temperature, which changed, and not on the number of particles. C: a factor of 4 is the change in kinetic energy if the speed doubled, which does not follow from doubling the temperature.

## Seed N-4.1-A (new) — Topic 4.1 Introduction for Reactions
**Skill:** account for a mass change in an open system by identifying the gas that enters or leaves.

A student heats 2.00 g of magnesium ribbon strongly in an open crucible until it has all been converted to a white solid, MgO. The white solid has a mass of 3.32 g. Which statement best explains the mass change?

- A. Mass is not conserved in this reaction because energy was released as heat and light.
- **B. Oxygen gas from the air combined with the magnesium, and the 1.32 g gain is the mass of oxygen that reacted, so the total mass of matter is conserved.**
- C. Magnesium atoms became heavier when they turned into Mg²⁺ ions, because an ion has more mass than its atom.
- D. Water vapor from the air condensed onto the hot crucible, and its mass accounts for the 1.32 g gain.

Check: 2.00 g ÷ 24.31 g/mol = 0.0823 mol Mg gives 0.0823 mol MgO = 3.32 g. The gain, 3.32 - 2.00 = 1.32 g, equals 0.0823 mol × 16.00 g/mol of oxygen atoms.
Rationales: A: the energy released as heat and light is far too small to change the measured mass, and the law of conservation of mass holds for all chemical reactions. C: an ion differs from its atom by electrons only, which have negligible mass, so the ion is no heavier. D: the crucible is far above 100°C, so water cannot condense, and the gain matches exactly one oxygen atom per magnesium atom in MgO.

**N-4.1-A-v1.** A student heats 6.18 g of green copper(II) carbonate, CuCO3, in an open crucible. It turns into black copper(II) oxide, CuO, with a mass of 3.98 g. Which statement best explains the mass decrease?

- A. Mass was destroyed when the bonds in CuCO3 were broken.
- B. The heat from the burner carried away mass equal to the 2.20 g difference.
- **C. A gas, CO2, formed and escaped into the air, and its mass is the 2.20 g difference.**
- D. Copper atoms evaporated from the crucible while it was heated.

Check: 6.18 g ÷ 123.56 g/mol = 0.0500 mol CuCO3 gives 0.0500 mol CuO = 3.98 g and 0.0500 mol CO2 = 2.20 g; 6.18 - 3.98 = 2.20 g.
Rationales: A: breaking bonds rearranges the same atoms and does not destroy mass; the atoms that left are in the escaping gas. B: heat is energy supplied to the sample and does not carry off matter with a mass of 2.20 g. D: copper is not volatile at these temperatures, and the CuO left behind holds all of the original copper.

**N-4.1-A-v2.** A student burns 1.12 g of iron wool in air and collects 1.60 g of rust-colored Fe2O3. Which statement is consistent with the law of conservation of mass?

- **A. The product contains 0.48 g of oxygen that came from the air, so the mass of iron plus the mass of oxygen that reacted equals the mass of Fe2O3.**
- B. Iron atoms were converted into heavier atoms when the iron was heated, which raised the mass by 0.48 g.
- C. The heat added 0.48 g of mass to the iron, which is why the product weighs more than the reactant.
- D. Conservation of mass applies only to closed containers, so the extra 0.48 g in the open crucible is not accounted for by any reactant.

Check: 1.12 g ÷ 55.85 g/mol = 0.0201 mol Fe gives 0.0100 mol Fe2O3 = 1.60 g; the oxygen is 1.60 - 1.12 = 0.48 g = 0.015 mol O2.
Rationales: B: a chemical reaction does not change one element into another, so the iron atoms keep their mass. C: heat is energy and its mass equivalent is far too small to measure, so it cannot account for 0.48 g. D: conservation of mass applies to every reaction, and in an open system the air is simply an unweighed reactant.

**N-4.1-A-v3.** A student places 2.10 g of NaHCO3 and an excess of vinegar in an open flask on a balance. As the CO2 bubbles away, the balance reading falls by 1.10 g. If the same reaction were run in a sealed, rigid flask on the same balance, what change in balance reading would be observed?

- A. A decrease of 1.10 g, the same as in the open flask.
- B. A decrease of 1.10 g, because gases have no mass and the CO2 does not count.
- C. An increase of 1.10 g, because the trapped gas adds to the mass.
- **D. No change, because the CO2 stays inside the flask and the total mass of everything on the balance is conserved.**

Check: 2.10 g ÷ 84.01 g/mol = 0.0250 mol NaHCO3 gives 0.0250 mol CO2 = 1.10 g. In a sealed flask all products remain on the balance, so the total mass is unchanged.
Rationales: A: in the open flask the CO2 leaves the balance, but in a sealed flask it cannot, so the reading does not drop. B: gases are matter and have mass; the 1.10 g loss in the open flask is exactly the mass of the CO2 that escaped. C: the trapped CO2 came from the reactants already in the flask, so no new mass is added.

## Seed N-4.1-B (new) — Topic 4.1 Introduction for Reactions
**Skill:** choose the balanced equation that matches the stated products, checking every atom.

Ethanol, C2H5OH, burns completely in excess oxygen to form only carbon dioxide and water. Which equation, written with the smallest whole-number coefficients, correctly represents this reaction?

- A. C2H5OH + 2 O2 → 2 CO + 3 H2O
- B. C2H5OH + 2 O2 → 2 CO2 + 3 H2O
- **C. C2H5OH + 3 O2 → 2 CO2 + 3 H2O**
- D. C2H5OH + 3 O2 → 2 CO2 + 3 H2O2

Check: left side C 2, H 6, O 1 + 6 = 7; right side C 2, H 6, O 4 + 3 = 7. Balanced and the products are CO2 and H2O.
Rationales: A: the equation is balanced, but CO is the product of incomplete combustion and the problem states that only CO2 and H2O form. B: it forgets the oxygen atom already in ethanol, leaving 5 O atoms on the left and 7 on the right. D: changing the subscript of water to make H2O2 changes the substance, and the equation still has 7 O atoms on the left and 10 on the right.

**N-4.1-B-v1.** Methanol, CH3OH, burns completely in excess oxygen to form only CO2 and H2O. Which equation, written with the smallest whole-number coefficients, is correct?

- A. CH3OH + O2 → CO2 + 2 H2O
- **B. 2 CH3OH + 3 O2 → 2 CO2 + 4 H2O**
- C. 2 CH3OH + 2 O2 → 2 CO + 4 H2O
- D. CH3OH + 2 O2 → CO2 + 2 H2O

Check: left side C 2, H 8, O 2 + 6 = 8; right side C 2, H 8, O 4 + 4 = 8.
Rationales: A: it has 3 O atoms on the left and 4 on the right, so oxygen is not balanced. C: the equation is balanced, but it produces CO, which is not the complete-combustion product stated. D: it counts only the oxygen from O2, forgetting the O atom in methanol, which leaves 5 O atoms on the left and 4 on the right.

**N-4.1-B-v2.** Ethane, C2H6, burns completely in excess oxygen to form only CO2 and H2O. Which equation, written with the smallest whole-number coefficients, is correct?

- A. C2H6 + 3 O2 → 2 CO2 + 3 H2O
- B. 2 C2H6 + 5 O2 → 4 CO + 6 H2O
- C. C2H6 + 7 O2 → 2 CO2 + 3 H2O
- **D. 2 C2H6 + 7 O2 → 4 CO2 + 6 H2O**

Check: left side C 4, H 12, O 14; right side C 4, H 12, O 8 + 6 = 14.
Rationales: A: carbon and hydrogen are balanced, but the right side has 7 O atoms and the left only 6. B: it is balanced, but CO is not the complete-combustion product. C: it keeps the coefficient 7 for O2 from the doubled equation without doubling the other coefficients, leaving 14 O atoms on the left and 7 on the right.

**N-4.1-B-v3.** Glucose, C6H12O6, is oxidized completely in the body to form only CO2 and H2O. Which equation, written with the smallest whole-number coefficients, is correct?

- **A. C6H12O6 + 6 O2 → 6 CO2 + 6 H2O**
- B. C6H12O6 + 6 O2 → 6 CO2 + 12 H2O
- C. C6H12O6 + 3 O2 → 6 CO + 6 H2O
- D. C6H12O6 + 9 O2 → 6 CO2 + 6 H2O

Check: left side C 6, H 12, O 6 + 12 = 18; right side C 6, H 12, O 12 + 6 = 18.
Rationales: B: it doubles the water to balance hydrogen from only the glucose and gets 18 O atoms on the left and 24 on the right. C: it is balanced, but CO is not the stated product of complete oxidation. D: it uses too much O2, giving 24 O atoms on the left and 18 on the right.

## Seed N-4.2-A (replaces 010 family) — Topic 4.2 Net Ionic Equations
**Skill:** write the net ionic equation when every ion in the mixture takes part in a precipitate.

Aqueous Ba(OH)2 and aqueous CuSO4 are mixed. Both BaSO4 and Cu(OH)2 are insoluble in water. Which is the net ionic equation for the reaction?

- A. Ba²⁺(aq) + SO4²⁻(aq) → BaSO4(s)
- B. Cu²⁺(aq) + 2 OH⁻(aq) → Cu(OH)2(s)
- C. Ba(OH)2(aq) + CuSO4(aq) → BaSO4(s) + Cu(OH)2(s)
- **D. Ba²⁺(aq) + 2 OH⁻(aq) + Cu²⁺(aq) + SO4²⁻(aq) → BaSO4(s) + Cu(OH)2(s)**

Check: every dissolved ion forms a solid, so there are no spectator ions and all four ions appear on the left.
Rationales: A: it shows only one of the two precipitates, but Cu²⁺ and OH⁻ also leave the solution as Cu(OH)2. B: it shows only the copper(II) hydroxide, but Ba²⁺ and SO4²⁻ also leave the solution as BaSO4. C: it is the molecular equation, which writes the soluble reactants as formula units rather than as ions.

**N-4.2-A-v1.** Aqueous Ba(OH)2 and aqueous Fe2(SO4)3 are mixed. Both BaSO4 and Fe(OH)3 are insoluble. Which is the net ionic equation?

- A. 3 Ba²⁺(aq) + 3 SO4²⁻(aq) → 3 BaSO4(s)
- **B. 3 Ba²⁺(aq) + 6 OH⁻(aq) + 2 Fe³⁺(aq) + 3 SO4²⁻(aq) → 3 BaSO4(s) + 2 Fe(OH)3(s)**
- C. Ba²⁺(aq) + 2 OH⁻(aq) + Fe³⁺(aq) + SO4²⁻(aq) → BaSO4(s) + Fe(OH)3(s)
- D. 2 Fe³⁺(aq) + 6 OH⁻(aq) → 2 Fe(OH)3(s)

Check: the ions Ba²⁺, OH⁻, Fe³⁺ and SO4²⁻ all end up in solids. Charge: left 6 + (-6) + 6 + (-6) = 0, and atoms balance.
Rationales: A: it leaves out the iron(III) hydroxide precipitate, but Fe³⁺ and OH⁻ also leave solution. C: it is unbalanced, with 1 Fe on the left and a charge of +1 left over instead of zero. D: it leaves out the barium sulfate precipitate, although Ba²⁺ and SO4²⁻ also leave solution.

**N-4.2-A-v2.** Aqueous Pb(NO3)2 and aqueous KI are mixed, and yellow solid PbI2 forms. KNO3 is soluble. Which is the net ionic equation?

- A. Pb²⁺(aq) + I⁻(aq) → PbI(s)
- B. Pb(NO3)2(aq) + 2 KI(aq) → PbI2(s) + 2 KNO3(aq)
- **C. Pb²⁺(aq) + 2 I⁻(aq) → PbI2(s)**
- D. Pb²⁺(aq) + 2 NO3⁻(aq) + 2 K⁺(aq) + 2 I⁻(aq) → PbI2(s) + 2 K⁺(aq) + 2 NO3⁻(aq)

Check: K⁺ and NO3⁻ stay dissolved and are spectators; only Pb²⁺ and I⁻ combine, in a 1:2 ratio for charge balance.
Rationales: A: lead here is Pb²⁺, which needs two I⁻ ions to balance its charge, so the formula PbI is wrong and must be PbI2. B: it is the molecular equation and shows no ions. D: it is the complete ionic equation, which keeps the spectator ions K⁺ and NO3⁻ on both sides.

**N-4.2-A-v3.** Aqueous KI and aqueous NaNO3 are mixed. Both possible products, KNO3 and NaI, are soluble. Which statement is correct?

- **A. All four ions stay dissolved and are spectators, so there is no net ionic equation.**
- B. K⁺(aq) + NO3⁻(aq) → KNO3(s) is the net ionic equation.
- C. Na⁺(aq) + I⁻(aq) → NaI(s) is the net ionic equation.
- D. A reaction occurs because the molecular equation KI + NaNO3 → KNO3 + NaI can be written.

Check: since no product is insoluble, a gas or a weak electrolyte, no ions are removed from solution.
Rationales: B: KNO3 is soluble, so K⁺ and NO3⁻ do not combine into a solid. C: NaI is soluble, so Na⁺ and I⁻ stay as separate ions in solution. D: a balanced swap of partners can always be written on paper, but a reaction occurs only if something removes ions from solution.

## Seed N-4.2-B (new) — Topic 4.2 Net Ionic Equations
**Skill:** write the net ionic equation for an acid-base reaction, keeping a weak acid in molecular form.

Aqueous acetic acid, HC2H3O2, a weak acid, is mixed with aqueous sodium hydroxide. Which is the net ionic equation for the reaction?

- **A. HC2H3O2(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → C2H3O2⁻(aq) + H2O(l)**
- B. H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H2O(l)
- C. HC2H3O2(aq) + Na⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → Na⁺(aq) + C2H3O2⁻(aq) + H2O(l)
- D. HC2H3O2(aq) + NaOH(aq) → NaC2H3O2(aq) + H2O(l)

Check: acetic acid is mostly molecular in water, so it is written as HC2H3O2; Na⁺ is a spectator and is omitted.
Rationales: B: it treats acetic acid as fully dissociated into H⁺, but a weak acid is mostly un-ionized and should be written as the molecule. C: it is the complete ionic equation, which shows the spectator ion Na⁺ on both sides. D: it is the molecular equation, which writes the soluble ionic compounds as formula units.

**N-4.2-B-v1.** Aqueous hydrofluoric acid, HF, a weak acid, is mixed with aqueous potassium hydroxide. Which is the net ionic equation?

- A. H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H2O(l)
- B. HF(aq) + K⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → K⁺(aq) + F⁻(aq) + H2O(l)
- **C. HF(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → F⁻(aq) + H2O(l)**
- D. HF(aq) + KOH(aq) → KF(aq) + H2O(l)

Check: HF is a weak acid and stays as a molecule; K⁺ is a spectator.
Rationales: A: it treats HF as a strong acid that is fully ionized to H⁺, which it is not. B: it is the complete ionic equation and includes the spectator ion K⁺. D: it is the molecular equation and not a net ionic equation.

**N-4.2-B-v2.** Aqueous ammonia, NH3, a weak base, is mixed with aqueous hydrochloric acid. Which is the net ionic equation?

- A. H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H2O(l)
- **B. NH3(aq) + H⁺(aq) → NH4⁺(aq)**
- C. NH4⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq) → NH4Cl(s)
- D. NH3(aq) + H⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq) → NH4⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq)

Check: HCl is a strong acid, so it supplies H⁺; NH3 accepts the proton to form NH4⁺; Cl⁻ is a spectator.
Rationales: A: aqueous ammonia contains only a tiny concentration of OH⁻, and the main base species is NH3, which reacts directly with H⁺. C: NH4Cl is soluble, so NH4⁺ and Cl⁻ do not form a solid. D: it is the complete ionic equation, which keeps the spectator ion Cl⁻ on both sides.

**N-4.2-B-v3.** Solid Mg(OH)2, which is essentially insoluble in water, dissolves in aqueous HCl. Which is the net ionic equation?

- A. H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H2O(l)
- B. Mg²⁺(aq) + 2 Cl⁻(aq) → MgCl2(s)
- C. Mg(OH)2(s) + 2 H⁺(aq) + 2 Cl⁻(aq) → Mg²⁺(aq) + 2 Cl⁻(aq) + 2 H2O(l)
- **D. Mg(OH)2(s) + 2 H⁺(aq) → Mg²⁺(aq) + 2 H2O(l)**

Check: the insoluble solid is written as a formula unit; Cl⁻ is a spectator; charge is +2 on both sides.
Rationales: A: it treats the Mg(OH)2 as if it were already dissolved to supply free OH⁻, but it is a solid and must be written as Mg(OH)2(s). B: MgCl2 is soluble, so Mg²⁺ and Cl⁻ remain dissolved. C: it is the complete ionic equation and includes the spectator ion Cl⁻ on both sides.

## Seed N-4.3-A (new) — Topic 4.3 Representations of Reactions
**Skill:** draw the product mixture from a particle count, applying the mole ratio and conservation of atoms.

A particle drawing of the reactant mixture shows 3 N2 molecules and 6 H2 molecules in a sealed container. The reaction N2 + 3 H2 → 2 NH3 then goes to completion. Which description of the final mixture is correct?

- A. 6 NH3 molecules and nothing else
- B. 6 NH3 molecules and 3 H2 molecules
- C. 4 NH3 molecules and 2 N2 molecules
- **D. 4 NH3 molecules and 1 N2 molecule**

Check: 6 H2 molecules supply 12 H atoms, enough for 4 NH3; these use 4 N atoms (2 N2), leaving 1 N2. Totals: N = 4 + 2 = 6 atoms, H = 12 atoms, as in the reactants.
Rationales: A: 6 NH3 would need 18 H atoms, but only 12 are present, and it ignores that H2 runs out first. B: 6 NH3 and 3 H2 contain 24 H atoms, twice as many as were available, so atoms are not conserved. C: 4 NH3 and 2 N2 contain 8 N atoms, but only 6 were present, so atoms are not conserved.

**N-4.3-A-v1.** A particle drawing of the reactant mixture shows 6 H2 molecules and 4 O2 molecules in a sealed container. The reaction 2 H2 + O2 → 2 H2O then goes to completion. Which description of the final mixture is correct?

- **A. 6 H2O molecules and 1 O2 molecule**
- B. 8 H2O molecules
- C. 6 H2O molecules and 3 O2 molecules
- D. 6 H2O molecules and 2 O2 molecules

Check: 6 H2 needs 3 O2 and makes 6 H2O, leaving 4 - 3 = 1 O2. Atoms: O = 6 + 2 = 8, H = 12, as before.
Rationales: B: 8 H2O would need 16 H atoms but only 12 are available, so hydrogen is limiting. C: it makes 6 H2O but does not subtract the O2 consumed, so oxygen atoms would not be conserved (12 O instead of 8). D: it leaves 2 O2, giving 10 O atoms in total instead of 8, so atoms are not conserved.

**N-4.3-A-v2.** A particle drawing of the reactant mixture shows 3 CH4 molecules and 4 O2 molecules in a sealed container. The reaction CH4 + 2 O2 → CO2 + 2 H2O then goes to completion. Which description of the final mixture is correct?

- A. 3 CO2 molecules and 6 H2O molecules
- **B. 2 CO2 molecules, 4 H2O molecules and 1 CH4 molecule**
- C. 2 CO2 molecules, 4 H2O molecules and 2 CH4 molecules
- D. 2 CO2 molecules, 2 H2O molecules and 1 CH4 molecule

Check: 4 O2 reacts with 2 CH4, giving 2 CO2 and 4 H2O, leaving 1 CH4. Atoms: C = 2 + 1 = 3; H = 8 + 4 = 12; O = 4 + 4 = 8.
Rationales: A: 3 CO2 and 6 H2O would need 12 O atoms, but only 8 are present because O2 is limiting. C: 2 CH4 left over would give 4 carbon atoms in total, but only 3 were present. D: 2 H2O gives only 8 H atoms in total, but 12 were present, so hydrogen is not conserved.

**N-4.3-A-v3.** A particle drawing of the reactant mixture shows 6 SO2 molecules and 5 O2 molecules in a sealed container. The reaction 2 SO2 + O2 → 2 SO3 then goes to completion. Which description of the final mixture is correct?

- A. 6 SO3 molecules and no other molecules
- B. 6 SO3 molecules and 4 O2 molecules
- **C. 6 SO3 molecules and 2 O2 molecules**
- D. 6 SO3 molecules and 5 O2 molecules

Check: 6 SO2 needs 3 O2 and makes 6 SO3, leaving 5 - 3 = 2 O2. Oxygen atoms: 18 + 4 = 22 = 12 + 10.
Rationales: A: this treats all the O2 as used up, but SO2 is the limiting reactant and 2 O2 remain. B: 6 SO3 and 4 O2 contain 26 O atoms, but only 22 were present. D: 6 SO3 and 5 O2 would mean no O2 was consumed, but 6 SO3 contain O atoms that came from it, giving 28 O atoms instead of 22.

## Seed N-4.3-B (new) — Topic 4.3 Representations of Reactions
**Skill:** decide which quantities are conserved in a particle-level representation of a reaction.

A sealed, rigid container holds 4 H2 molecules and 2 O2 molecules. They react completely according to 2 H2 + O2 → 2 H2O. Which statement correctly compares the contents before and after the reaction?

- **A. The total number of atoms of each element and the total mass are the same, but the number of molecules changes from 6 to 4.**
- B. The total number of molecules and the total mass are the same, but the number of atoms changes.
- C. The total number of molecules and the total number of atoms are the same, but the total mass changes.
- D. The number of molecules, the number of atoms of each element and the total mass all change because a new substance forms.

Check: before, 6 molecules containing 8 H and 4 O atoms; after, 4 H2O molecules containing 8 H and 4 O atoms. The mass is the same.
Rationales: B: the number of molecules is not conserved, since 6 molecules form 4, and atoms are conserved instead. C: mass is conserved because the number of each kind of atom is conserved, so mass does not change. D: forming a new substance rearranges atoms but never changes how many atoms of each element there are or their total mass.

**N-4.3-B-v1.** A rigid container at constant temperature holds 4 CO molecules and 2 O2 molecules at a pressure P0. They react completely according to 2 CO + O2 → 2 CO2. What is the pressure after the reaction, if all species are gases?

- A. P0, because mass and atoms are conserved.
- B. 1.5 P0, because 4 CO2 molecules are formed from the reactants.
- **C. (2/3) P0, because the number of gas molecules falls from 6 to 4.**
- D. (1/2) P0, because the 2 O2 molecules and 2 of the CO molecules disappear.

Check: before, 6 gas molecules; after, 4 CO2 molecules. At constant V and T, pressure is proportional to the number of molecules, so P = (4/6) P0.
Rationales: A: pressure depends on the number of gas molecules, which is not conserved, even though mass and atoms are. B: it divides in the wrong direction, treating the number of molecules as rising from 4 to 6. D: after the reaction only the 4 CO2 molecules remain, so the ratio of molecules is 4 to 6, and not 1 to 2; no CO is left over because the 4 CO and 2 O2 react in exactly the 2:1 ratio.

**N-4.3-B-v2.** A sealed rigid container holds 6 H2O2 molecules, which decompose completely according to 2 H2O2 → 2 H2O + O2. Which statement about the contents after the reaction is correct?

- A. There are 6 molecules, because the number of molecules is conserved in a reaction.
- **B. There are 9 molecules and the total number of atoms is 24, unchanged.**
- C. There are 9 molecules and the total number of atoms is 30.
- D. There are 12 molecules, because each H2O2 gives one H2O and one O2.

Check: 6 H2O2 gives 6 H2O + 3 O2 = 9 molecules; atoms before 6 × 4 = 24; after 6 × 3 + 3 × 2 = 24.
Rationales: A: the number of molecules is not conserved, since 2 H2O2 make 3 molecules. C: the extra 6 atoms are counted from nothing, but atoms are conserved in a chemical reaction. D: each pair of H2O2 gives two H2O and one O2, so each H2O2 gives one H2O and only half an O2.

**N-4.3-B-v3.** A beaker holds a solution with 5 Ag⁺, 5 NO3⁻, 3 Na⁺ and 3 Cl⁻ ions (represented as particles). AgCl is insoluble and NaNO3 is soluble. Which description of the mixture after the precipitation is correct?

- A. 5 AgCl(s) with 3 Na⁺ and 5 NO3⁻ in solution
- B. 3 AgCl(s) with 2 Ag⁺, 3 Na⁺ and 2 NO3⁻ in solution
- C. 3 AgCl(s), 3 NaNO3(s), and 2 Ag⁺ and 2 NO3⁻ in solution
- **D. 3 AgCl(s) with 2 Ag⁺, 3 Na⁺ and 5 NO3⁻ in solution**

Check: only 3 Cl⁻ are available, so 3 AgCl form and 2 Ag⁺ remain. The solution has charge +2 + 3 = +5 and -5, so it is neutral.
Rationales: A: it forms 5 AgCl, but only 3 Cl⁻ are present, so no more than 3 can form. B: it removes 3 nitrate ions without a reason, leaving a solution with +5 charge and only -2 charge. C: NaNO3 is soluble and its ions are spectators, so it does not form a solid.

## Seed N-4.4-A (new) — Topic 4.4 Physical and Chemical Changes
**Skill:** judge which observation is real evidence of a new substance rather than a change of state or dissolving.

A student records four separate observations. Which one is the strongest evidence that a chemical change occurred?

- A. White crystals stirred into water disappear, and the temperature of the water drops by 3°C.
- B. A colorless liquid is heated to boiling; the vapor is condensed, and the collected liquid has the same boiling point as the original liquid.
- **C. A shiny gray metal ribbon burns in air with a bright white flame, leaving a white powder that is heavier than the ribbon and does not conduct electricity.**
- D. A blue solid melts to a deep blue liquid at 110°C and solidifies back to the same blue solid when cooled.

Check: only C produces a substance with properties different from the starting material (metal to nonconducting powder). A is dissolving, B is a boiling and condensing cycle, and D is melting and freezing.
Rationales: A: dissolving can absorb heat, but the crystals can be recovered unchanged on evaporation, so a temperature change alone does not show a new substance. B: the condensed liquid has the same boiling point, which shows it is the same substance as before. D: a color-preserving melting and refreezing returns the identical solid, so only a change of state occurred.

- C. Both need the same volume, because each acid reacts 1:1 with NaOH.
- D. The HCl, because its volume is larger.

Rationales: A: strong acids are not neutralized with more base; the moles of acid decide. C: the 1:1 ratio is true, but the two samples contain different moles of acid. D: volume alone does not decide it, since concentration matters, and the product gives the moles.

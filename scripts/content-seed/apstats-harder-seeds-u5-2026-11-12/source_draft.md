**Skill:** read axes, form, direction, and an unusual point together



A researcher records (hours of sunlight, daily plant growth) for five independent observations: (2, 3), (4, 6), (6, 9), (8, 12), (10, 2). The first quantity is used to explain the second. Which description of the scatterplot is best supported?



- **A. Put hours of sunlight on the horizontal axis and daily plant growth on the vertical axis; four points follow a positive, roughly linear pattern, with the last point unusually low.**

- B. Put daily plant growth on the horizontal axis and hours of sunlight on the vertical axis; the last point proves that the response causes the explanatory variable.

- C. The association is negative and roughly linear because the final response is smaller than the first response.

- D. No pattern is visible because one unusual point cancels the consistent increase in the other four observations.



Check: The explanatory variable is the first coordinate. The first four response values rise with x; the fifth lies well below that pattern. One unusual point does not reverse the four-point direction.

Rationales: B: The variables were recorded as explanatory then response; exchanging the axes changes the intended prediction, and a scatterplot alone does not establish causation. C: The direction of the main pattern comes from all observations rather than comparing only the first and last response values. D: The first four points form a visible positive pattern; the unusual fifth point should be named rather than erasing that pattern.

## Seed N-5.2-A (new) — Topic 5.2 Correlation

**Skill:** track correlation through transformations and avoid causal inference



In a sample, the correlation between distance in miles and delivery time is +0.78. An analyst converts the first variable to a new unit using a positive scale factor, then reports the negative of the second variable instead. Which statement is justified about the new correlation and a possible causal claim?



- **A. The new correlation is -0.78; the sign reverses because one variable was negated, and neither correlation establishes causation.**

- B. The new correlation is +0.78; changing units and negating a variable cannot change correlation's direction.

- C. The new correlation is -0.78; its magnitude establishes that changing distance in miles causes a change in delivery time.

- D. The new correlation is 0 because both variables were transformed before it was computed.



Check: A positive rescaling preserves r=+0.78; multiplying one variable by -1 changes its sign to -0.78. Association alone cannot prove causation.

Rationales: B: Positive rescaling preserves correlation, but negating one of the variables reverses its sign. C: The sign calculation is right, but the size of a correlation does not by itself identify a causal effect. D: Linear transformations do not in general destroy association; a positive rescaling and a sign reversal leave the magnitude unchanged.

A linear model fitted using practice sessions from 1 to 10 predicts score points by y-hat=42+4x. What should an analyst report for x=12?



- **A. The model predicts 90 score points, but this is extrapolation beyond the fitted x-range and is less reliable.**

- B. The model predicts 90 score points, and this is interpolation because the equation accepts x=12.

- C. The model predicts 50 score points, since the model input must be the distance past the largest observed x.

- D. No predicted value can be calculated once x lies outside the observed range.



Check: Substitute x=12: 42+4(12)=90. Since 12>10, the prediction extrapolates; the equation yields a value but reliability is weaker outside the data range.

Rationales: B: A fitted equation can be evaluated outside the observed x-range, but that use is extrapolation, not interpolation. C: The model uses the actual explanatory value x, not the distance past the edge of the data range. D: Extrapolation is less reliable, yet the model still produces a numerical prediction when x is substituted.

## Seed N-5.4-A (new) — Topic 5.4 Residuals

**Skill:** calculate a residual and interpret its sign in context



A fitted line for fitness score versus training hours is y-hat=12+3x. The observed responses are 21 at x=4 and 32 at x=5. Which statement correctly compares the two prediction errors?



- **A. The residuals are -3 and +5; the second observation has the larger absolute prediction error.**

- B. The residuals are +3 and -5; the second observation has the larger absolute prediction error.

- C. The residuals are -3 and +5; the first observation has the larger absolute prediction error.

- D. The residuals are the predicted responses 24 and 27; the larger prediction is always the larger error.



Check: Predictions are 24 and 27. Observed minus predicted gives residuals -3 and +5; compare their absolute values, 3 and 5.

Rationales: B: Residuals subtract predicted from observed, not observed from predicted, so both signs are reversed. C: The residual values are correct, but comparing absolute errors selects the other observation. D: Predicted values are not residuals; the size of a prediction does not by itself show its error.

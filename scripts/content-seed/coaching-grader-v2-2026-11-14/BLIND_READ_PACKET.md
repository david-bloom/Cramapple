## 1

**Question:** A student is asked to explain how cell compartmentalization arose and how it differs between prokaryotes and eukaryotes. Which statement gives the most accurate explanation?

**Student chose:** Mitochondria and chloroplasts descended from engulfed free-living prokaryotes. Prokaryotes have no internal organization, so all of their molecules and reactions mix freely, while eukaryotes partition the cell with internal membranes.

**Text A**

**What the question required:** An explanation of two things: how compartmentalization arose (the endosymbiotic origin of organelles) and how prokaryotes and eukaryotes differ in internal organization.

**What your answer did:** You correctly identified the origin: mitochondria and chloroplasts came from engulfed free-living prokaryotes. But you then claimed prokaryotes have no internal organization at all, so their molecules mix freely.

**Why that matters:** The point being tested is the difference in membrane-bound organelles, not a total absence of structure. Prokaryotes typically lack membrane-bound organelles, yet they still have specialized internal regions where particular functions occur. A claim that denies all organization is inaccurate, so it cannot earn the point even with a correct origin.

**Next move:** Rewrite the difference as "prokaryotes lack membrane-bound organelles, while eukaryotes partition the cell with them," and check that every clause is limited to membranes, not to all structure.

**Text B**

You chose B, and the origin part is right: mitochondria and chloroplasts came from engulfed free-living prokaryotes. The error is the claim that prokaryotes have no internal organization. Prokaryotes usually lack membrane-bound organelles, but they still have specialized internal regions where particular functions happen. Look again at the difference you described. Does "no internal organization" match "no membrane-bound organelles," or is it broader than that?

**Better:**  A / B / tie     **Gives away the answer:**  A / B / both / neither

## 5

**Question:** A researcher wants to study their satisfaction with bus arrival times among weekday bus riders in a city. Sampling plan: The researcher uses responses from riders who choose to answer after seeing a survey QR code on posters inside buses. Which choice best describes the sampling method?

**Student chose:** SRS, because any member of the population could have ended up in the response group.

**Text A**

Your choice of SRS is incorrect because, although the riders could be from any part of the population, the sample is not random. Riders only enter the sample if they see the poster and choose to answer the survey, which makes the group self-selected rather than randomly drawn. Random sampling requires that every member have a known, equal chance of selection, and voluntary participation does not provide that. Think about who is most likely to scan the QR code and what that does to the sample.

Which feature of a sample must be built in by the researcher, rather than left up to individuals, for it to count as a simple random sample?

**Text B**

(1) The question asked you to name the sampling method used. The key features are riders who choose to respond after seeing a QR code poster, so participation depends on self-selection, not on chance.

(2) You chose SRS, reasoning that anyone in the population could have ended up in the group. That describes a possibility, not a method. A simple random sample requires every possible sample of that size to have an equal chance of selection, and the researcher picked nobody. Riders decided whether to respond.

(3) This matters because a random method is what lets results generalize to the whole population. A voluntary-response sample only reflects people motivated enough to answer, so the label must be voluntary response or convenience, not random.

(4) Next move: for each choice, ask "who selected the sample, and by what chance process?" If the answer is "the participants themselves," eliminate any random label.

**Student chose:** Randomization is present because students' usual schedules vary naturally.

**Text A**

Choice B is wrong because it mixes up randomization with the natural variation in students' schedules. Randomization means researchers assign participants to groups by chance. Here, the researcher only observed students' existing habits and did not assign anyone to a condition, so the term does not describe this study. Review what randomization requires and how it differs from observation.

Which feature of a study must the researcher control or assign for randomization to exist?

**Text B**

The question asked you to identify the design element or flaw in a study where screen time and sleep were recorded as they naturally occurred, then compared across groups. You needed to recognize what kind of study this is and whether any assignment was made by the researcher.

You chose B, which claims randomization is present. Randomization means the researcher assigns participants to groups by chance. Here, students' usual screen time was simply observed, not assigned, so the claim does not hold. Varied natural habits are not randomization.

To earn this point next time, first ask: did the researcher or chance decide who fell into each group? If not, randomization is absent. Then look for what the study can and cannot conclude from observed differences.

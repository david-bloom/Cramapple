### Unit 9 — Thermodynamics and Electrochemistry (7-9%; runs through Topic 9.11, not the older 9.7-9.10 numbering)

- **9.1 Introduction to Entropy.** EK: entropy increases as matter
  disperses (e.g. solid→liquid→gas phase changes; gas volume increase at
  constant T) and as the total gas-phase product mole count exceeds
  reactant mole count. Entropy also increases as energy disperses — per
  KMT, the kinetic-energy distribution broadens with increasing
  temperature, increasing system entropy. Zero explicit exclusion
  statements.
- **9.2 Absolute Entropy and Entropy Change.** **EQN:**
  $\Delta S^{\circ}_{reaction}=\Sigma S^{\circ}_{products}-\Sigma S^{\circ}_{reactants}$.
  Zero explicit exclusion statements.
- **9.3 Gibbs Free Energy and Thermodynamic Favorability.** EK: standard
  Gibbs free energy change ($\Delta G^{\circ}$) applies when all
  reactants/products are in a standard state (pure substance, 1.0 M
  solution, or 1.0 atm/1.0 bar gas). "Thermodynamically favored" ($\Delta
  G^{\circ}<0$) is the CED's preferred term over "spontaneous" (see General
  Conventions above). **EQN:**
  $\Delta G^{\circ}_{reaction}=\Sigma\Delta G_f^{\circ}{}_{products}-\Sigma\Delta G_f^{\circ}{}_{reactants}$;
  **EQN:** $\Delta G^{\circ}=\Delta H^{\circ}-T\Delta S^{\circ}$. Sign-table
  shortcut (no calculation needed for these two cases): $\Delta H^{\circ}<0,
  \Delta S^{\circ}>0$ → favored at all T; $\Delta H^{\circ}>0, \Delta
  S^{\circ}<0$ → favored at no T. (The other two sign combinations —
  both negative, or both positive — require the numeric $\Delta
  H^{\circ}-T\Delta S^{\circ}$ calculation, favored at low T or high T
  respectively.) Zero explicit exclusion statements.
- **9.4 Thermodynamic and Kinetic Control.** EK: many thermodynamically
  favored processes occur at immeasurably slow rates ("kinetic control,"
  commonly from high activation energy); a process not proceeding at a
  measurable rate does not imply the system is at equilibrium. Zero
  explicit exclusion statements.
- **9.5 Free Energy and Equilibrium.** EK: "thermodynamically favored"
  ($\Delta G^{\circ}<0$) means products are favored at equilibrium ($K>1$)
  under standard conditions. **EQN:** $K=e^{-\Delta G^{\circ}/RT}$; **EQN:**
  $\Delta G^{\circ}=-RT\ln K$. When $\Delta G^{\circ}\approx0$, $K\approx1$;
  larger-magnitude $\Delta G^{\circ}$ (either sign) pushes $K$ further from
  1. Zero explicit exclusion statements.
- **9.6 Free Energy of Dissolution.** EK: dissolution $\Delta G^{\circ}$
  reflects three factors — breaking solid IMFs, solvent reorganization
  around the dissolved species, and dissolved-species/solvent interaction —
  whose enthalpic/entropic signs and magnitudes can be estimated
  individually, though total-ΔG prediction is genuinely hard due to
  cancellation among the three. Zero explicit exclusion statements. (Confirms
  the existing fact pack's correction that this topic is 9.6, distinct from
  Unit 7 solubility equilibria.)
- **9.7 Coupled Reactions.** EK: an external energy source can drive a
  thermodynamically unfavorable process (e.g. electrical energy for
  electrolysis/battery charging; light driving CO₂→glucose photosynthesis).
  Coupling an unfavorable reaction to a favorable one via a shared
  intermediate (e.g. ATP↔ADP) can achieve a net-favorable ($\Delta
  G^{\circ}<0$) overall reaction. Zero explicit exclusion statements.
- **9.8 Galvanic (Voltaic) and Electrolytic Cells.** EK: each cell component
  (electrodes, half-cell solutions, salt bridge, voltage/current meter) has
  a specific functional role, describable at both macroscopic and
  particulate levels. Galvanic/voltaic cells involve a thermodynamically
  favored reaction; electrolytic cells involve an unfavored one. Oxidation
  always occurs at the anode, reduction always at the cathode (for *all*
  electrochemical cells, not just galvanic). **Exclusion Statement
  (verbatim):** "Labeling an electrode as positive or negative will not be
  assessed on the AP Exam." (Source of existing flat-list item 14.)
- **9.9 Cell Potential and Free Energy.** EK: thermodynamically favored
  redox reactions in a cell give positive voltage; unfavored ones give
  negative voltage (requiring externally applied potential). Standard cell
  potential is calculated from half-reactions' standard reduction
  potentials. **EQN:** $\Delta G^{\circ}=-nFE^{\circ}$ — positive
  $E^{\circ}$ ↔ thermodynamically favored; negative $E^{\circ}$ ↔ unfavored.
  Zero explicit exclusion statements.
- **9.10 Cell Potential under Nonstandard Conditions.** EK: cell potential
  under nonstandard conditions is a driving force toward equilibrium — the
  farther from equilibrium, the larger the potential magnitude; the closer,
  the smaller, reaching zero exactly at equilibrium ($Q=K$). Le Châtelier's
  principle does *not* apply to electrochemical systems (they are not at
  equilibrium). **EQN (qualitative use only — see exclusion):**
  $E=E^{\circ}-(RT/nF)\ln Q$ (Nernst equation). **Exclusion Statement
  (verbatim, distinct framing — not a hard prohibition on the Nernst
  equation itself, but on algorithmic-only use):** "Algorithmic calculations
  using the Nernst equation are insufficient to demonstrate an
  understanding of electrochemical cells under nonstandard conditions.
  However, students should qualitatively understand the effects of
  concentration on cell potential and use conceptual reasoning, including
  the qualitative use of the Nernst equation... to solve problems." This is
  a distinct, more nuanced boundary than a simple exclusion and is not
  captured by the existing flat list — item authors should require
  qualitative justification alongside any Nernst-equation numeric answer,
  not accept a bare plug-and-chug.
- **9.11 Electrolysis and Faraday's Law.** EK: Faraday's laws relate
  electrons transferred, mass deposited/removed at an electrode
  (electroplating), current, elapsed time, and ionic species charge.
  **EQN:** $I=q/t$. Zero explicit exclusion statements.

**Documented misconception (2025 FRQ Q3 Parts B/F and Q6, Topics
9.1/9.3/9.8/9.9 — entropy/Gibbs reasoning was the single most conceptually
under-performing content pair on the whole 2025 exam):** the entropy-
decrease explanation point (Q3 Part B(i)) scored only 0.25/1.0; the
dominant failure was giving a "more order/less disorder" justification
*without* any particle-level discussion of the specific reaction species
(gas dispersal vs. solid dispersal, or microstate counting) — vague
order/disorder language alone did not earn credit. The companion
$\Delta G^{\circ}=\Delta H^{\circ}-T\Delta S^{\circ}$ favorability-reasoning
point (Part B(ii), 0.35/1.0) most often failed by claiming *both* $\Delta
H^{\circ}$ and $\Delta S^{\circ}$ contribute favorably when only one term's
sign actually supports favorability — some responses transcribed the
equation from the reference sheet without engaging the specific prompt at
all. The $K_p$-vs-temperature-shift reasoning point (Part F(ii), 0.31/1.0)
most often confused equilibrium-shift reasoning with an ideal-gas-law
pressure argument (claiming higher T directly raises system pressure)
instead of applying Le Châtelier's principle to an exothermic reaction.
For the galvanic-cell FRQ (Q6, the single lowest-mean FRQ of 2025 at
1.34/4), the maximum-cell-potential point (Part D, 0.23/1.0) had a
striking, well-documented misconception: many students assumed Zn had to
remain the *cathode* throughout the question (because it was the cathode
in Parts A-C) and never considered that Zn could instead serve as the
*anode* against a more-positive-reduction-potential metal (Au) to generate
a larger cell potential — a "the role established earlier in the question
transfers to later parts" reasoning error, not a calculation error, since
the arithmetic for the (wrong) Zn-cathode case was frequently done
correctly.
